# 100 Creative Writing Prompts (Fiction, Journaling, Poetry) — free download from DollarWise (dollarwise.xyz)

Pick one prompt, set a timer for 10–15 minutes, and write without stopping.
The full guide to using these prompts is on the DollarWise post page.

## Fiction prompts (25)

1. Your character finds a letter addressed to them, dated ten years in the future. It warns them about today.
2. A small town's only traffic light has been blinking red for thirty years. Tonight, it turns green.
3. Write about a family recipe that everyone refuses to make, and the reason is finally revealed at a reunion dinner.
4. A lighthouse keeper discovers a ship on the rocks that isn't on any map — and it isn't empty.
5. Your protagonist inherits a house with one locked room and a key labeled "do not open."
6. Every time your character lies, one object in their home disappears. This morning, the bed is gone.
7. A retired detective receives a birthday card from a case they never solved.
8. Two rival food truck owners are forced to share one parking spot for a summer.
9. Your character wakes up with a stranger's memories — and the stranger wants them back.
10. A time traveler can only visit Tuesdays. They've been trying to fix the same Tuesday for a decade.
11. Write a story where the main character's biggest enemy is their own reflection.
12. A small village holds a festival every year for a monster nobody has ever seen. This year, it shows up.
13. Your protagonist finds their own obituary in the morning paper — dated tomorrow.
14. A musician who lost their hearing composes their greatest piece in total silence.
15. Two siblings return to sell their childhood home and find something buried in the backyard.
16. An astronaut receives a radio message from Earth — but Earth stopped transmitting years ago.
17. Your character can hear one person's thoughts, and it's the one person they were trying to avoid.
18. A wedding planner falls in love with the best man of the wedding she's organizing.
19. Write about the last video rental store on Earth and its final customer.
20. A thief steals a painting and discovers the landscape inside it is changing.
21. Your protagonist's phone starts predicting the future in its autocorrect suggestions.
22. A chef loses their sense of taste and must cook the most important meal of their life.
23. Write a story set entirely in a laundromat over one long night.
24. A child claims their imaginary friend moved away — and left a forwarding address.
25. Your character buys a one-way ticket to nowhere, and the train never stops.

## Journaling prompts (25)

26. Describe the happiest ordinary day you can remember — not an event, just a day.
27. What is one belief you held strongly five years ago that you have completely changed your mind about?
28. Write a letter to yourself at 80. What do you want that person to know about who you are right now?
29. What does your ideal morning look like, in detail, from the moment you wake up?
30. List ten small things you are grateful for today and why each one matters.
31. What is something you have been avoiding, and what is the smallest first step?
32. Describe a place that feels like home, even if you have never lived there.
33. What would you do differently if you knew you could not fail?
34. Write about a conversation you wish you could have again — what would you say this time?
35. What are three things you want to be remembered for?
36. Describe your relationship with money in one honest paragraph.
37. What did you love doing as a child that you have completely stopped doing?
38. Write about a time you were brave and nobody noticed.
39. What is the best advice you have ever received, and did you follow it?
40. Describe the version of your life you are secretly working toward.
41. What drains your energy the most, and what gives you the most?
42. Write a letter to someone who changed your life, whether or not you ever send it.
43. What is one fear you are ready to outgrow?
44. Describe a perfect day with no obligations and no screens.
45. What do you need to forgive yourself for?
46. List five things you know now that you wish you had known at 18.
47. What does "enough" look like in your life right now?
48. Write about a stranger you still think about.
49. What is one habit that would change everything if you actually kept it?
50. Describe the last time you felt truly proud of yourself.

## Poetry prompts (25)

51. Write a poem where every line begins with a different color.
52. Describe a thunderstorm as if you have never seen one before and have no word for "rain."
53. Write an ode to an ordinary object in your kitchen.
54. Compose a poem using only questions.
55. Describe your hometown as a person — what are they wearing, how do they speak?
56. Write a poem about the hour before dawn without using the word "dark."
57. List everything you can hear right now, then turn the list into a poem.
58. Write about grief as a weather pattern.
59. Compose a haiku about your morning routine, then expand it into a longer poem.
60. Write a poem where each stanza is a different season of the same memory.
61. Describe falling in love using only images from nature.
62. Write an elegy for something small you lost: a key, a sock, a version of yourself.
63. Compose a poem in the voice of the ocean writing a letter to the shore.
64. Write about a meal that changed you.
65. Describe silence in a crowded room.
66. Write a poem that starts with a lie and ends with the truth.
67. Compose an ode to hands — yours, or someone else's.
68. Write about the moon as if it owes you money.
69. Describe your favorite smell so vividly a stranger could recognize it.
70. Write a poem using only one-syllable words.
71. Compose a piece about the last light of the day in your neighborhood.
72. Write about time as a river you cannot step out of.
73. Describe a childhood bedroom from memory, room by room.
74. Write a poem addressed to your future self.
75. Compose a poem about beginnings that starts at the end.

## Dialogue prompts (25)

76. "You weren't supposed to be here." / "Neither were you." — write the conversation that follows.
77. Two strangers are stuck in an elevator. One of them recognizes the other but won't say from where.
78. A job interview where the interviewer keeps answering their own questions.
79. Two old friends meet after twenty years. One of them is lying about everything.
80. A customer tries to return something to a store with a strict no-returns policy — the item is a dragon egg.
81. Write a breakup conversation that happens entirely in a grocery store checkout line.
82. A detective interrogates a suspect who only speaks in riddles.
83. Two astronauts argue about what song to play on the way home.
84. A parent and teenager negotiate a later curfew like rival diplomats.
85. Write the conversation between two ghosts haunting the same house who disagree about everything.
86. A barista and a regular have their morning exchange — but today the regular orders something different, and it matters.
87. Two rival coaches are stuck sharing a taxi to the championship game.
88. Write a conversation where one person is trying to confess something and the other keeps changing the subject.
89. A time traveler tries to explain smartphones to someone from 1850.
90. Two siblings divide their late parent's belongings and uncover an old argument.
91. Write the dialogue of a first date where both people are pretending to be someone else.
92. A doctor has to deliver difficult news, and the patient already knows.
93. Two neighbors argue over a fence, but the real argument is about something else entirely.
94. Write a conversation between a writer and their main character, who has complaints.
95. A teacher and student debate whether the test was fair — the student has evidence.
96. Two friends plan a heist that is actually just returning a library book three years late.
97. Write the last conversation between two people who know they will never meet again.
98. A customer service call where both the agent and the caller are having the worst day of their lives.
99. Two superheroes argue about who gets to save the city this time.
100. Write a conversation that takes place entirely through notes passed in class.
