# 50 Image Alt Text Examples for SEO

Alt text does two jobs at once: it tells blind and low-vision visitors what your images show, and it tells Google what your images are about — which is how pictures end up ranking in image search and supporting the page's topic. Yet most sites either leave it blank or write "image1" and move on, throwing away free SEO.

## How to use these examples

1. Find the example closest to your image type and copy its sentence structure, swapping in your own details.
2. Write one sentence under 125 characters: subject first, then one or two visible details (color, setting, action).
3. Add your page's keyword only if it describes the image naturally — never force it.
4. Leave decorative images with empty alt text (alt="") so screen readers skip them.
5. Audit your ten most-visited pages: fix their image alt text first, where it earns the most.

Good alt text is a habit, not a project. Use this pattern on every image you publish from now on, and the download below keeps all 50 examples handy for your team or your future self.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long should alt text be?**

Aim for under 125 characters — most screen readers cut off longer text, and concise descriptions rank just as well. Describe what is in the image and why it matters on the page; one clear sentence is almost always enough.

**Should I stuff keywords into alt text?**

No. Write for a person who cannot see the image first, and let one natural keyword appear only if it genuinely describes the picture. Keyword-stuffed alt text ('cheap red shoes buy shoes online shoes') reads as spam to both screen readers and search engines.

**Do decorative images need alt text?**

No — purely decorative images (dividers, background textures, icons next to labeled buttons) should have empty alt text (alt="") so screen readers skip them. Save descriptive alt text for images that carry meaning: products, people, charts, and diagrams.
