50 Product Photo Shot List for Ecommerce — free resource from DollarWise

1. Front hero on white — the clean, straight-on shot every listing leads with.
2. Front hero on a colored background — the same angle on a brand color for a distinctive store look.
3. Three-quarter angle — the product turned slightly, showing front and side together for depth.
4. Side profile — a straight side view showing thickness, shape, and silhouette.
5. Back view — the reverse side, including labels, closures, and back details.
6. Top-down flat lay — the product shot from directly above on a clean surface.
7. 45-degree overhead — a slightly angled top view that feels natural and dimensional.
8. Low angle — shot from below to make the product feel larger and more premium.
9. High angle — shot from above at an angle to show the product in its setting.
10. Hero with soft shadow — a gentle natural shadow that grounds the product without clutter.
11. Material close-up — a tight shot of the fabric, leather, wood, or metal texture.
12. Stitching or seam detail — close on seams, hems, and joins to prove build quality.
13. Closure detail — zippers, buttons, clasps, or lids shown working and finished.
14. Label or logo detail — branding, care labels, and engraved marks in sharp focus.
15. Edge and finish detail — polished, painted, or raw edges that show craftsmanship.
16. Interior shot — the inside of bags, boxes, cases, or containers, fully visible.
17. Hardware detail — buckles, hinges, handles, and fittings in close-up.
18. Pattern detail — prints, weaves, or textures filling the frame.
19. Wear or use detail — the part that touches the user, like a handle grip or strap.
20. Packaging detail — the box, pouch, or wrapping the product arrives in.
21. Product in use at home — the item in a real living space, styled naturally.
22. Product in use outdoors — the item in daylight and a real-world setting.
23. Product held in hand — a hand holding or wearing the product for instant scale and warmth.
24. Product worn by a model — clothing, accessories, or wearables on a real person.
25. Product on a desk or workspace — the item in the environment where it will be used.
26. Product with complementary items — styled with things it pairs with, not competitors.
27. Morning routine scene — the product as part of a daily ritual, like coffee or skincare.
28. Gift scene — the product wrapped or presented as a gift, ready for gifting searches.
29. Seasonal lifestyle — the product in a seasonal setting, like summer light or winter coziness.
30. Action lifestyle — the product mid-use: poured, worn, carried, or played with.
31. Next to a ruler or coin — an unmistakable size reference for small products.
32. Next to a familiar object — beside a phone, book, or mug for everyday scale.
33. Held against the body — worn or held to show true proportions on a person.
34. On a shelf with similar items — sized against other products shoppers know.
35. Dimension overlay — the product with clean measurement graphics added in editing.
36. In its storage spot — inside a drawer, shelf, or bag to show how it fits away.
37. Before and after — the result the product creates, side by side.
38. Flat lay with all included parts — everything in the box laid out and labeled.
39. Unboxing sequence — the opening experience in three to five frames.
40. Product next to its packaging — item and box together so expectations match delivery.
41. Every color variation — each color photographed in the same hero angle for consistency.
42. Every size variation — each size shown with the same scale reference.
43. Bundle shot — the full set or bundle together as one offer.
44. Single item from the bundle — each bundle piece alone for its own listing image.
45. Pattern or print variations — each pattern in the same angle and light.
46. Set in use together — a matching set arranged as it would actually be used.
47. Comparison shot — old versus new, or this model versus the previous one.
48. Spare parts and accessories — included extras photographed clearly, not forgotten.
49. Care and cleaning shot — the product being cleaned or maintained, showing durability.
50. Night or low-light shot — the product in evening light for items used after dark.
