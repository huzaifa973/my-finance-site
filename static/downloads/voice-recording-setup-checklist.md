# Voice Recording Setup Checklist: 20 Steps to Clean Audio

Clean voice audio is 80% setup and 20% gear. Podcasters, YouTubers, course creators, and voiceover beginners all make the same mistake: they buy a microphone, hit record in an echoey room, and wonder why it sounds amateur. This 20-step checklist walks you through the entire setup — room choice, noise control, mic placement, gain staging, and file habits — before you record a single word that matters.

## The 20 setup steps

1. Pick the quietest room in your home — small rooms with soft furnishings beat big empty ones every time.
2. Turn off everything that hums: air conditioning, fans, the fridge, and every notification sound.
3. Record ten seconds of silence and listen on headphones — you will hear noise you never noticed.
4. Use a real microphone: even a $30 USB mic dramatically beats any built-in laptop microphone.
5. Get the mic off the desk on a stand or boom arm — desks transmit every bump and keystroke.
6. Position the mic 4-6 inches from your mouth, slightly off to the side, not directly in front.
7. Add a pop filter or foam windscreen to tame harsh P and B plosive blasts.
8. Wear closed-back headphones and monitor yourself live so problems get caught, not discovered later.
9. Set gain so your loudest peaks land around -12dB to -6dB — clean signal with headroom, never hitting 0.
10. Record at 44.1kHz or 48kHz, 24-bit WAV — maximum quality now, compress to MP3 at export.
11. Close unnecessary apps so a calendar ping does not ruin a perfect take.
12. Put your phone on airplane mode or Do Not Disturb — vibrations count as noise too.
13. Warm up your voice: hum, do lip trills, and read a paragraph aloud before the real recording.
14. Keep water nearby and sip often — most mouth clicks come from a dry mouth.
15. Hold the same posture for the whole session — shifting changes your distance and tone.
16. Mark mistakes with a loud clap or finger snap so they are easy to find when editing.
17. Record thirty seconds of room tone (pure silence) for noise removal in editing later.
18. Do a full test recording and listen back on headphones before committing to the real take.
19. Save with a clear filename: project, date, take number — e.g. podcast_2026-10-22_take1.
20. Back up the file to a second location immediately after recording — drives fail, takes do not come back.

## How to use this checklist

1. Walk through all 20 steps in order the first three times you record — it takes about ten minutes.
2. Pay special attention to steps 1-3: if the silence test sounds bad, fix the room before touching the mic.
3. Once the routine is familiar, it compresses to a five-minute pre-flight: room, mic, gain, test, go.
4. Keep the downloadable checklist taped near your recording spot until it is muscle memory.
5. Re-run the full list whenever you change rooms, mics, or software — new variables, new check.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need an expensive microphone for good voice recordings?**

No. A $30-$100 USB microphone in a quiet, soft-furnished room will outperform a $500 microphone in an echoey kitchen. Room and mic placement matter far more than price. Upgrade your space first — blankets and closed doors are free.

**Why does my recording echo even with a good mic?**

Echo comes from hard, parallel surfaces: bare walls, tile floors, empty rooms. The mic hears your voice plus its reflections. Soften the room — record in a smaller space, hang blankets, add rugs and curtains, and get the mic close to your mouth so your voice dominates the reflections.

**What recording settings should I use?**

Record at 44.1kHz or 48kHz, 24-bit, as WAV. That gives you maximum quality to edit with; you can always export a compressed MP3 later. Set your gain so the loudest peaks land around -12dB to -6dB — loud enough to be clean, with headroom so nothing clips.
