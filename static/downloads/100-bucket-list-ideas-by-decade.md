# 100 Bucket List Ideas by Decade (Your 20s Through Your 70s) — free download from DollarWise (dollarwise.xyz)

Borrow freely from every decade — this is inspiration, not a rulebook.
Pick 10–15 items that genuinely excite you and put one on the calendar.

## Your 20s (1–17)

1. Take a solo trip to a country where you don't speak the language
2. Learn to cook five dishes from five different cuisines from scratch
3. Watch the sunrise from the top of a mountain
4. Go swimming in the ocean at midnight
5. Attend a music festival with your best friends
6. Learn a second language to conversational level
7. Volunteer abroad for at least two weeks
8. Ride a motorcycle or scooter through a scenic route
9. Camp under the stars with no tent
10. Learn to surf, ski, or skateboard — pick one and commit
11. Go to a drive-in movie theater
12. Take an unplanned road trip with no destination
13. Learn to play three songs on an instrument
14. Go stargazing in a certified dark-sky location
15. Try ten foods you've never eaten before
16. Write and perform something — a song, a poem, a stand-up set
17. Dance in the rain on purpose

## Your 30s (18–34)

18. Run a marathon, or walk one — finishing is what counts
19. Master a skill that takes real practice, like photography, pottery, or a musical instrument
20. See the Northern Lights
21. Take a cooking class in another country
22. Go on a safari or whale-watching expedition
23. Learn to scuba dive and explore a coral reef
24. Buy your first home, or build your dream rental space
25. Start the business or side project you've been talking about
26. Hike a famous long trail (or a section of one)
27. Take your parents on a trip they will never forget
28. Learn to sail a boat
29. Attend a major sporting event live — World Cup, Olympics, championship final
30. Write a book, or at least start one seriously
31. Go hot air ballooning at sunrise
32. Learn to dance properly — salsa, tango, swing, your pick
33. Swim with dolphins or sea turtles in the wild
34. Plant a tree and watch it grow year after year

## Your 40s (35–51)

35. Take your dream road trip with no fixed itinerary — just a direction and a full tank
36. Mentor someone younger in your field and watch them surpass you
37. Relearn something you gave up as a kid — piano, drawing, a sport
38. Take a sabbatical or an extended break purely for adventure
39. See one of the Seven Wonders of the World in person
40. Learn to fly a plane, or at least take a flying lesson
41. Host the legendary dinner party people talk about for years
42. Go on a multi-day trek — Machu Picchu, Kilimanjaro base camp, the Alps
43. Learn a third language
44. Take your kids (or nieces/nephews) somewhere magical
45. Swim in as many of the world's oceans as you can reach
46. Go to a film festival — a big one or your local favorite
47. Restore something old: a car, a house, a piece of furniture
48. Write letters to everyone who shaped your life and deliver them
49. Try paragliding or skydiving if you haven't yet
50. Spend a week completely offline in nature
51. Take a masterclass from someone you admire

## Your 50s (52–68)

52. Sleep under the stars in a desert and watch the Milky Way
53. Revisit the place from your favorite childhood memory and see it with adult eyes
54. Take the luxury trip you once thought was "someday" — this is someday
55. Learn to paint, sculpt, or throw pottery seriously
56. Walk the Camino de Santiago or another pilgrimage route
57. See your favorite band or artist live before they retire
58. Take a cruise through fjords, glaciers, or tropical islands
59. Learn to cook like a chef with professional classes
60. Go on an African safari at golden hour
61. Reconnect with old friends you lost touch with decades ago
62. Take up golf, tennis, or another "lifetime sport" properly
63. Visit all the places your ancestors came from
64. Adopt a rescue animal and give it the best life
65. Write your memoir — even a private one
66. Learn ballroom dancing with your partner
67. Go dog sledding, or ride horses through open country
68. Volunteer for a cause that uses your lifetime of skills

## Your 60s (69–84)

69. Write your life story — even a short one — for your family to keep
70. Take your grandchildren (or young relatives) on an adventure
71. See the cherry blossoms in Japan in spring
72. Learn a completely new skill just for fun — no career value required
73. Take a slow river cruise through Europe
74. Revisit your honeymoon spot or first travel destination
75. Plant a garden that will outlive you
76. Take a photography expedition somewhere wild
77. Learn to meditate properly on a real retreat
78. Go whale watching in Alaska or Baja
79. Host a reunion of everyone who matters to you
80. Take a cooking tour through Italy, France, or Southeast Asia
81. Learn to play bridge, chess, or another classic game well
82. Give an important speech — a toast, a eulogy, a graduation address
83. Adopt the "yes" year: say yes to every reasonable invitation for twelve months
84. Watch a total solar eclipse from the path of totality

## Your 70s (85–100)

85. Do the thing everyone says you're too old for — then tell the story at dinner
86. Take a hot air balloon ride over beautiful countryside
87. Write letters to your great-grandchildren about the world you knew
88. Learn to use new technology fearlessly — whatever's next
89. Revisit your top five favorite places on Earth, one per year
90. Take a scenic train journey — the Rockies, the Alps, or the Trans-Siberian
91. Teach your signature recipe to the next generation, hands-on
92. Go on a gentle safari or a wildlife river cruise
93. Attend a concert in a legendary venue
94. Start a small scholarship or fund in your name
95. Take a painting or writing workshop somewhere beautiful
96. Spend a month living somewhere you've always dreamed about
97. Record your family's oral history on video
98. Go stargazing with an astronomer guide
99. Take the slow road: a no-rush road trip with no schedule at all
100. Celebrate a milestone birthday exactly the way you want — no compromises
