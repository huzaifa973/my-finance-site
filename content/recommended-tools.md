## 1. YNAB (You Need a Budget)

A paid budgeting app built around giving every dollar a job. Best for people who want a strict, hands-on system.

**Pros:**

- Forces intentional spending — every dollar is assigned before the month starts
- Excellent educational content and live workshops included
- Syncs with most US/UK bank accounts

**Cons:**

- Paid subscription (around $15/month) — steep if you're on a tight budget
- Learning curve in the first couple of weeks
- Overkill if you just want a simple spending tracker

## 2. EveryDollar

Dave Ramsey's budgeting app. The free tier is a solid zero-based budgeting tool for beginners.

**Pros:**

- Free plan covers the core zero-based budget workflow
- Very simple interface — easy for complete beginners
- Good if you follow (or like) the baby-steps method

**Cons:**

- Bank sync requires the paid "Premium" plan
- Fewer reports and insights than YNAB
- US-focused; UK bank connections are limited

## 3. High-yield savings account

Not an app — a type of account. Parking your emergency fund in a high-yield savings account (e.g. Ally, Marcus by Goldman Sachs, or a competitive UK easy-access saver) earns many times the interest of a regular checking account.

**Pros:**

- Your emergency fund actually grows while it waits
- Money stays separate from spending cash (less temptation)
- No fees at most online banks; 1–2 day access is ideal

**Cons:**

- Rates change with the market — shop around yearly
- Some "high-yield" accounts have balance or activity requirements
- Don't chase rates across five banks; pick one and move on

## 4. Credit Karma (free credit score)

Free credit scores and reports in the US, with UK equivalents like ClearScore and Credit Karma UK.

**Pros:**

- Completely free credit scores and monitoring
- Alerts you to hard inquiries and new accounts
- Simple explanations of what moves your score

**Cons:**

- Shows VantageScore, while many lenders use FICO (numbers can differ)
- Ad-supported — expect offers for cards and loans
- Monitoring is no substitute for actually paying on time

## 5. Rocket Money (subscription tracker)

Connects to your accounts, finds recurring subscriptions, and helps you cancel or negotiate them.

**Pros:**

- Finds forgotten subscriptions fast — most people are shocked by the total
- Cancellation assistance saves awkward phone calls
- Spending alerts catch price hikes early

**Cons:**

- Premium features cost a monthly fee
- Bill-negotiation takes a cut of what it saves you
- You could do the same manually with a spreadsheet for free

## 6. Notion budget template

A free Notion template for tracking income, expenses, and savings goals — ideal if you like spreadsheets but want something prettier.

**Pros:**

- Free and endlessly customizable
- Works on phone and desktop, syncs everywhere
- Great for visual goal tracking (savings thermometers, debt payoff boards)

**Cons:**

- Manual entry — no automatic bank sync
- Easy to abandon if you don't build the habit
- Template quality varies; pick a simple one
