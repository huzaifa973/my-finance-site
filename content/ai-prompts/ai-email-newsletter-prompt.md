# Email Newsletter Prompt ✉️
Stop staring at a blank screen every week. Describe your topic once and get a complete, send-ready newsletter: five subject line variants, preview text, a three-section body that opens with a story and closes with a clear CTA, plus a P.S. that almost always gets read.

<pre class="prompt-text"><code>Write a complete email newsletter for my audience of [AUDIENCE, e.g. freelance designers] on the topic: [TOPIC, e.g. how to raise your rates without losing clients].

Include, in this exact order:
1. Five subject line variants (under 50 characters each, mix of curiosity, benefit, and urgency styles).
2. Preview text (40–90 characters) that complements the subject line without repeating it.
3. A warm greeting that uses the subscriber's first name placeholder.
4. A three-section body, 350–500 words total:
   - Section 1 (Story): open with a short, relatable personal story or anecdote that sets up the topic.
   - Section 2 (Value): deliver the core lesson — 3 actionable takeaways with specific examples.
   - Section 3 (CTA): one clear call-to-action with a single link, explained in 2–3 sentences.
5. A P.S. line that reinforces the main benefit and repeats the CTA.
Tone: [TONE, e.g. friendly, direct, slightly witty], like a note from an experienced friend. Avoid jargon. Use short paragraphs (2–3 sentences) and one subhead per section.
Then provide an A/B variant: rewrite the subject line and the first 100 words of the body in a more direct, no-fluff style so I can test which converts better.</code></pre>

## Preview — what this prompt creates
You get back a finished email you can paste straight into ConvertKit, Mailchimp, Beehiiv, or Substack. The five subject lines give you a month of A/B test material, the story opening lifts reply rates, and the P.S. line often outperforms the main CTA because it's the last thing readers see. The A/B variant lets you run a real split test instead of guessing which voice your list prefers.
## Best AI tools for this
- **ChatGPT (GPT-4o / GPT-4)** — best all-rounder, strong subject lines
- **Claude (Opus / Sonnet)** — excellent long-form storytelling tone
- **Gemini Advanced** — fast, good at matching a brand voice if you paste examples
- **Jasper** — built-in email templates and brand-voice training
- **Copy.ai** — great for generating many subject-line variants at once
## Make it yours
- **[AUDIENCE]** — be specific ("parents of toddlers" beats "parents"); the AI writes sharper copy when it knows exactly who is reading.
- **[TOPIC]** — one topic per email. Bundling three ideas dilutes the CTA.
- **[TONE]** — paste two paragraphs of your own past newsletters and add "match this voice exactly" for best results.
- **Word count** — raise it to 700 for deep-dive issues, or drop to 200 for a quick tip roundup.
## Pro tips
- Always replace the AI's generic anecdote with one real story from your week — authenticity is the biggest open-rate lever you have.
- Send at a consistent time; consistency beats "optimal send time" research.
- Track your reply rate, not just opens — replies tell you the content actually landed.
- Never use all five subject lines on one send. Rotate them across your next five newsletters.
