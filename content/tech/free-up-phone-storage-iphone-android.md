"Storage Almost Full" — the most annoying notification on any phone. The good news: you can usually reclaim 10–30 GB in under an hour without losing anything you care about. Here's the exact step-by-step, for iPhone and Android.

## See exactly what's eating your storage

Before deleting anything, get the full picture. Your phone breaks storage down by category, which tells you where the big wins are.

**On iPhone:** go to Settings > General > iPhone Storage. Wait a few seconds for the colored bar to load. It shows how much space apps, photos, messages, and system data use — and iOS even suggests personalized cleanups at the top.

**On Android:** go to Settings > Storage. **On Samsung:** go to Settings > Battery and device care > Storage. You'll see the same category breakdown: apps, images, videos, audio, and system.

Take a screenshot of this screen. In thirty minutes you'll enjoy comparing the before and after.

## Offload or uninstall the apps you don't use

Apps are usually the biggest single category, and most people carry a dozen they haven't opened in months. A single game can eat 5–15 GB.

**On iPhone — offload, don't just delete.** Go to Settings > General > iPhone Storage, tap any app you rarely use, and choose Offload App. This removes the app but keeps all its documents and data, so reinstalling later restores everything. You can also enable Offload Unused Apps at the top of the same screen to let iOS do this automatically.

**On Android:** go to Settings > Apps, sort by size, and uninstall what you don't need. Android doesn't have a true "offload," so check whether the app stores your data in the cloud (most Google, Microsoft, and streaming apps do) before removing it. Games with large downloads are the best targets — you can always reinstall.

**The 3-month rule:** if you haven't opened an app in three months and it doesn't hold irreplaceable data, it goes. Be honest with yourself — that airline app from one trip in 2024 is not coming back.

## Move photos and videos to the cloud, then clear the originals

For most people, photos and videos are the real storage hog — a year of 4K clips can swallow 50 GB. The fix isn't deleting memories; it's moving them.

**On iPhone:** turn on iCloud Photos at Settings > [your name] > iCloud > Photos, choose Optimize iPhone Storage, and let iCloud upload everything. Once uploads finish (check the progress at the bottom of the Photos app), full-resolution originals move to iCloud and your phone keeps lightweight versions.

**On Android:** open Google Photos, tap your profile picture > Photos settings > Backup, and turn backup on. Once backup completes, use the "Free up space" option in Google Photos (Library > Utilities > Free up space) to remove the device copies of already-backed-up photos.

**Critical warning — back up first, delete second.** Never delete originals until the cloud app confirms the upload is complete. Open the cloud service on another device or the web and spot-check a few recent photos before clearing anything. A backup you haven't verified is not a backup.

## Clear the WhatsApp media avalanche

WhatsApp is a silent storage monster. Every forwarded video, meme, and "good morning" image lands on your phone, and group chats multiply it.

**In WhatsApp:** go to Settings > Storage and Data > Manage Storage. You'll see exactly how much space each chat uses. Tap the biggest ones and delete large files — or clear everything from chats you don't need to keep.

**Stop the flood at the source:** in the same Storage and Data screen, set Media auto-download to "No media" (or Wi-Fi only) for photos, audio, video, and documents. You'll still receive everything — it just won't save to your phone until you tap it.

This one step alone often frees 5–15 GB on phones that have been in active group chats for years.

## Clear caches safely (without losing logins)

Caches are temporary files apps build up over time — map tiles, image thumbnails, preloaded feeds. Clearing them is safe and they rebuild automatically.

**On Android:** go to Settings > Apps, tap an app > Storage & cache > Clear cache. Start with browsers, social apps, and shopping apps, which accumulate the most. **Important:** Clear cache is safe; Clear data (or Clear storage) is not — that wipes logins, settings, and offline content. Never tap Clear data unless you're prepared to set the app up again from scratch.

**On iPhone:** iOS doesn't offer a system-wide clear-cache button. Instead, offload and reinstall heavy apps (Settings > General > iPhone Storage > Offload App, then reinstall from the App Store) — this dumps the cache while keeping your data. For Safari, go to Settings > Safari > Clear History and Website Data, which clears cached web files (note: this also signs you out of websites).

## The two-minute maintenance routine

Storage fills up again if you never tend it. Once a month, spend two minutes on this:

1. Check Settings > Storage for the new biggest category.
2. In WhatsApp, run Manage Storage on your top three chats.
3. Delete screenshots and duplicates — on iPhone, Photos > Albums > Duplicates; on Android, Google Photos flags blurry shots and screenshots for review.
4. Clear the downloads folder (Files app on both platforms) — installers, PDFs, and memes go to die there.
5. Empty the trash: iPhone Photos > Albums > Recently Deleted > Delete All; Google Photos > Library > Trash > Empty trash. Deleted files still count against storage for 30 days otherwise.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it safe to clear app cache on Android?

Yes — clearing an app's cache only deletes temporary files like thumbnails and preloaded data. The app rebuilds them as needed. It is completely different from "Clear data" or "Clear storage," which wipes your logins and saved content. Never tap Clear data unless you're prepared to log back in and lose offline downloads.

### What is the difference between offloading and deleting an app on iPhone?

Offloading removes the app itself but keeps its documents and data, so reinstalling restores everything exactly as it was. Deleting removes the app and its data together. Offload is the safe choice for apps you might want back; delete only apps you're done with for good.

### Will deleting WhatsApp media delete my chats?

No. Clearing media from WhatsApp storage management deletes photos, videos, and voice notes — your text conversations stay intact. Just make sure important media is backed up (Google Photos, iCloud, or a chat backup) before you clear large files.

### How much free space should I keep on my phone?

Aim for at least 10–15% of total storage free (roughly 10–20 GB on a 128 GB phone). Below that, phones slow down, updates fail, the camera may refuse to save photos, and apps start crashing. Storage headroom is performance headroom.

## The bottom line

Work the list in order — audit, offload apps, cloud your photos, tame WhatsApp, clear caches — and verify your backup before deleting anything. Most people recover 10–30 GB in one sitting, and the monthly two-minute routine keeps the "Storage Almost Full" warning away for good.
