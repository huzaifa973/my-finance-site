Your iPhone says "Storage Full" and suddenly you can't take a photo, update iOS, or download the app you need. The good news is that most iPhones are carrying several gigabytes of pure waste — cached files, old attachments, forgotten downloads — that you can clear in minutes without deleting anything important. Work through these ten fixes in order and you will typically reclaim 10–30 GB on a phone that felt completely full.
## 10 ways to free up iPhone storage
**1. Offload unused apps (keep their data).** Offloading deletes the app itself but keeps all your documents and settings, so if you reinstall it later everything is exactly where you left it. Go to **Settings > General > iPhone Storage**, wait for the list to load, and tap any app you have not used in months. Choose **Offload App** — or turn on automatic offloading by going to **Settings > App Store** and enabling **Offload Unused Apps**. This is the single fastest way to reclaim space with zero risk.
**2. Clear Safari cache and website data.** Safari quietly stores gigabytes of cached pages, cookies, and site data over time. Go to **Settings > Safari** and tap **Clear History and Website Data**. Confirm the prompt. This logs you out of websites you were signed into, but it can free up 1–3 GB instantly. If you use Chrome or another browser, clear its cache from inside that app's settings too.
**3. Optimize iPhone storage for photos.** Photos and videos are almost always the biggest storage hog on an iPhone. Go to **Settings > Photos** and select **Optimize iPhone Storage** instead of **Download and Keep Originals**. Full-resolution photos and videos stay in iCloud while smaller, space-saving versions live on your phone — you can still view everything, and originals download automatically when you zoom or edit. If you do not use iCloud Photos, enable it: the free 5 GB tier alone can offload your most recent shots, and paid plans start at a very low monthly price.
**4. Delete old Messages attachments.** Years of group chats store every photo, video, sticker, and voice note ever sent to you — often multiple gigabytes. Go to **Settings > General > iPhone Storage > Messages**. You will see a breakdown by category: Photos, Videos, GIFs and Stickers. Tap **Review Large Attachments**, swipe left on anything you do not need, and delete it. You can also set messages to auto-delete after 30 days or one year in **Settings > Messages > Keep Messages**.
**5. Remove large attachments in Mail.** If you use the Mail app, every attachment you ever opened is cached on your phone. Go to **Settings > General > iPhone Storage** and check how much space Mail is using — it is often surprising. The cleanest fix is to delete and re-add your mail account: go to **Settings > Mail > Accounts**, tap the account, choose **Delete Account**, then add it back fresh. All cached attachments vanish while your mail stays on the server.
**6. Clear app cache manually.** Some apps — especially social media and streaming apps — cache huge amounts of data. Go to **Settings > General > iPhone Storage** and look for apps whose "Documents & Data" size far exceeds the app itself. For apps like Instagram, TikTok, or Snapchat, the only reliable way to clear their cache is to delete the app and reinstall it (your account data is in the cloud). For apps with a built-in cache clearer — like Spotify or YouTube — use the option inside the app's settings.
**7. Delete old downloads in Files and Safari.** Open the **Files** app and check **On My iPhone** for old PDFs, videos, and zip files you downloaded and forgot. Also open Safari, tap the downloads icon (the arrow in a circle), and clear anything there. Check third-party downloader apps and WhatsApp's "Saved" media too — these folders accumulate quietly.
**8. Remove offline content from streaming apps.** Netflix, YouTube Premium, Spotify, and Amazon Prime Video let you download shows and music for offline viewing. Those downloads can easily eat 10–20 GB. Open each app, go to its Downloads section, and delete anything you have already watched or no longer need. A quick pass through all your streaming apps often reclaims the most space of any single step.
**9. Use free cloud storage to offload files.** You do not have to pay to move files off your phone. Google Drive gives you 15 GB free (shared with Gmail and Photos), Dropbox gives 2 GB, OneDrive gives 5 GB, and pCloud and MediaFire offer free tiers as well. Upload your large videos and documents to one of these, confirm they are safely stored, then delete the local copies from your iPhone.
**10. Know when to upgrade — or reset.** If after all of this you are still constantly hitting the ceiling, your phone's base storage is simply too small for how you use it. Next time you buy an iPhone, step up at least one storage tier — the difference in price at purchase is far cheaper than the ongoing frustration. As a last resort before upgrading, back up to iCloud or a computer and do a full **Erase All Content and Settings** (**Settings > General > Transfer or Reset iPhone**), then restore selectively. This wipes years of accumulated system cache and often frees 5–10 GB that no other method can reach.
## Frequently asked questions
**Will offloading apps delete my photos or messages?**
No. Offloading only removes the app binary. Your photos, messages, and the app's own documents and data are completely preserved. When you reinstall the app, it picks up exactly where you left off.
**Does deleting a photo in Photos also delete it from iCloud?**
Yes — if iCloud Photos is enabled, deleting from your phone deletes it everywhere after the 30-day Recently Deleted recovery window. To free space without losing photos, use "Optimize iPhone Storage" (step 3) rather than deleting. If you do delete, check **Recently Deleted** and empty it to reclaim the space immediately.
**How much free space should I keep?**
Aim for at least 10% of your total storage free — so 12 GB free on a 128 GB phone. iOS needs working space for updates, temporary files, and app caches; below that threshold your phone slows down and some features stop working.
**Why does "System Data" take up so much space, and can I delete it?**
System Data includes caches, logs, and temporary files that iOS manages itself. You cannot delete it directly, but restarting your phone, clearing Safari and app caches, and doing the occasional full reset (step 10) will shrink it over time. It usually stabilizes at a reasonable size once you free up space elsewhere.
