Your phone or laptop says it's connected to Wi-Fi, yet no website loads and no app works. This usually means your device can reach the router but the router can't reach the internet — or your device's network settings are stuck. Here's how to fix it, step by step.

## 7 fixes that work

**1. Restart your router and your device.** Unplug your router's power cable, wait a full 30 seconds, and plug it back in. While it reboots (about two minutes), restart your phone or computer too. This alone fixes the problem most of the time.

**2. Check whether it's just one device.** Try loading a website on another phone or laptop on the same Wi-Fi. If other devices work, the problem is your device's settings — keep going below. If nothing works, the issue is your router or your internet provider, and you may need to call them.

**3. Forget the network and reconnect.** A corrupted saved connection can block internet access. On Windows, go to Settings > Network & Internet > Wi-Fi, click the network, and choose Forget. On Android or iPhone, go to Wi-Fi settings, tap the network name, and tap Forget. Then reconnect and re-enter the password.

**4. Toggle airplane mode.** This forces your device to drop and rebuild all network connections. Turn airplane mode on, wait 10 seconds, then turn it off and let the Wi-Fi reconnect.

**5. Flush your DNS cache (Windows).** A stale DNS cache can make it look like there's no internet. Open Command Prompt as administrator, type `ipconfig /flushdns`, and press Enter. Then type `ipconfig /renew` and press Enter to get a fresh connection.

**6. Switch to a public DNS server.** Sometimes your provider's DNS servers fail while everything else works. On Windows, go to Settings > Network & Internet > Wi-Fi, click your network, and set the DNS to manual with 8.8.8.8 and 8.8.4.4 (Google's free DNS). On Android, long-press the network in Wi-Fi settings, tap Modify network > Advanced, and change the DNS the same way.

**7. Reset your network settings.** If nothing else worked, start fresh. On Windows, go to Settings > Network & Internet > Advanced network settings > Network reset and confirm. On Android, go to Settings > System > Reset options > Reset Wi-Fi, mobile & Bluetooth. You'll need to re-enter Wi-Fi passwords afterward.

## The bottom line

The router restart in step one solves this for most people, so always start there. If the problem keeps coming back every few days, your router may be overheating or dying — try placing it somewhere ventilated, and if that fails, it's probably time for a replacement.
