A slow Android phone is one of the most frustrating tech problems there is — apps stutter when you open them, the keyboard lags behind your typing, and scrolling feels like wading through mud. The good news is that most slowdowns are fixable without buying a new phone. Android accumulates clutter over months and years: cached files, bloatware, background processes, and outdated software all pile up and drag performance down. Work through these eleven fixes in order and your phone will feel noticeably faster — often like new.
## 11 fixes to speed up a slow Android phone
**1. Restart your phone first.** It sounds too simple, but a restart clears out all stuck background processes, frees up RAM, and dumps temporary system cache. If your phone has been running for weeks without a reboot, this alone can make a huge difference. Hold the power button, choose Restart, and give it two minutes after boot before judging speed. Get into the habit of restarting once a week.
**2. Clear the system cache partition.** Over time Android's system cache can become bloated or corrupted, causing lag and app crashes. Turn off your phone, then boot into recovery mode — the exact key combination varies by brand (usually Power + Volume Up, or Power + Volume Down). Use the volume keys to select **Wipe Cache Partition** and confirm with the power button. This deletes only temporary system files, not your personal data, and can resolve mysterious slowdowns.
**3. Uninstall bloatware and unused apps.** Pre-installed carrier and manufacturer apps — and apps you installed once and never opened — run background services, consume RAM, and eat storage. Go to **Settings > Apps**, sort by size or last used, and uninstall everything you do not recognize or need. For apps that cannot be uninstalled (system bloatware), choose **Disable** instead — this stops them from running and hides them from your app drawer. Aim to have no more than 40–50 apps installed total.
**4. Clear app cache for heavy apps.** Apps like Facebook, Instagram, Chrome, and TikTok cache hundreds of megabytes of data that can grow corrupt over time. Go to **Settings > Apps**, select the app, tap **Storage**, and choose **Clear Cache** (not Clear Data — that logs you out). Do this for your top five most-used apps. If an app is still misbehaving after clearing cache, then use Clear Data and sign back in.
**5. Disable or reduce animations.** Fancy transition animations look nice but make every action feel slower, especially on older or mid-range phones. Go to **Settings > About Phone** and tap **Build Number** seven times to unlock Developer Options. Then go to **Settings > System > Developer Options** and set **Window animation scale**, **Transition animation scale**, and **Animator duration scale** all to **0.5x** — or turn them off entirely. Your phone will feel instantly snappier.
**6. Switch to lite or go versions of apps.** Facebook, Messenger, Instagram, Twitter/X, and many others offer "Lite" versions designed for low-end phones. They use a fraction of the storage, RAM, and data, and run dramatically faster. For Google apps, use the **Go** editions (Google Go, Maps Go, Gallery Go) if your phone is low-end. Replacing just Facebook and Messenger with their Lite versions can free a gigabyte of RAM on many phones.
**7. Keep storage below 85% full.** This is one of the most important and least-known rules. When internal storage passes about 85% full, Android's flash storage slows down dramatically because it cannot efficiently perform garbage collection and wear-leveling. Go to **Settings > Storage** and check your usage. If you are above 85%, delete old photos, videos, downloads, and unused apps until you are below that threshold — you will feel the difference immediately.
**8. Run a malware scan.** Malware and adware run hidden background processes that consume CPU and battery. Install a reputable free scanner like Malwarebytes or Avast, run a full scan, and remove anything it finds. Be careful what you grant permissions to going forward — avoid "cleaner" and "booster" apps from unknown developers, as these are often the problem themselves.
**9. Update your OS and apps.** Outdated system software contains performance bugs and security holes that newer versions fix. Go to **Settings > System > Software Update** and install any pending update. Also open the Play Store, go to **Manage apps & device**, and tap **Update all** — app updates frequently include performance improvements. Reboot after updating.
**10. Turn off battery saver and power-saving restrictions.** Battery saver mode intentionally throttles your CPU, reduces background activity, and disables some visual effects to save power — which makes the phone feel slow. If you have it on permanently, turn it off in **Settings > Battery** when you need full performance. Also check **Settings > Battery > Background limits** and make sure apps you use frequently are not being put to "Deep sleep" — that causes delays when you open them.
**11. Factory reset as a last resort — and know when to upgrade.** If you have tried everything and the phone is still painfully slow, back up your data (photos to Google Photos, contacts to Google, files to a cloud drive) and do a factory reset via **Settings > System > Reset options > Erase all data**. Set it up fresh, install only the apps you actually need, and it will feel like a new phone. If it slows down again within weeks, the hardware is simply too old — RAM under 4 GB and processors more than 3–4 years old cannot keep up with modern apps. That is the signal it is time to upgrade rather than keep fighting it.
## Frequently asked questions
**Will a factory reset delete my photos and contacts?**
Yes — a factory reset erases everything on the phone. Back up first: photos and videos to Google Photos, contacts to your Google account, and any important files to Google Drive or a computer. After reset, sign in with the same Google account and most things restore automatically.
**Do "RAM booster" or "cleaner" apps actually help?**
Most do not — and many make things worse by running constantly in the background themselves. Android already manages RAM efficiently; killing apps manually just makes them relaunch slower. Stick to the built-in storage and cache tools, and avoid third-party booster apps entirely.
**How much RAM does an Android phone need in 2026?**
For smooth performance with modern apps, aim for at least 6 GB of RAM — 8 GB is the sweet spot, and 4 GB is the absolute minimum for basic use. If your phone has 3 GB or less, expect lag regardless of what you do; that is a hardware limitation, not a software fix.
**Why is my phone slow even with a fast processor?**
Usually one of three things: storage is over 85% full (step 7), too many apps are running in the background, or the phone is overheating and throttling the CPU. Check storage first, then uninstall/disable unused apps, and if the phone gets hot during use, remove its case and avoid using it while charging.
