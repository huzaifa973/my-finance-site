If your phone can't make it through the day anymore, you're not imagining it. Battery drain usually comes from a handful of settings and habits — not from a broken phone. Work through these fixes and most people get hours of extra life back without spending a cent.

## Find out what's draining your battery first

Don't guess — check. Your phone already tracks exactly which apps use the most power.

**On iPhone:** go to Settings > Battery and scroll to Battery Usage By App. Tap the clock icon to see background versus on-screen time. An app with huge background numbers is your prime suspect.

**On Android:** go to Settings > Battery > Battery usage (on Samsung: Settings > Battery and device care > Battery) for the same per-app breakdown. Whatever tops the list and surprises you is where your fix starts — usually a social app, navigation left running, or a game.

## Stop apps from running wild in the background

Background App Refresh lets apps fetch new content when you're not looking. Most apps don't need this privilege.

**On iPhone:** go to Settings > General > Background App Refresh. Switch it off entirely, or better, leave the master switch on and disable it per app — keep it for messaging and email, kill it for shopping apps, games, and social feeds.

**On Android:** open Settings > Apps, tap an app, then Mobile data & Wi-Fi (or Battery) and restrict background activity for apps you don't need live. On Pixel, Settings > Battery > Adaptive preferences also lets the system learn which apps to throttle automatically.

While you're at it, check location access. An app pinging your GPS all day is a silent battery killer. **On iPhone:** Settings > Privacy & Security > Location Services — set rarely-used apps to Never. **On Android:** Settings > Location > App location permissions, and switch infrequent apps to "Don't allow" or "Allow only while using the app."

## Tame the biggest drain of all: your screen

On most phones the display is the single largest power consumer, often 30–40% of total drain. Three settings make the biggest difference:

**1. Lower the brightness — or let the phone do it.** Turn on auto-brightness (iPhone: Settings > Accessibility > Display & Text Size > Auto-Brightness; Android: Settings > Display > Adaptive brightness). Manual max brightness outdoors is fine; indoors, full brightness is just wasted power.

**2. Shorten the screen timeout.** Your screen shouldn't stay on for two minutes after you set the phone down. Set it to 30 seconds or 1 minute: iPhone at Settings > Display & Brightness > Auto-Lock; Android at Settings > Display > Screen timeout.

**3. Lower the refresh rate if your phone allows it.** 120Hz displays are smooth but thirsty. On Samsung: Settings > Display > Motion smoothness > Standard (60Hz). On iPhone Pro models, Low Power Mode caps it automatically.

Also worth trying: dark mode on phones with OLED screens (most flagships), since black pixels on OLED draw almost no power.

## Check your battery health — know when it's the hardware

Sometimes the problem isn't settings at all; the battery itself is worn out. Lithium batteries lose capacity with every charge cycle, and after two to three years of daily charging, many hold only 75–85% of their original charge.

**On iPhone:** go to Settings > Battery > Battery Health & Charging. If Maximum Capacity is under 80%, Apple flags the battery as significantly degraded — a replacement (far cheaper than a new phone) will feel like a brand-new device.

**On Android:** there's no single built-in number on most phones, but Samsung shows battery status under Settings > Battery and device care > Diagnostics, and Pixel phones show cycle counts in Settings > About phone > Battery information. If your phone dies at 20% or shuts off in the cold, the battery is worn regardless.

## Charging habits that actually make batteries last

You don't need to baby your phone, but a few habits genuinely slow battery aging:

- **Use your phone's optimized charging.** iPhone: Settings > Battery > Battery Health & Charging > Optimized charge limit (set an 80% limit if you mostly charge overnight). Pixel: Settings > Battery > Adaptive charging. Samsung: Settings > Battery > Battery protection > Maximum.
- **Don't leave it baking.** Heat degrades batteries faster than anything else. Never leave your phone on a sunny dashboard or under a pillow while charging.
- **Any decent charger is fine.** You don't need the brand-name charger, just one from a reputable maker that supports your phone's standard (USB-PD for most). Avoid no-name gas-station chargers — unstable power delivery is what actually damages batteries.

## Quick wins worth doing today

If you want the fastest results, do these five in order — they take about ten minutes total:

1. Check battery usage and restrict the worst background offender.
2. Set screen timeout to 30 seconds and turn on adaptive brightness.
3. Turn off Background App Refresh (or background data) for apps you rarely open.
4. Switch location permission to "While Using" for everything except maps and ride apps.
5. Turn on your phone's battery saver for the last stretch of the day: iPhone at Settings > Battery > Low Power Mode; Android via the Quick Settings tile or Settings > Battery > Battery Saver.

## When to stop troubleshooting

If you've done everything above and the phone still dies by lunch, look for these red flags: the back of the phone is swelling or the screen is lifting (stop using it — a swollen battery is a safety issue), the phone gets hot while idle, or battery health is under 80%. At that point it's a hardware problem — a battery replacement or a new phone — and no setting will fix it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why does my phone battery drain overnight while I'm not using it?

Overnight drain is almost always background activity: apps refreshing content, push notifications waking the screen, or a weak signal forcing the radio to work harder. Turn off Background App Refresh for non-essential apps, silence notifications overnight, and keep the phone somewhere with decent signal — or switch on airplane mode if you don't need calls.

### Does closing apps from the app switcher save battery?

No — and it can actually use more. Both iOS and Android freeze unused apps in memory, which costs almost nothing. Force-closing and reopening an app repeatedly burns more power than leaving it alone. Only force-close an app that is misbehaving or stuck.

### Should I charge my phone to 100% every time?

Lithium batteries last longest when they spend less time at full charge and high heat. Use your phone's optimized charging feature (iPhone: Settings > Battery > Battery Health & Charging; Pixel: Settings > Battery > Adaptive charging) and don't stress about the occasional full charge — it's the daily habit that matters.

### Is fast charging bad for my battery?

Modern phones are designed for fast charging, and the charger and phone negotiate a safe rate. Heat is the real enemy, not speed — so avoid gaming or video calls while fast charging, and take thick cases off if the phone gets warm during a charge.

## The bottom line

Battery drain is a settings problem far more often than a hardware problem. Check your usage stats, rein in background activity, tame the screen, and adopt gentler charging habits — in that order. Most phones gain two to four hours of real-world life from those changes alone, and if they don't, the battery health screen will tell you it's time for a replacement.
