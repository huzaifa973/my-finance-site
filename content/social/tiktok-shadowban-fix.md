Your TikTok views suddenly fell off a cliff and you're wondering if you've been shadowbanned. First, some honesty: TikTok has never confirmed shadowbans exist, but creators widely report periods where reach collapses without any official warning. Here's how to tell what's happening and what to do about it.

## Signs you may be shadowbanned

- Your views drop 80–90% overnight across several videos, not just one flop.
- Your videos stop appearing on the For You page — check Analytics > Content to see traffic sources.
- Your videos don't show up under the hashtags you used when searched from a different account.
- Follower growth stalls completely even though you're posting as usual.

One bad video isn't a shadowban. Look for the pattern across five or more uploads.

## Common causes

- Posting content that brushes against Community Guidelines (borderline content, reused clips, misleading captions).
- Spam-like behavior: mass following, excessive hashtags, or posting ten videos in an hour.
- Using flagged or banned hashtags, sometimes without realizing it.
- Copyrighted music or reposted content that gets quietly suppressed rather than removed.

## How to check

**Search your own hashtags from another account.** Post a video with a niche hashtag, then look it up from a friend's account or a second profile. If your video doesn't appear under "Recent," your reach is being limited.

**Check your Account status.** In TikTok, go to Profile, tap the menu icon, then Settings and privacy. Look for the Account status or Safety Center section — it shows any active violations or strikes against your account.

**Review your analytics.** Tap the menu on your profile, go to Creator tools > Analytics, and look at video views and For You traffic over the last 7–28 days. A sudden, sustained drop in For You percentage points to suppression rather than bad content.

## How to fix it

**1. Stop posting for 48–72 hours.** This is the most commonly reported fix. A short break lets any temporary flag expire instead of stacking more suppressed videos on top of it.

**2. Delete anything borderline.** Remove videos that might have triggered the limit — reposted clips, edgy jokes, or anything with a warning label. Then avoid that type of content for a while.

**3. Appeal any violations.** If Account status shows a strike you disagree with, submit an appeal through the app. Successful appeals often restore reach quickly.

**4. Clean up your hashtags.** Drop any hashtag that returns no results or a warning when searched — those are banned. Stick to a few relevant, active hashtags per video.

**5. Switch to a personal account and back.** Some creators report this resets recommendation flags. Go to Settings and privacy > Manage account > Switch to Personal Account, wait a day, then switch back to Creator or Business.

## The bottom line

Most "shadowbans" are temporary limits that lift within one to two weeks if you pause posting, remove risky content, and come back with original, guideline-safe videos. If your Account status is clean and reach doesn't recover after two weeks of normal posting, your content — not a ban — is likely the issue, and that's a strategy problem to solve.
