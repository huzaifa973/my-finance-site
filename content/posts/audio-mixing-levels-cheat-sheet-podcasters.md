Muddy audio loses listeners faster than a weak topic. When the music drowns the voice, the guest sounds distant, or the final export is far quieter than every other show, people reach for the skip button — even if the content is excellent. This cheat sheet gives you fifty concrete mixing level targets for voices, instruments, music beds, sound effects, and final loudness, so you can mix with numbers instead of guesswork. It is built for podcasters, streamers, musicians recording at home, and video creators who want clean, professional-sounding audio without a studio budget.

## What's inside this resource

The targets start with the voice, because the voice is the star of almost every podcast and video: lead and backing vocals, podcast speech, and narration. Then come instruments in the order you usually meet them, from guitars and drums to synths and strings, followed by effects, ambience, and the sends that add space. The final section covers the master bus and loudness targets for streaming, podcast, and CD delivery, including the headroom you must leave so your audio never clips. Levels are given in dBFS peaks and LUFS loudness, the two numbers that matter most in a home mix.

## How to use this resource

1. Start your mix with the voice at its target level and build everything else around it — never mix music first and squeeze the voice in afterward.
2. Set each track's peak near its cheat sheet target, then adjust by ear in small steps, since every recording is a little different.
3. Check the voice against music beds: speech should sit clearly above background music, roughly 15 to 20 dB louder.
4. Leave at least 6 dB of headroom on the master bus before mastering, and check loudness in LUFS, not just peaks.
5. Listen to your final export on phone speakers, earbuds, and a car stereo — if the voice stays clear everywhere, the mix is done.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between dBFS and LUFS?

dBFS measures peak level against digital clipping, where 0 is the maximum and going over means distortion. LUFS measures perceived loudness over time, which is what streaming platforms normalize to. You need both: peaks to avoid clipping, LUFS to match the loudness listeners expect.

### Why does my podcast sound quieter than other shows?

Usually because its integrated loudness is far below the -16 LUFS podcast standard. A limiter on the master bus, set to catch peaks at -1 dB true peak, lets you raise overall loudness without distortion and match other shows.

### Should I mix with headphones or speakers?

Use both if you can. Headphones reveal detail and noise, while speakers reveal balance and low-end problems. At minimum, check your finished mix on the devices your audience actually uses, especially phone speakers and earbuds.
