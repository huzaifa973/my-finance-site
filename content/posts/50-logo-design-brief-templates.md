Most logo projects go wrong before any design starts — not because the designer lacks skill, but because the brief was vague. 'Make it modern' means ten different things to ten different people, and a designer guessing at your taste will burn revisions fast. A strong brief closes that gap.

These 50 brief questions and prompts cover everything a designer needs: your business, your audience, your competitors, your style preferences, colors, typography, usage, and budget. Clients can answer them to write a clear brief; designers can send them to new clients as an intake form. Either way, the result is fewer revisions and a logo that actually fits.

If you are a client, one extra step makes this brief even stronger: build a small mood board before you answer. Collect five to ten images — logos, packaging, photography, even architecture — that feel like your brand, and five that feel like the opposite. You do not need design vocabulary to do this; gut reactions are exactly what a designer needs. If you are a designer, ask for that mood board alongside the answers, and confirm your understanding in a one-paragraph creative direction before you sketch. That single paragraph prevents more wasted revisions than any other habit in the process.

## What's inside this resource

The questions are grouped into eight sections: business basics, audience, competitors, brand personality, visual direction, color and typography, practical usage, and project details. Answer every section honestly — including what you dislike, which is often more informative than what you like. The downloadable file is the full list in plain text, ready to paste into an email, a form, or a project document.

## How to use this resource

Clients: answer in full sentences, not single words, and include links to logos you admire and ones you dislike, with a note on why. Designers: send this list as your intake questionnaire and use the answers to write a one-page creative direction before sketching anything. Agree on the direction in writing first, and set the number of revision rounds in advance.

## Frequently asked questions

### How detailed should a logo brief be?

Detailed enough that a stranger could describe your brand's look and feel back to you. One or two pages of real answers beats a paragraph of adjectives.

### Should I include logos I like in my brief?

Yes — with a note on what you like about each one. References are the fastest way to align taste, but explain the why so the designer does not just copy them.

### What if I don't know what style I want?

Say so, and describe your audience and competitors instead. A good designer can propose directions from that — but expect a discovery round before final concepts.
