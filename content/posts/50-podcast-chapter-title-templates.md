Chapter markers are the table of contents for a podcast episode — they let listeners jump to the part they care about, and they show up in players that support chapters. But most episodes ship with chapters like 'Part 1' and 'Part 2', which help nobody. These fifty templates give every section of an episode a clear, skimmable title, across five common topics. They are written for podcasters, audio editors, and show producers adding chapters to new or back-catalog episodes.

## What's inside this resource

The templates follow the shape of a real episode: an introduction, why the topic matters, the story behind it, getting started, common mistakes, tools, listener questions, the future, quick wins, and the takeaways. Write chapter titles the way listeners scan — topic first, promise second. 'Tools for budget travel' beats 'Some tools I like' because a scanning listener knows in one second whether to jump in or skip ahead.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do podcast chapters help with discovery?

Indirectly. Chapters improve the listening experience, which improves completion and retention — and players increasingly surface chaptered episodes more prominently.

### How long should a chapter title be?

Short enough to read at a glance — under 40 characters is ideal. If the title wraps to two lines in most players, trim it.

### Should old episodes get chapters?

If an episode still gets listens, yes. Adding chapters to a popular back-catalog episode is a quick win that makes it feel current again.
