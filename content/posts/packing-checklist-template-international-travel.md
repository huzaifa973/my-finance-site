Every traveler has a story about the thing they forgot — the power adapter, the prescription, the visa printout discovered missing at check-in. International trips punish forgetfulness harder than domestic ones because replacements are expensive, far away, or simply unavailable. This free packing checklist template for international travel covers the full inventory: documents, money, clothing, toiletries, electronics, health items, carry-on essentials, and the before-you-leave-home sweep. It is built as a check-off list you can print or copy into a notes app, and it is deliberately complete — delete what does not fit your trip rather than trying to remember categories from scratch at midnight before an early flight.

## What's inside this checklist

The template is organized into eight groups in packing order. Documents and essentials (8 items) comes first because it is the only category that can end a trip before it starts — passport validity, visas, bookings, and backups. Money (4 items) covers cards, bank notifications, and the backup-card rule. Clothing (12 items) is built around mix-and-match layering plus the extras that prevent laundry emergencies. Toiletries (9 items), electronics (7 items), and health and comfort (7 items) handle the personal kit. Carry-on essentials (7 items) is the never-check-these list, and the before-you-leave-home sweep (8 items) secures the house you are leaving behind. Sixty-plus items total, each one checkable.

## How to use this checklist

1. Download the full checklist a week before your trip and delete the lines that do not apply.
2. Add trip-specific items the template cannot know: event outfits, gear for planned activities, gifts.
3. Pack the carry-on essentials first — documents, medications, valuables, one change of clothes.
4. Check items off as they go into the bag, not as you think of them; the list only works if the check means "packed."
5. Do the before-you-leave-home sweep last, with the front door in sight.

The golden rule of the whole list: if losing it would ruin the trip, it goes in the carry-on. Everything else is replaceable.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the most forgotten item when packing for international travel?**
Power adapters and prescription medications top every survey. Pack both in your carry-on first, before anything else, and the rest of the list becomes low-stakes — almost everything else can be bought at your destination.

**How far in advance should I start packing?**
Start the list a week out and pack the bag two to three days before departure. The gap between listing and packing is when you remember the things you almost forgot, and it leaves time to buy what is missing.

**Should I roll or fold clothes when packing?**
Rolling wins for most travelers: it saves space, reduces wrinkles in casual fabrics, and makes items visible in the suitcase. Fold structured items like blazers that rolling would crease badly.
