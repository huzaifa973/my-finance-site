Sending out a draft contract, a price quote, or a design proof? A **text watermark** — that faint diagonal "DRAFT" or "CONFIDENTIAL" stamped across each page — is the simplest way to mark a document's status and discourage unauthorized sharing. Here's how to add one to any PDF free, online, in about a minute.

## Why watermark your PDFs?

Text watermarks solve real everyday problems:

- **Status signaling.** DRAFT, FINAL, APPROVED, or FOR REVIEW tells every reader exactly where the document stands — no confusion, no outdated versions circulating as current.
- **Confidentiality.** A CONFIDENTIAL or DO NOT DISTRIBUTE watermark reminds recipients the document isn't for forwarding, and it looks intentional in a way an email note doesn't.
- **Ownership.** Stamping your company name (plus a date) across preview copies makes it obvious where a leaked document came from.
- **Sample protection.** Course creators, photographers, and designers watermark previews so the free sample can't substitute for the paid product.

A watermark won't stop a determined thief — but it stops casual misuse, which is 95% of the problem.

## How to add a text watermark online (4 steps)

The quickest path is a free browser-based tool — no installs, no accounts, and your file never leaves your device:

1. **Upload your PDF** to our [free PDF text watermark tool](/tools/pdf-text-watermark/). It processes everything locally in your browser, so confidential documents stay confidential.
2. **Type your watermark text.** "CONFIDENTIAL," "DRAFT," your company name — keep it to 1–3 words for the cleanest look.

4. **Apply to all pages and download.** The watermark stamps every page at once. Download your watermarked PDF — done.

## Getting the look right

A bad watermark screams amateur; a good one is barely noticed until needed. Follow these rules:

- **Opacity: 15–30%.** Below 15% it's invisible when printed; above 30% it fights the content for attention. Start at 20%.
- **Diagonal for status, horizontal for branding.** The 45° angle is the universal "this is a draft/confidential" signal. A subtle horizontal footer watermark works better for persistent branding.
- **Size matters.** The text should span much of the page width — timid little watermarks in the corner get cropped out of screenshots.
- **Color with intent.** Medium gray for neutral status marks, red for CONFIDENTIAL/URGENT, your brand color for ownership marks.
- **Mind the background.** On image-heavy pages, a light watermark can disappear. Slightly higher opacity or a darker tone fixes it — preview a photo page before finalizing.

## Text watermark vs image watermark

**Text watermarks** (this guide) are best for status labels and confidentiality notices — quick to create, easy to read, professional.

**Image watermarks** (logos, signatures, seals) suit branding and official documents. If you need a logo stamped on every page, use an image watermark tool instead — text can't replicate a logo.

Many documents benefit from both: a diagonal "DRAFT" text watermark plus a small logo in the footer.

## Watermark wording for common situations

Picking the right word matters — it sets the legal and practical tone of the document:

- **DRAFT** — work in progress; signals the content may change. Standard for contracts and proposals under review.
- **CONFIDENTIAL** — restricted distribution. Use for internal reports, financials, and anything under NDA.
- **SAMPLE / PREVIEW** — marketing copies and trial content. Tells the reader this isn't the full product.
- **DO NOT COPY / DO NOT DISTRIBUTE** — stronger deterrent for sensitive material, though still not technically enforceable on its own.
- **Your company name + date** — ownership marking. Ideal for portfolios, quotes, and design proofs where you want credit if the file travels.

Avoid stacking multiple watermarks ("DRAFT CONFIDENTIAL DO NOT COPY SAMPLE") — one clear word beats four competing ones. If you genuinely need two signals, put the status diagonally and the company name small in the footer.

## What watermarks can't do

Be honest about the limits:

- **They don't prevent copying.** Anyone can still copy text or screenshot pages. Watermarks deter; they don't lock.
- **They can be removed** by someone with PDF editing software and motivation. For sensitive documents, pair watermarks with real restrictions: password-protect the PDF and disable copying/printing where the format allows.
- **They're permanent.** Once applied and saved, the watermark is baked into the pages. Always keep an unwatermarked original.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I add a text watermark to a PDF for free?**
Upload your PDF to a free online watermark tool, type your watermark text (e.g. CONFIDENTIAL), choose position, opacity, size, and rotation, then download the watermarked PDF. It runs in your browser with no signup.

**Can I remove a text watermark from a PDF later?**
Only if you kept the original file. A properly applied watermark becomes part of the page content, so always save a clean copy before watermarking. Watermarks are meant to be permanent.

**What's the best text for a PDF watermark?**
Use clear status labels: DRAFT, CONFIDENTIAL, SAMPLE, DO NOT DISTRIBUTE, or your company name plus a date. Keep it short — one to three words reads best at an angle across the page.

**Will a watermark prevent copying my PDF content?**
No — a watermark discourages misuse and proves ownership, but it does not technically block copying. For real protection, combine watermarks with password restrictions on copying and printing.
