Great tutorial videos are made before you press record. The difference between a tutorial people finish and one they abandon is rarely the presenter's skill — it is preparation: clean audio, a readable screen, a plan for what happens on it, and an editing pass that respects the viewer's time. Viewers forgive a lot, but they never forgive bad audio or a rambling twenty minutes that should have been eight.

This free checklist gives you 30 steps across the four stages of a tutorial recording: setup, recording, editing, and publishing. It is written for educators, course creators, software trainers, and YouTubers making how-to content. Work through it in order and your tutorials will look and sound deliberate from the first upload.

## What's inside this checklist

The checklist follows the real production order. Phase 1 covers setup: your recording software, audio chain, screen preparation, and a run-through of the demo so nothing surprises you mid-take. Phase 2 covers recording technique: pacing, narration habits, and what to do when you make a mistake. Phase 3 is the editing pass that turns raw footage into a tight tutorial. Phase 4 handles publishing: thumbnails, titles, chapters, and the description that helps people find the video.

## The full 30-step screen recording checklist

Prepare more than you think you need to — every minute of setup saves five minutes of editing.

## How to use this checklist

1. Run through phase 1 completely before your first take — most recording disasters are setup failures, not talent failures.
2. Record in short segments using the mistake-marker technique, so editing becomes cutting rather than rescuing.
3. Edit with the viewer's time as your metric: if a section does not teach something, cut it.
4. Publish with an outcome-focused title, a simple thumbnail, and chapters in the description.
5. After 48 hours, check audience retention graphs to find where viewers drop off, and fix that section in your next tutorial.

The uncomfortable truth about tutorials: your first ones will be too long. Watch your retention graph honestly — the cliff where viewers leave is telling you exactly what to cut. Creators who tighten their pacing based on that data improve faster than creators who buy better gear.

## Frequently asked questions

**What screen recording software should I use?**
OBS Studio is the best starting point: free, open-source, and capable of screen capture, webcam picture-in-picture, and multi-track audio. Paid tools like Camtasia or ScreenFlow add conveniences such as automatic smart zoom and simpler editing timelines. Start free; upgrade only when a specific missing feature is costing you real time.

**What resolution should I record my screen at?**
Record at 1920x1080 as a minimum. If you work on a 4K monitor, you can record at native resolution and downscale at export for extra sharpness, or simply set your recording canvas to 1080p. The critical rule: UI text must be readable on a phone screen after the platform compresses your upload, so zoom the interface to 125-150% during recording.

**How do I avoid background noise in my recordings?**
Control the room first, the gear second. Record in the quietest room available, close doors and windows, turn off fans and humming electronics, and put your phone on silent. Use a directional microphone positioned close to your mouth, and record a few seconds of room tone at the start so you can remove noise cleanly in editing. A cheap microphone in a quiet room always beats an expensive one in a noisy room.
