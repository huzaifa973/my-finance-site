A great game night does not need a stocked game shelf or a catering budget. It needs people, a plan, and enough structure that nobody sits around asking "so... what are we doing?" This checklist walks you through hosting a memorable night for friends on roughly $15–$25 total — games, snacks, setup timeline, and all.

## Set the basics first: date, headcount, and budget

Everything else flows from three decisions:

1. **Pick the date.** A Friday or Saturday evening works for most schedules. Send the invite 5–7 days out — short enough that people commit, long enough that they can plan.
2. **Lock the headcount.** Five to eight guests is the sweet spot: enough for team games and buzz, few enough that nobody waits out a turn. Cap the invite list explicitly ("bringing 6–8 people") so you can plan snacks and seating.
3. **Set your real budget.** Decide a number up front — $20 is plenty. The biggest budget killer is vague "I'll figure it out" spending at the store. Write the number down and shop to it.

**Invite checklist:**

- Send a group text or chat message with the **date, start time, address, and end time** (an end time like "wrapping by 11" stops the night from drifting)
- State the food plan: "snacks provided, BYOB" or "bring a snack to share"
- Ask about **dietary needs** (one vegan or gluten-free guest changes the snack math)
- Ask who can bring a **folding chair** if seating is tight
- Confirm headcount two days before with a simple "still on for Saturday?"

## Pick games that cost nothing

You do not need to buy a single game. Some of the best group games ever invented need nothing but people:

- **Mafia / Werewolf** — 6+ players, zero equipment, endless replay value. One person learns the rules on YouTube and moderates.
- **Two Truths and a Lie** — perfect icebreaker, takes five minutes, works for any group size.
- **Charades or Pictionary** — paper, pens, and a phone timer. Split into teams for competition.
- **DIY trivia** — write 20 questions across easy/medium/hard rounds on your phone. Categories: movies, music, "stuff about us" (inside jokes score big).
- **Card games** — one $3 deck of cards unlocks dozens of group games: President, BS, Spoons (grab real spoons from your kitchen).
- **Phone party games** — several well-known party game apps have free tiers that work on one shared phone screen.

**Game-night structure that works:** plan a 3-hour arc. Start with a 15-minute icebreaker (Two Truths), run one main game (60–90 minutes), take a snack break, then close with something short and loud (a rapid trivia final or charades lightning round). End on a high-energy game, not a long one — people remember the last thing.

## Snack math: feed 8 people for under $15

Snacks are where game nights feel generous or cheap, and the secret is buying the right *volume* per person rather than fancy items. Budget roughly **6–8 ounces of snacks per person** for a 3-hour night.

**The $15 shopping list for 8 people:**

- **Popcorn** — 2 large bags (or kernels popped at home): ~$2
- **Tortilla chips + salsa** — one big bag + one jar: ~$4
- **Pretzels or crackers** — one family bag: ~$2.50
- **A sweet option** — a box of cookies or a bag of candy: ~$2.50
- **Something fresh** — baby carrots or apples (balance matters after hour two): ~$2
- **Drinks base** — a 2-liter of soda or a pitcher of lemonade: ~$2

Total: about **$15**. The two upgrades that cost nothing: declare **BYOB for alcohol** (stated in the invite, never awkward) and make it **potluck-style for extras** ("bring a snack to share if you want") — half your guests will happily contribute, stretching the spread without spending more.

Setup trick: put snacks in bowls, not bags. It looks abundant, slows grazing, and keeps greasy fingers off phones and cards.

## Setup timeline: the night runs itself

Thirty minutes of setup on the day makes the difference between a host who plays and a host who scrambles.

**One week before:**

- Send invites with date, time, address, food plan, and BYOB note
- Pick your 2–3 games and learn the rules of any you have not played

**Two days before:**

- Confirm headcount ("still on for Saturday?")
- Buy non-perishable snacks

**Day of — 2 hours before:**

- Buy anything fresh (ice, fruit, drinks to chill)
- Clear the main table and arrange seating in a circle or around it
- Set up a snack station *away* from the game area (spills happen)

**Day of — 30 minutes before:**

- Put snacks in bowls, drinks on ice
- Queue up a background playlist (instrumental or low-key — music with lyrics fights with conversation)
- Set out phones, paper, pens, and the deck of cards
- Do a 2-minute rules refresher for yourself on the first game

**During the night:** start on time even if one person is late — waiting for stragglers kills momentum and punishes the punctual. Explain each game's rules in under 2 minutes (demo a round instead of lecturing). And keep a rough eye on energy: when a game drags, call it and move to the next one. Nobody remembers the game that overstayed.

## After: the 10-minute reset

When the night ends, thank people in the group chat the next morning ("great night — rematch next month?"). That one message turns a one-off hangout into a tradition. Cleanup is genuinely 10 minutes if the snack station was separate: bowls in the dishwasher, trash bag out, chairs back. Future-you will thank present-you for hosting again.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much does it cost to host a game night for friends?**

A solid game night can cost $15–$25 total if you lean on free games, potluck-style snacks, and a BYOB drink policy. The venue is free (your place), and the games themselves are mostly printable, phone-based, or classics everyone knows.

**What are the best free games for a game night?**

The best free options need zero purchases: Mafia/Werewolf, Two Truths and a Lie, charades, Pictionary on paper, trivia you write yourself, and phone-based party games. A deck of cards alone supports dozens of group games.

**How many people is ideal for a game night?**

Five to eight people is the sweet spot — enough for team games and lively energy, but small enough that everyone plays instead of watching. For larger groups, split into two simultaneous games.

**How far in advance should I plan a game night?**

Invite people 5–7 days ahead so they can clear their schedules. The actual planning — games, snacks, setup — takes about 2–3 hours spread across the week, plus 30 minutes of same-day setup.
