## Why Convert PDF to PNG?

PDFs are great for documents, but images win in plenty of situations:

- **Sharing on social media** — platforms display images natively; PDFs get ignored or rejected.
- **Presentations** — drop a page straight into PowerPoint or Google Slides as a crisp image.
- **Thumbnails and previews** — show a document's contents at a glance on a website.
- **Printing control** — a PNG locks in exactly how the page looks, avoiding font and layout shifts.
- **Editing** — crop, annotate, or combine pages in any image editor.

PNG is the right image format for this job: it's lossless, so text and line art stay sharp, and it supports transparency. ## Choosing the Right Quality (DPI)

DPI — dots per inch — controls how sharp your PNG looks and how big the file is. Pick based on what you're doing with the image:

- **72–96 DPI:** quick previews and thumbnails. Small files, noticeably soft text. Fine for drafts.
- **150 DPI:** the sweet spot for screen use — presentations, websites, social media, chat apps. Text looks crisp at normal viewing sizes.
- **300 DPI:** print quality. Use this when the PNG will be printed or zoomed heavily. Files are much larger.
- **Above 300 DPI:** rarely needed. Only for professional print work or extreme close-ups.

A common mistake is converting at maximum quality "just in case" and ending up with a 50 MB image for a Slack message. Match the DPI to the job and you'll save yourself resizing headaches later.

## Tips for the Best Results

- **Convert only the pages you need.** A 200-page PDF becomes 200 images — selecting a page range keeps things fast and organized.
- **Check text sharpness.** After converting, zoom into the PNG at 100%. If small text looks blurry, bump the DPI up and re-convert.
- **Watch the file size.** 300 DPI pages can be several megabytes each. For web use, 150 DPI usually looks identical while being a fraction of the size.
- **Keep the original PDF.** PNGs are flattened images — you can't select text or edit the layout afterward. Always keep the source file.
- **Name your downloads.** Rename files as you download (e.g. `report-page-3.png`) so they don't end up as `page-1.png` in a folder full of other projects' page 1s.
