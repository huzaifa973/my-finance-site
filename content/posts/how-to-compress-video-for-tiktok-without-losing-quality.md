You filmed a crisp video, but the file is 400MB — too big to upload comfortably, and TikTok's own compression turns it into a blurry mess anyway. The fix isn't "compress harder." It's compressing **smarter**: giving TikTok a file that's already in its favorite format, so its recompression has less damage to do.

Here's exactly how to do it, free, on any device.

## Why TikTok Uploads Look Blurry

TikTok recompresses **every** video you upload — no exceptions. The quality you end up with depends on what you feed its encoder:

- **Good source** (1080p, H.264, healthy bitrate) → light recompression → crisp result.
- **Bad source** (4K 200MB file, odd codec, vertical video exported as square) → heavy recompression → mush.

Counterintuitively, uploading a massive 4K file doesn't help — TikTok downscales it to 1080p anyway, and the extra data just slows your upload. The sweet spot is a file that already matches TikTok's specs.

## TikTok's Recommended Upload Specs

| Setting | Recommended value |
|---------|-------------------|
| Resolution | 1080 x 1920 (9:16 vertical) |
| Format | MP4 (MOV also works) |
| Video codec | H.264 |
| Audio codec | AAC, 128 kbps+ |
| Frame rate | 30 fps (60 fps for action) |
| Bitrate | 8–12 Mbps for 1080p |
| Max file size | 287MB (iOS) / 72MB (Android) |
| Max length | 10 minutes (uploads); 60 min for some accounts |

Match these and TikTok's encoder does minimal damage. Miss them — especially resolution and codec — and quality drops fast.

## Doing It Manually: Settings That Preserve Quality

If you're exporting from an editor (CapCut, Premiere, DaVinci Resolve) or using a converter with manual controls:

### Resolution: 1080x1920, always

- Export vertical video at exactly **1080 x 1920**. Not 4K (wasted bytes), not 720p (TikTok upscales it and it looks soft).
- If your footage is square or horizontal, crop or reframe to 9:16 rather than letterboxing — pillarboxed bars waste bitrate on black pixels.

### Codec: H.264 + AAC

- **H.264** is TikTok's native language. H.265/HEVC files get transcoded harder and sometimes glitch on older Android phones.
- **AAC audio at 128–192 kbps** — don't starve the audio; tinny sound kills retention as fast as blurry video.

### Bitrate: the quality dial

Bitrate is the real lever. Too low = blocky; too high = wasted megabytes:

- **1080p talking head / static scenes:** 8 Mbps is plenty.
- **1080p movement, dancing, sports:** 10–12 Mbps.
- **60 fps:** add ~50% to the above.

A 60-second video at 10 Mbps is about 75MB — comfortably under limits and crisp after TikTok's pass.

### Frame rate: match your footage

Export at the frame rate you shot (usually 30fps). Converting 30→60fps doesn't add smoothness — it just doubles the data TikTok has to throw away.

## 5 Mistakes That Ruin TikTok Quality

1. **Uploading over a weak connection.** TikTok sometimes serves a lower-quality version if the upload stalls. Use Wi-Fi.
2. **Letting the app "save data."** In TikTok settings, turn on "Upload HD" / high-quality uploads.
3. **Screen-recording instead of exporting.** A screen recording of your edit is already compressed once — then TikTok compresses again. Always export the project file directly.
4. **Adding text in a separate app at low resolution.** Burn captions in at 1080p so they stay sharp.
5. **Re-uploading a downloaded TikTok.** Downloaded videos carry TikTok's watermark and compression; re-uploading compounds the damage. Always keep your original export.
