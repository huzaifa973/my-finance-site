"How many times should I use my keyword?" is one of the most common questions in SEO writing — and the answer has quietly changed over the years. Modern search engines read topics, not tally marks. But counting keywords is still genuinely useful: it keeps you consistent, catches accidental stuffing, and reveals whether your article covers the topic with enough natural variety. Here is how to do it right, with a free tool that does the counting for you.

## Why count keywords at all?

Counting is a diagnostic, not a target. Run your article through a counter and you learn three things fast:

- **Consistency:** did you actually use your primary keyword, or did you accidentally write 1,500 words about "budgeting apps" while targeting "budget planner"? Drift is common in long articles.
- **Stuffing risk:** a primary keyword at 5% density reads like a robot wrote it. The count flags the problem before you publish.
- **Topical depth:** the most frequent words in your article should be on-topic words. If the top terms are filler ("things," "stuff," "very"), the article probably lacks substance — or at least substance that a search engine can detect.

1. **Paste your article text** into the counter. Include the full draft: headings, body, and FAQ.

5. **Spot the filler.** If generic words dominate the top of the list, rewrite those sentences with specific, on-topic language.

## The density question: what is "right"?

Search engines have never published an ideal keyword density, and the old 2–3% rules come from an era when algorithms were far more literal. Today, the honest answer is: **use your keyword naturally, then verify with the count.**

As a sanity range, most experienced SEOs land somewhere around:

- **Primary keyword:** roughly 1–2% of total words, appearing in the intro, one or two headings, and naturally through the body.
- **Secondary keywords:** a handful of mentions each, woven into relevant sections.
- **Related terms and synonyms:** scattered freely — there is no upper limit on natural variation.

If your primary keyword lands at 0.3%, you probably drifted off-topic. If it lands at 4%+, read the article aloud — you will hear the stuffing yourself.

## Beyond the count: what search engines actually reward

Keyword counts are a hygiene check. What actually moves rankings in 2026 is everything the count cannot measure:

- **Intent coverage:** does the article answer what the searcher really wanted, including the follow-up questions? An FAQ section handles this well.
- **Entity richness:** people, brands, places, and concepts related to the topic. Articles that mention the real entities in a space look authoritative.
- **Structure:** clear headings, short paragraphs, lists, and tables. Structure is how readers (and crawlers) scan.
- **Original angles:** firsthand experience, data, examples, and opinions — the things that distinguish your article from the ten other results.

Count keywords to keep yourself honest, then spend your real energy on these.

## A quick pre-publish checklist

Run through this before hitting publish on any SEO article:

1. Primary keyword appears in the title, first 100 words, and at least one H2.
2. Density sits in the sane 1–2% range — not 0.3%, not 5%.
3. Two to four related terms appear naturally in the body.
4. No paragraph repeats the keyword twice in a forced way.
5. The FAQ answers the "people also ask" style questions on the topic.

## Common keyword-counting mistakes

Counting is simple, but a few mistakes make the numbers misleading:

- **Counting before the draft is done.** Early drafts shift constantly; count on the near-final version or you will chase a moving target.
- **Forgetting headings and alt text.** Keywords in H2s and image alt attributes count toward topical relevance — include them in the pasted text.
- **Treating all mentions equally.** A keyword in the title and first paragraph carries more weight than one buried in paragraph twelve. Density is a flat metric; placement is not.
- **Chasing the number instead of the reader.** If hitting 1.5% requires an awkward sentence, skip it. One natural mention beats two forced ones every time.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a good keyword density for SEO?
There is no official ideal density, but most guides aim for 1–2% for the primary keyword — mentioned naturally, not forced. Modern search engines rank on overall topic coverage, so variety matters more than repetition.

### How do I check how many times a keyword appears in my article?
Paste your article into a free keyword counter tool. It scans the text and reports how often each word or phrase appears, plus total word count and density percentages.

### What is keyword stuffing and does it still hurt rankings?
Keyword stuffing is repeating a keyword unnaturally to manipulate rankings. Yes, it still hurts — Google penalizes obvious stuffing and, more importantly, readers bounce from robotic-sounding content.

### Should I count LSI keywords too?
Yes. Related terms and synonyms show search engines your article covers the topic in depth. A keyword counter that lists all frequent words helps you spot whether you are using enough natural variation.
