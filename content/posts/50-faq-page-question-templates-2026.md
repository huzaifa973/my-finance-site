A good FAQ page answers the questions visitors actually ask, before they have to email you or leave your site. It also quietly helps your search rankings: well-written FAQ content can appear directly in search results, and it builds trust by showing you have nothing to hide. The hard part is knowing which questions to include in the first place.

These 50 templates cover the questions nearly every website needs: pricing, ordering, shipping, returns, accounts, privacy, and support. Use them as a starting point, keep the ones that fit your business, rewrite the answers in your own words, and delete anything that does not apply. A shorter, honest FAQ beats a long, generic one every time.

A few writing habits make the difference between an FAQ page visitors trust and one they skip. Answer in your own brand voice, not in legalese — if your business is friendly, your FAQ should sound friendly. Avoid answers that only say 'it depends' without explaining what it depends on. Where an answer involves a number, a date, or a deadline, state it plainly instead of hiding behind 'contact us for details.' And keep the page alive: set a reminder every quarter to re-read it, remove questions nobody asks anymore, and add the new ones your support inbox keeps producing.

## What's inside this resource

The templates are grouped by theme so you can lift a whole section at once: getting started, pricing and payment, ordering and delivery, returns and refunds, accounts and security, and support. Adapt the wording to your product or service, and write answers that are specific — vague answers are worse than no answer.

## How to use this resource

Pick the questions your customers genuinely ask — check your inbox, support tickets, and comments for the real ones, then fill gaps from this list. Write each answer in one or two short paragraphs, and link to the relevant page (pricing, contact, or policy) where it helps. Keep the page updated: every time the same question arrives twice, add it to the FAQ. If you use FAQ schema markup, your answers may appear as rich results in search.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many questions should an FAQ page have?

There is no fixed number. Most effective FAQ pages have between 10 and 30 questions. Start with the questions customers ask most, and grow the page as new ones appear.

### Should FAQ answers be short or detailed?

Short and direct first — answer in the first sentence, then add detail if needed. Visitors scan FAQ pages, so put the key fact up front.

### Can FAQ pages help with SEO?

Yes. FAQ content targets question-based searches, and adding FAQ schema markup can make your answers eligible for rich results in Google search.
