The best memes look effortless — a perfectly timed caption on a familiar image, shared a thousand times before lunch. ## The anatomy of a good meme

Before you open any tool, understand what makes memes work:

- **Top text sets it up, bottom text delivers the punchline.** This classic two-line structure is instantly readable — your brain knows where to look.
- **Timing beats polish.** A meme about today's trending topic posted today beats a pixel-perfect meme posted next week. Speed matters more than design.
- **Keep text short.** The funniest memes are usually under 15 words total. If you need a paragraph, it's not a meme — it's a blog post wearing a costume.
- **Relatability is the currency.** The best memes say what everyone was already thinking. "Me pretending I understand the meeting" works because we've all been there.
- **The image does half the work.** Pick a template whose expression or situation already matches your joke — the caption just completes it.

## Caption and style tips

- **Use the classic look:** bold white Impact-style text with a thick black outline, centered. It reads clearly over any background and it's what people expect.
- **Mind the contrast:** if your image is bright, the black outline is what saves your text. Never use thin colored text on a busy photo.
- **Test at small size:** most people see your meme on a phone. Zoom out to thumbnail size — if you can't read it there, shorten the text or enlarge the font.
- **Line breaks matter:** split long captions across two lines rather than letting them shrink to fit. Two readable lines beat one tiny one.
- **Don't over-explain the joke:** the funniest caption trusts the reader to get it. Cut the setup to its bones.

## Where to post (and what size to use)

Different platforms crop differently — match your meme's shape to where it's going:

- **Square (1080×1080):** the universal meme size. Works for Instagram feed, Facebook, X, Reddit, and WhatsApp forwards without cropping.
- **Vertical 9:16 (1080×1920):** for Instagram Stories, Reels, TikTok, and YouTube Shorts. Leave the top and bottom ~15% clear of text so profile icons and captions don't cover your punchline.
- **Landscape (1200×630):** best for Facebook link-style posts and embedding in articles.

A good habit: create the meme once in square format, then re-export a 9:16 version with bigger text for stories. Two minutes of extra work, double the reach.

## Copyright etiquette for meme makers

Memes live in a legal gray area, and for personal sharing nobody will bother you — but know the basics:

- **Use original or widely-shared templates** for casual posting. Classic reaction images that have circulated for years are culturally "public," even if technically copyrighted.
- **Credit photographers when you can.** If your meme uses someone's recognizable photo (not a movie still), a credit in the caption costs nothing.
- **Don't monetize others' content.** Selling merch with a celebrity's face or a studio's film still on it is where "funny meme" becomes "lawsuit."
- **For brand or business accounts,** the safest path is original templates: your own photos, screenshots you took, or properly licensed stock images. Original memes also stand out more than the 10,000th Drake-format repost.
