The secret that experienced creators share is that they rarely start from scratch — they work from proven structures. A good script template hands you the skeleton: where the hook goes, where the value lands, where the call to action sits.

## How to use this template pack

1. Download the full pack using the button below and choose the template that fits your video idea.
2. Replace every [BRACKETED PLACEHOLDER] with your own topic, points, and wording.
3. Read the script out loud once — anywhere you stumble, rewrite the line in simpler words.
4. Mark your B-roll, graphics, and on-screen text cues before you film, not during.
5. Film in sections and edit to the template's timing; cut anything that does not serve the next beat.

The templates are deliberately plain-spoken. Write the way you talk, not the way you write essays — contractions, short sentences, and direct address ("you") all keep viewers engaged. If a section feels too long when you read it aloud, it is too long on camera. Trust the structure: thousands of successful videos follow these exact beats, and the format works because it matches how attention actually behaves.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to follow a script template word for word?**
No. Use the structure — the order of the sections and the job each one does — and write the actual lines in your own voice. The template keeps you on track; your personality keeps viewers watching.

**Which template should a beginner start with?**
The talk-to-camera template is the easiest starting point because it mirrors a natural conversation: hook the viewer, explain the topic, deliver the points, and close with one clear call to action.

**How long should my script be for a 10-minute video?**
Most speakers deliver about 130 to 150 words per minute on camera. A 10-minute video therefore needs roughly 1,300 to 1,500 words of script, plus pauses for B-roll and demonstrations. For Shorts and Reels, aim for 75 to 150 words total.
