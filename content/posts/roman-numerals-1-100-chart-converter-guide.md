## How Roman numerals work: the 7 symbols

The entire system is built from just seven letters:

| Symbol | Value |
|---|---|
| I | 1 |
| V | 5 |
| X | 10 |
| L | 50 |
| C | 100 |
| D | 500 |
| M | 1,000 |

For 1–100 you only need the first five: **I, V, X, L, C**. Write symbols largest to smallest and add: VIII = 8, XXVII = 27.

## The subtractive notation trick

<!-- ADSENSE: in-article ad slot -->

Here's the rule that unlocks everything: **a smaller numeral placed before a larger one is subtracted**. This keeps numbers compact — instead of writing 40 as XXXX, you write XL (50 − 10 = 40).

There are only six subtractive pairs to learn:

| Pair | Value | How to remember |
|---|---|---|
| IV | 4 | 1 before 5 |
| IX | 9 | 1 before 10 |
| XL | 40 | 10 before 50 |
| XC | 90 | 10 before 100 |
| CD | 400 | 100 before 500 |
| CM | 900 | 100 before 1,000 |

For 1–100 you only need the first four: **IV, IX, XL, XC**. Three sub-rules keep the system consistent:

1. **Only subtract powers of ten** (I, X, C) — never V or L. So 45 is XLV, never VL.
2. **Subtract only from the next one or two larger values** — I goes before V or X only; X goes before L or C only.
3. **Never repeat a symbol more than three times** — that's exactly why 4 is IV instead of IIII.

## Worked examples: building numbers step by step

**Example 1: 44 → XLIV**
Break 44 into 40 + 4. Forty is XL (10 before 50). Four is IV (1 before 5). Combine: **XLIV**.

**Example 2: 99 → XCIX**
Break 99 into 90 + 9. Ninety is XC (10 before 100). Nine is IX (1 before 10). Combine: **XCIX**.

**Example 3: 67 → LXVII**
Break 67 into 50 + 10 + 7. Fifty is L, ten is X, seven is VII. Combine: **LXVII**.

**Example 4: Reading backwards — MMXXVI = 2026.**
Beyond 100, the same logic applies: 1,000 + 1,000 + 10 + 10 + 5 + 1.

The general method: take the largest chunk you can, write it down, and repeat with the remainder.

## Where you'll actually meet Roman numerals

- **Clock faces** — traditionally IIII instead of IV for 4 (a centuries-old clockmaking convention).
- **Movie sequels and game titles** — Rocky II, Super Bowl LX, Final Fantasy VII.
- **Book chapters and outlines** — Chapter XII, Appendix IV.
- **Monarchs and popes** — Elizabeth II, Henry VIII, John Paul II.
- **Film copyright dates** — older movies hide the year in the end credits (MCMXCIV = 1994).
- **Tattoos and wedding dates** — always double-check with a converter before the ink.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do you write 44 in Roman numerals?**
44 is XLIV: XL (40, from 50−10) plus IV (4, from 5−1). Break the number into tens and ones, convert each part, and join them.

**What are the 7 Roman numeral symbols?**
I (1), V (5), X (10), L (50), C (100), D (500), M (1,000). For 1–100 you only need I, V, X, L, and C.

**Why is 4 written as IV instead of IIII?**
Subtractive notation (IV = 5−1) is shorter than IIII and became the standard. IIII still survives on some traditional clock faces.

**How do I convert Roman numerals back to numbers?**
Add each symbol's value left to right, except when a smaller value precedes a larger one — then subtract. So XLIV = (50−10) + (5−1) = 44.
