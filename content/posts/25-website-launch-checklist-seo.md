Launching a website without an SEO checklist is how good sites ship invisible: the design is done, the copy is live, and nobody can find it because the sitemap was never submitted or every page shares the same title. This checklist covers twenty-five checks across content, technical setup, and measurement — the essentials to verify before launch day, and the first-week tasks that catch what launch day missed. It suits new sites, redesigns, and anyone handed a site five minutes before it goes public.

## What's inside this resource

The checks are ordered the way a launch actually happens: content first (titles, descriptions, headings), then technical (sitemap, robots, speed, mobile), then measurement (analytics, Search Console, indexing). Tick them in order and you avoid the classic launch-week scramble of discovering, too late, that the staging site was indexed instead of the real one.

## The resource (sample)

1. Every page has a unique, descriptive title under 60 characters.
2. Every page has a meta description under 160 characters that invites a click.
3. One H1 per page, matching the page's main topic.
4. Images have descriptive alt text and compressed file sizes.
5. Internal links connect related pages with descriptive anchor text.
6. A sitemap.xml exists and is submitted to Search Console.
7. Robots.txt allows the pages you want indexed and blocks the rest.
8. The staging or development site is blocked from search engines.
9. Pages load in under three seconds on a mobile connection.
10. The site is fully usable on a phone, with readable text and tappable buttons.

## How to use this resource

1. Work through the list in order, from content to technical to measurement.
2. Fix anything marked “before launch” first; “first week” items can follow within seven days.
3. Re-run the speed and mobile checks on a real phone, not just a desktop emulator.
4. After launch, check Search Console daily for the first week for crawl errors.
5. Keep the completed checklist with the project — it is the record of what was verified.

A resource is only useful when it gets used — pick one item today, use it as written, and adapt it from experience rather than from theory.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is this checklist enough for a large site?**

It covers the essentials. Large sites should add checks for hreflang, faceted navigation, and crawl budget on top of these twenty-five.

**What is the single most common launch mistake?**

Indexing the staging site, or blocking the live site with robots.txt or a noindex tag left over from development. Check both on launch day.

**How long should the checks take?**

For a small site, one focused afternoon. Most checks are quick verifications, not rebuilds.
