Budgeting advice usually fails at the same point: it tells you to track everything but never tells you what "good" looks like. The 50/30/20 rule fixes that with three buckets — 50% of after-tax income for needs, 30% for wants, 20% for savings and debt payoff. One glance tells you whether your spending is healthy or drifting. This worksheet turns the rule into action: a fill-in template with auto-calculated targets, a worked example on a $3,000 income, a clear needs-vs-wants classifier, and a month-end scorecard. First-time budgeters, couples aligning on money, and anyone whose savings never seem to grow can print it and finish their first budget in fifteen minutes.

## What's inside this worksheet

The template starts with your monthly after-tax income and calculates your three targets instantly. Then come the three fill-in sections — Needs, Wants, and Savings — each with the common line items pre-listed so you don't forget anything, plus an actual-vs-target line that shows your variance at a glance. ## How to use this worksheet

1. Write down your monthly after-tax income and calculate the three targets: half for needs, 30% for wants, 20% for savings.
2. Fill in your actual spending per line item honestly — pull the numbers from your bank app, not your memory.
3. Compare each bucket's total to its target and circle the biggest variance; that's your project for the month.
4. Move the 20% savings on payday, before spending anything — "pay yourself first" is what makes the rule work.
5. Fill in the month scorecard in under ten minutes at month-end, and write down exactly one change for next month.

The power of this worksheet is repetition, not perfection. Nobody nails the percentages in month one; the win is seeing the numbers clearly and improving one category at a time.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the 50/30/20 budget rule?**
It's a simple budgeting framework: 50% of after-tax income goes to needs, 30% to wants, and 20% to savings and debt payoff. It was popularized by Senator Elizabeth Warren and is one of the most widely used budgeting methods in the world.

**What if my rent alone is more than 50% of my income?**
That's common in expensive cities. Shrink the wants category first and protect the 20% savings if at all possible. If needs still exceed 60%, the math is telling you the housing cost — not the budget — is the problem to solve.

**Does the 50/30/20 rule work on irregular income?**
Yes, with one tweak: budget on last month's actual income rather than this month's estimate. In high-earning months the extra flows to savings; in lean months the percentages still hold because they're relative.
