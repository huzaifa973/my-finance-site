Search engines cannot see your shopfront, your menu board, or your opening-hours sign — they only see your code. Schema markup is how you hand them that information in a structured form they can turn into rich results: star ratings, opening hours, prices, and event dates shown right on the search page. These fifty examples cover the schema types local businesses actually need, from the basic LocalBusiness block to reviews, menus, services, and job postings. Each example names the type and the key fields to include, so you can adapt it to your own business in minutes. They are written for small business owners, marketers, and web developers who want local search visibility without hiring an SEO agency. Treat this list as a working reference: keep it open while you audit your own site, tick off the types you already have, and add the missing ones one page at a time. Even the core ten, done well, put a small business ahead of most local competitors.

## What's inside this resource

The examples move from the essential to the advanced. The first ten give you the core LocalBusiness markup every local site should have. The next groups add the trust signals search engines love — ratings, reviews, and frequently asked questions — then the business-specific types for restaurants, stores, clinics, and service firms, and finally the advanced patterns for events, jobs, and multi-location businesses. Use one type per page where it fits, and keep the information identical to what visitors see on the page itself: mismatched details are worse than no markup at all.

## How to use this resource

1. Start with the LocalBusiness block on your home and contact pages, filling in every field you can verify.
2. Add one supporting type per page — reviews on testimonials, menus on restaurant pages, events on event pages.
3. Keep the marked-up facts identical to the visible page content and to your map and directory listings.
4. Test each page with a rich-results testing tool before publishing, and fix any errors it reports.
5. Revisit the markup when hours, prices, or services change, so search results never show stale details.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a developer to add schema markup?

Not necessarily. These examples are plain JSON-LD blocks you paste into a page's head section. A developer helps for large sites, but a small business can add the core LocalBusiness block by hand.

### Will schema markup guarantee rich results?

No — it makes your pages eligible, but search engines choose when to show rich results. Accurate, complete markup plus genuine reviews gives you the best chance.

### Can I use several schema types on one page?

Yes, and you should. A restaurant page can carry Restaurant, menu, aggregateRating, and FAQPage markup together, as long as each block describes real page content.
