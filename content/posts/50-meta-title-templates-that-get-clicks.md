## How to use this resource

1. Pick the group that matches your page's intent: how-to for tutorials, list for roundups, comparison for buyers.
2. Fill every bracket with specifics — real numbers, real audiences, real outcomes. '7 budgeting tips' beats '[number] tips' every time.
3. Put your main keyword in the first half of the title so it is visible even if the end is cut off on mobile.
4. Count the characters and trim to under 60 so the full title shows in search results.
5. After publishing, check Search Console after two to four weeks: if a page gets impressions but few clicks, rewrite its title with a different template from the pack.

Start with your highest-impression, lowest-click pages — that is where a better title pays off fastest.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long should a meta title be?**

Aim for under 60 characters. Longer titles get cut off with an ellipsis in search results, which hides your ending and can weaken the click. Every template here is designed to fit once you fill the brackets concisely.

**Should the keyword always come first?**

Not always, but it helps. Put the keyword near the front when it reads naturally, and never force it at the cost of clarity. A title people understand beats a title that is merely optimized.

**Can I reuse the same template on many pages?**

Yes — that is what templates are for. Two pages using the same formula with different topics and numbers produce completely different titles. Uniqueness comes from what you put in the brackets.
