Screen recording used to mean downloading sketchy software and hoping it did not install a toolbar. Today every major device records its screen out of the box, and browser-based recorders cover everything else. Whether you are making a tutorial, recording a meeting, or capturing a bug to show support, here is how to do it free on Windows, Mac, and Chromebook — plus the no-install option that works anywhere.

## Screen record on Windows: Xbox Game Bar

Windows has a built-in recorder hiding inside the Xbox Game Bar. Despite the name, it records any app, not just games.

**Step-by-step:**

1. Open whatever you want to record (a browser tab, an app, a presentation).
2. Press **Windows + G** to open the Game Bar overlay.
3. Click the **Capture** widget, then the red **record button** — or just press **Windows + Alt + R** to start recording immediately.
4. A small recording bar appears with a timer and stop button.
5. Click **Stop** (or press Windows + Alt + R again) when you are done. The video saves to **Videos > Captures**.

**To record with your microphone:** click the microphone icon in the Game Bar capture widget before recording so your narration is captured too.

**Limitations:** Game Bar cannot record the Windows desktop itself or File Explorer — it only records app windows. If you need full-desktop recording or a webcam overlay, skip to the browser method below.

## Screen record on Mac: the screenshot toolbar

Macs have had a built-in screen recorder since macOS Mojave, and it is genuinely good.

**Step-by-step:**

1. Press **Shift + Command + 5** to open the screenshot toolbar.
2. Choose **Record Entire Screen** or **Record Selected Portion** (drag to select an area).
3. Click **Options** to pick your save location, microphone, and whether to show mouse clicks.
4. Click **Record**.
5. Stop by clicking the **stop button** in the menu bar. The recording saves to your Desktop by default (or wherever you chose in Options).

**Pro tip:** under Options, enable "Show Mouse Clicks" when recording tutorials — it adds a visual circle around each click so viewers can follow along.

## Screen record on Chromebook

ChromeOS has a screen recorder built into the system — no extension needed.

**Step-by-step:**

1. Press **Ctrl + Shift + the Overview key** (the rectangle-with-lines key above the 6), or open **Quick Settings > Screen capture**.
2. Switch from screenshot mode to **Screen record**.
3. Choose full screen, partial screen, or a single window.
4. Click anywhere to start (or click Record). A 3-second countdown begins.
5. Click the **stop button** in the shelf (bottom-right) when done. Your recording saves to **Downloads**.

**Note:** Chromebook recordings save as WebM files, which play fine in browsers and most players. If you need MP4 for a specific platform, convert afterward with a free video converter.

## The no-install option: a free online screen recorder

If you cannot install software (school laptop, work computer, library machine), or you want extras like a webcam picture-in-picture overlay, use a browser-based screen recorder like [DollarWise's screen recorder](/tools/screen-recorder/).

**Step-by-step:**

1. Open the tool in your browser and click **Start recording**.
2. Your browser asks what to share: an entire screen, an application window, or a single tab. Pick one.
3. Optionally enable your webcam and microphone for a picture-in-picture tutorial.
4. Click **Share**, then **record**. Present whatever you need to show.
5. Click **Stop sharing** (or the tool's stop button) when finished.
6. Preview and download the recording — usually as a WebM video.

**Why this method wins for tutorials:** tab recording keeps your bookmarks and other tabs private (only the selected tab is captured), the webcam overlay adds a human face to tutorials, and there is nothing to install or update. Your recording never uploads anywhere — it is captured and saved locally in your browser.

**Browser requirements:** this works in Chrome, Edge, and other Chromium browsers, plus Firefox with slightly fewer options. Safari's support for screen capture in web pages is still limited, so Mac and iPhone users are better off with the built-in tools above.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I screen record on Windows for free?

Press Windows + G to open the Xbox Game Bar, click the record button (or press Windows + Alt + R), and Windows saves your recording to the Videos > Captures folder. For anything beyond gaming, a free online screen recorder in your browser is more flexible.

### How do I screen record on a Mac?

Press Shift + Command + 5 to open the screenshot toolbar, choose the record-entire-screen or record-selected-portion option, and click Record. Stop from the menu bar; the video saves to your Desktop by default.

### Can I screen record on a Chromebook?

Yes — press Ctrl + Shift + the Overview key (or open Quick Settings > Screen capture), choose Screen record, and select full screen, partial, or window. Recordings save to your Downloads folder.

### Do I need to install software to record my screen?

No. Every major platform has a built-in recorder, and free online screen recorders work entirely in your browser with nothing to install — ideal for school or work computers where you cannot install apps.
