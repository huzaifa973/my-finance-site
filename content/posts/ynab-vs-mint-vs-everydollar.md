Three names dominate every "best budgeting app" conversation: YNAB, Mint, and EveryDollar. But Mint shut down, YNAB costs real money, and EveryDollar has a free tier with a catch. Here's the honest 2026 comparison so you pick the right one instead of the popular one.

## Quick verdict

- **Best overall for serious budgeters:** YNAB — the method actually changes behavior, and the price pays for itself if you use it.
- **Best free option:** EveryDollar (free tier) or Credit Karma — depending on whether you want zero-based budgeting or automatic tracking.
- **Best for former Mint users:** Credit Karma for continuity, YNAB or Monarch if you want an upgrade.

If you only read one section, read the pricing breakdown below — cost is where these three differ most.

## What happened to Mint? (important context)

Intuit shut down Mint and moved users to **Credit Karma**. If you're searching "Mint" in 2026, what you actually get is Credit Karma's money management features: free credit scores, transaction tracking, and basic budgeting. It works, it's free, but it's thinner than Mint was — no custom categories depth, weaker goal tracking, and the upsell to Credit Karma's financial products is constant. Former Mint power users mostly scattered to YNAB, EveryDollar, and Monarch.

## YNAB: the behavior-changer

**Price:** ~$14.99/month or ~$99/year (34-day free trial).

YNAB (You Need A Budget) isn't really an app — it's a method with software attached. The core rules: give every dollar a job, embrace your true expenses, roll with the punches, age your money. Users who commit routinely report saving hundreds in the first months, not because the app is magic, but because zero-based budgeting forces you to confront every dollar.

**Strengths:** bank sync, excellent goal tracking, the best educational content of any budgeting app, and a method that genuinely works for debt payoff.

**Weaknesses:** the price stings, the learning curve is real (expect 2–3 weeks before it clicks), and it's overkill if you just want to passively watch spending.

**Verdict:** worth every penny if you'll actually use it weekly. A waste if it'll sit unopened.

## EveryDollar: the Ramsey simplicity pick

**Price:** free tier (manual entry) or ~$79.99/year with Ramsey+ (bank sync + extras).

<!-- AFFILIATE: relevant offer -->

EveryDollar is Dave Ramsey's app, and it shows: dead-simple zero-based budgeting, baby steps integration, no clutter. The free version does the core job — plan your month, track spending manually, watch every dollar get assigned.

**Strengths:** the free tier is genuinely useful (unlike many "free" apps that are demos), the interface is the simplest of the three, and it pairs perfectly with the Ramsey debt-payoff method.

**Weaknesses:** bank sync costs money, the app is basic compared to YNAB (weaker reporting, fewer features), and you're buying into the Ramsey ecosystem whether you want it or not.

**Verdict:** the best free starting point for hands-on budgeters. Upgrade only if manual entry becomes a chore you avoid.

## Credit Karma: the free passive tracker

**Price:** free (ad-supported).

Credit Karma absorbed Mint's user base and offers what most casual users actually used Mint for: automatic transaction syncing, spending categories, and free credit scores in one dashboard. Setup takes 10 minutes and then it runs itself.

**Strengths:** completely free with bank sync, credit score monitoring built in, zero effort after setup.

**Weaknesses:** budgeting is shallow (tracking, not planning), the constant product recommendations get old, and data-privacy-conscious users should know the business model is selling financial products to you.

**Verdict:** fine for "where did my money go?" awareness. Not a tool for "where will my money go?" planning.

## Which should you actually choose?

**Choose YNAB if:** you're in debt or serious about saving, you'll engage weekly, and $99/year is less than what you'd save (it almost always is).

**Choose EveryDollar free if:** you're a beginner who wants to learn zero-based budgeting without paying, or you follow the Ramsey baby steps.

**Choose Credit Karma if:** you want free, automatic, zero-effort tracking and you're honest that you won't maintain a hands-on budget.

## Switching tips

- **From Mint:** export your Mint data before it's gone (if you haven't already), then pick based on the guide above — don't default to Credit Karma just because Intuit pointed you there.
- **To YNAB:** start at the beginning of a month, connect only checking/savings first (add credit cards in month two), and watch the official 20-minute setup videos. The trial is 34 days for a reason.
- **To EveryDollar:** the free tier takes 15 minutes to set up. Build next month's budget this weekend and see if manual entry sticks before paying for sync.

### FAQ

**Q:** Is YNAB worth the price in 2026?
A: YNAB is worth it if you actively budget every dollar and stick with the method — users report saving far more than the subscription costs. It's not worth it if you won't log in weekly, since the value comes from the habit, not the software.

**Q:** What happened to Mint?
A: Mint was shut down by Intuit, and users were migrated to Credit Karma. Credit Karma now covers Mint's old ground (free budgeting, credit scores), but it lacks Mint's depth — which is why many former Mint users moved to YNAB, EveryDollar, or Monarch.

**Q:** Is EveryDollar free version enough?
A: The free version covers manual-entry zero-based budgeting, which is the core of the Ramsey method. You only need the paid version if you want automatic bank syncing. Manual entry is slower but makes you more aware of spending.

**Q:** Which budgeting app is best for beginners?
A: EveryDollar's free tier is the gentlest start — simple, zero-based, no cost. YNAB is best if you're serious about changing money habits and don't mind paying. Credit Karma (ex-Mint users) is best if you want free automatic tracking with minimal effort.
