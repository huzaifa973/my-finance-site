New York has one of the more complex state tax systems in the US: nine brackets, a separate New York City tax, and different thresholds depending on filing status. It looks intimidating, but the underlying idea is simple — and once you see the brackets laid out plainly, estimating your tax takes minutes.

Here's the 2026 picture in plain English. (Note: this is an educational overview, not tax advice — for your specific situation, check the NY Department of Taxation and Finance or a tax professional.)

## How New York's Graduated Tax Works

New York uses **marginal brackets**, like the federal system. Your income is sliced into layers, and each layer is taxed at its own rate. Only the dollars *inside* a bracket get that bracket's rate.

**Example:** if you're single with $60,000 of taxable income, you don't pay one flat rate on $60,000. The first $8,500 is taxed at 4%, the next chunk at 4.5%, and so on. Your **effective rate** — total tax divided by income — ends up well below your top ("marginal") bracket rate.

This also means the common fear is a myth: **moving into a higher bracket never makes you poorer.** Only the income above the threshold is taxed at the higher rate.

## 2026 New York State Brackets (Single Filers)

| Taxable income | Rate |
|---|---|
| Up to $8,500 | 4.00% |
| $8,501 – $11,700 | 4.50% |
| $11,701 – $13,900 | 5.25% |
| $13,901 – $80,650 | 5.50% |
| $80,651 – $215,400 | 6.00% |
| $215,401 – $1,077,550 | 6.85% |
| $1,077,551 – $5,000,000 | 9.65% |
| $5,000,001 – $25,000,000 | 10.30% |
| Over $25,000,000 | 10.90% |

For **married filing jointly**, thresholds are roughly doubled through the middle brackets (e.g., the 5.5% bracket extends to $161,550), and **heads of household** get their own threshold table. The rates themselves are the same.

**Where most people land:** the wide 5.5% bracket ($13,901–$80,650 single) covers a huge share of earners, and the 6% bracket catches incomes up to $215,400. The 9.65%+ top brackets only touch seven-figure incomes.

## Don't Forget: NYC (and Yonkers) Add Their Own Tax

This surprises newcomers: **New York City residents pay a city income tax on top of state tax.**

| NYC taxable income (single) | NYC rate |
|---|---|
| Up to $12,000 | 3.078% |
| $12,001 – $25,000 | 3.762% |
| $25,001 – $50,000 | 3.819% |
| Over $50,000 | 3.876% |

So a single NYC resident earning $100,000 faces roughly 6% state + 3.876% city on their top dollars — a combined marginal rate near 10% before federal tax. **Yonkers** residents instead pay a city income tax surcharge equal to 16.75% of their state tax liability.

Live in Buffalo, Albany, Rochester, or anywhere else in the state? You pay **state tax only** — no city layer.

## Worked Example: Single Filer, $75,000

Let's walk through it. Assume $75,000 of New York taxable income (after the ~$8,000 standard deduction is already removed), single, living outside NYC:

## 5 Things That Trip People Up

1. **Withholding ≠ your actual tax.** Your employer withholds an estimate. The brackets determine what you truly owe — the difference is your refund or balance due.
2. **You may owe tax in two states.** Work in New York but live in New Jersey or Connecticut? You'll generally file in both, with credits preventing double taxation — but the paperwork is real.
3. **NYC tax applies where you *live*, not where you work.** Commute into Manhattan from New Jersey and you don't owe NYC tax (though you do owe NY state tax on NY-source income).
4. **The standard deduction matters.** For 2026 it's about $8,000 single / $16,050 joint — subtract it before bracketing. Itemizing (mortgage interest, SALT capped at federal limits, charitable gifts) can beat it.
5. **Estimated payments for freelancers.** No employer withholding? You likely owe quarterly estimated payments to both the IRS and New York — underpayment penalties apply to the state too.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What are the New York state income tax brackets for 2026?**
New York uses nine graduated brackets for 2026, ranging from 4% on the first $8,500 of taxable income up to 10.9% on income over $25 million (single filers). Most earners fall in the 5.5%–6.85% middle brackets.

**Do I pay New York City income tax on top of state tax?**
Yes, if you live in New York City. NYC levies its own income tax of 3.078%–3.876% on top of state tax. Yonkers residents pay a separate city surcharge instead. Living elsewhere in the state means state tax only.

**How is New York state tax calculated — is it a flat rate?**
No, it's graduated (marginal). Each bracket's rate applies only to the income within that bracket — not your whole income. Earning into a higher bracket never reduces your take-home pay; only the dollars above the threshold are taxed at the higher rate.

**What is the New York standard deduction for 2026?**
For 2026, New York's standard deduction is $8,000 for single filers and $16,050 for married couples filing jointly (amounts adjust slightly each year for inflation). You subtract it from your income before applying the bracket rates.
