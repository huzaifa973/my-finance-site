A bad scan is worse than no scan. The crooked page with gray shadows, the text you cannot select or search, the 80 MB file that bounces back from every email — everyone has received one, and everyone has quietly cursed the sender. Whether you are archiving contracts, submitting paperwork, or sharing research, a quick quality pass before you hit send is the difference between looking professional and looking sloppy. This 25-point checklist covers the four things that make or break a scanned PDF: capture quality (resolution, alignment, lighting), the text layer (OCR, searchability), file optimization (size, format), and the final pre-share review. Run through it once and your scans will be crisp, searchable, and emailable every time.

## What's inside this checklist

The checklist is organized into the four stages of a quality scan. Capture checks (8 points) cover scanner settings before and during the scan: DPI, color mode, page alignment, lighting, and what to do about double-sided originals. Text layer checks (6 points) verify OCR actually worked — selecting text, searching for a known word, and checking reading order so screen readers and copy-paste behave. Optimization checks (6 points) bring file sizes down without hurting readability: compression, page size, and when to split multi-hundred-page scans. Pre-share checks (5 points) are the final gate: file name, page count, blank pages, and a last visual skim. Office managers, freelancers, students, and anyone who handles paperwork get a repeatable routine that turns "good enough" scans into professional documents.

## How to use this checklist

1. Set your scanner defaults once: 300 DPI, grayscale for text, color only for photos — most checklist failures start at the wrong settings.
2. Scan, then immediately run OCR before doing anything else; an image-only PDF is a dead document.
3. Work through the four sections in order — capture, text layer, optimization, pre-share — checking each point.
4. Do the 100% zoom skim last: it catches the visual problems no automated check finds.
5. Save these scanner settings as a preset named "Quality Scan" so every future scan starts compliant.

The checklist takes about three minutes per document once the habit forms. That is dramatically less time than re-scanning a 40-page contract because page 17 was upside down.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I fix a bad scan without rescanning?**
Partially. You can deskew, crop, and compress an existing scan, and you can add OCR afterward. But you cannot recover detail that was never captured — a 100 DPI blurry scan stays blurry. Rescanning is faster than fighting a bad original.

**Should I scan in color or black and white?**
Grayscale for text documents (best size-to-quality ratio), pure black-and-white for simple forms and receipts, color only when color carries information — photos, highlighted annotations, stamps, or signatures in colored ink.

**What file size is reasonable per page?**
Roughly 50–150 KB per page for grayscale text at 300 DPI after compression. If your 10-page scan is 50 MB, something is wrong — usually full-color mode or 600+ DPI.
