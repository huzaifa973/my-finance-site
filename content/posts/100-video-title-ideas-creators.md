A video's title decides whether anyone presses play, and most creators spend hours on the video and minutes on the title. The difference between a good title and a great one is rarely the topic — it is the angle: a promise, a challenge, a mistake to avoid, or a time frame that makes the video feel doable. These one hundred title ideas give YouTubers, TikTok creators, and course makers proven title formats across twenty niches, from cooking and fitness to freelancing and finance. Each idea uses a format that earns clicks honestly, by matching the title to real value inside the video — no misleading claims, no bait-and-switch.

## What's inside this resource

The ideas span twenty creator niches, with five title formats for each: the 30-day challenge, the beginner's guide, the mistakes roundup, the ten-minutes-a-day promise, and the wish-I-knew reflection. Seeing the same five formats across twenty topics shows how portable they are — pick the format that fits your video's actual content, then fill in your niche. The formats work because each one answers a viewer's unspoken question: how long will this take, is it for beginners, what should I avoid, or what did you learn that I can skip ahead to.

## How to use this resource

1. Choose the niche closest to your channel, then pick the title format that honestly matches what your video delivers.
2. Replace the topic placeholder with your specific angle, and keep the title under sixty characters so it is not cut off in search.
3. Put the strongest hook word near the front — a number, a time frame, or a clear benefit.
4. Check the title against your video: if a viewer would feel misled after watching, rewrite it before publishing.
5. Track which formats earn the best click-through rate on your channel, and lean into your top two formats for future videos.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should a YouTube title be?

Under sixty characters is the safe range — longer titles get cut off in search results and on mobile. Put the most important words first so the hook survives even if the end is trimmed.

### Is clickbait ever okay for titles?

Curiosity is fine; deception is not. A title that promises something the video delivers is good packaging. A title that promises something the video never delivers earns a quick exit, and viewers rarely come back.

### Should titles include keywords?

Yes, naturally. Including the topic phrase helps YouTube match your video to searches, but write for the human first — a title stuffed with keywords that no one wants to click helps no one.
