Internal links are the most underused SEO lever on most blogs. Every link you add from one of your pages to another passes authority, tells Google which pages matter most, and gives readers a reason to stay on your site longer. But the anchor text — the clickable words — is where most people slip up. "Click here" and "this article" waste the opportunity, while keyword-rich, natural anchors help the target page rank for exactly the terms you want. This pack gives you 50 ready-to-use anchor text examples organized by the six styles Google responds to best. Replace the bracketed topics with your own pages, and every post you write becomes a small SEO campaign for the rest of your site.

## What's inside this pack

The 50 examples cover every anchor style you need in a healthy internal linking strategy. Exact-match anchors (10) use the target page's primary keyword verbatim — the strongest signal you can send. Partial-match anchors (10) blend the keyword with natural phrasing so links read smoothly in context. Descriptive anchors (10) tell readers exactly what they will find on the other side. Question anchors (8) mirror the "People also ask" phrasing searchers actually type. Branded and generic anchors (7) keep your profile natural. Contextual action anchors (5) invite clicks with benefit-led language. SEO consultants, bloggers, and content marketers get a swipe file that turns every new article into ranking fuel for older posts, and every older post into a discovery path for new readers.

## How to use this pack

1. Pick 3 to 5 older pages you want to boost and note each page's primary keyword.
2. When writing a new post, scan the pack for anchors matching that keyword or its close variants.
3. Place links where they solve a reader's next question — inside a sentence, never dumped at the bottom.
4. Vary the style: mix exact-match, partial-match, and descriptive anchors across articles so no two links look identical.
5. Revisit your top 20 posts once a quarter and add fresh internal links to newly published pages.

The examples are patterns, not prescriptions. Swap in your own page titles and keyword phrases, and match the anchor's tone to your article — a playful blog can use playful anchors, a corporate guide should keep them factual.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do internal links help a new page rank faster?**
Yes. A new page with no inbound links can take months to be discovered and ranked, but linking to it from an established, crawled page puts it in Google's path immediately. It is the fastest free SEO win available to site owners.

**Should internal links open in a new tab?**
Generally no. Internal links should open in the same tab to keep the reader's journey continuous and preserve the back-button flow. Reserve new tabs for external links.

**Can I add too many internal links?**
You can dilute a page when every other sentence is a link. Stay near the 3-to-10-per-1,000-words range, and ask of each link: would a reader be glad this is here? If not, cut it.
