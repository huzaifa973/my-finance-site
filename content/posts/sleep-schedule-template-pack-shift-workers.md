Shift work breaks sleep in predictable ways — and a schedule built for a 9-to-5 life makes it worse. This pack gives ten ready-made sleep templates, one per shift pattern: nights, early mornings, swing shifts, rotating rosters, and 12-hour days and nights. Each template sets a sleep window, a wind-down start time, and the light rules that actually shift your body clock. It is written for nurses, factory workers, drivers, security staff, and anyone whose roster ignores the sun.

## What's inside this resource

Every template follows the same three rules. First, protect the sleep window: same hours every workday, even when it is tempting to flip back on days off. Second, control light: bright light early in the shift to stay alert, dark glasses on the way home so daylight does not tell your brain to wake up. Third, keep a 60-minute buffer before the shift for waking, eating, and getting ready — rushing out the door after waking is how shifts start badly. Adjust the template's times to your actual roster and keep them consistent for at least a week before judging them.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I flip back to a day schedule on days off?

For short breaks, no — flipping twice a week means you never adapt to either schedule. Keep a compromise sleep window on days off, shifted only a few hours from your work window.

### Do dark glasses on the way home really help?

Yes. Morning daylight is the strongest signal telling your brain to stay awake. Blocking it after a night shift is one of the most effective, cheapest tools shift workers have.

### How long does it take to adjust to nights?

Most people need several days of consistent timing. If your roster rotates faster than that, focus on the light rules and sleep protection rather than full adaptation.
