California has the most progressive — and steepest — state income tax in the country. Nine brackets carry rates from 1% all the way to 12.3%, and earners above $1 million face an extra 1% surcharge on top of that. If you are new to the state, those headline numbers can look intimidating. But marginal rates mean nobody pays the top rate on their whole income — the high rates only touch the top slice. This guide explains California's illustrative 2026 brackets, how the standard deduction shrinks your taxable income, a worked marginal-rate example, and how to estimate your tax in minutes with our free calculator.

## How California's progressive brackets work

Like the federal system, California taxes income in tiers. Your first chunk of taxable income is taxed at 1%, the next chunk at 2%, and so on up to the top bracket. The rate that applies to your *last* dollar of income — your marginal rate — is higher than your *effective* rate, which is your total tax divided by your total income. Confusing the two is the most common tax misunderstanding: a raise that pushes you into a higher bracket never leaves you with less take-home pay, because only the income above the threshold is taxed at the higher rate.

California's brackets are also relatively wide compared with some states, and they include a 1% mental-health-services surcharge on income over $1 million — which is why the state's true top marginal rate is 13.3%, the highest in the nation.

## The standard deduction: shrinking your taxable income

Bracket rates apply to *taxable* income, not to your full salary. Before any rate touches your earnings, you subtract deductions:

- **Standard deduction.** California offers a standard deduction for filers who do not itemize (illustrative: around $5,400 for single filers and about $10,800 for joint filers). If your itemized deductions — mortgage interest, state taxes paid, charitable gifts — exceed the standard amount, itemizing saves more.
- **Personal exemptions.** California also provides personal exemption credits (illustrative: roughly $154 per dependent), which reduce your final tax directly rather than your taxable income.

The practical takeaway: a single filer earning $80,000 does not have $80,000 of taxable income. After the illustrative standard deduction alone, the bracket math starts at roughly $74,600 — and every dollar of deduction keeps a dollar out of the highest bracket you would otherwise touch.

## Marginal rates in action: a worked example

Take a single filer with $75,000 of taxable income (after deductions), using the illustrative brackets above:

- First $10,412 at 1% = $104.12
- $10,413 – $24,684 ($14,272) at 2% = $285.44
- $24,685 – $38,959 ($14,275) at 4% = $571.00
- $38,960 – $54,081 ($15,122) at 6% = $907.32
- $54,082 – $68,350 ($14,269) at 8% = $1,141.52
- $68,351 – $75,000 ($6,650) at 9.3% = $618.45

Total estimated tax: **$3,627.85** — an effective rate of about 4.8%, far below the 9.3% marginal rate. That gap between marginal and effective rates is exactly why headline bracket numbers mislead. The surcharge only enters the picture at very high incomes: an additional 1% on income above $1 million, bringing the illustrative top marginal rate to 13.3%.

## Steps to estimate your 2026 California tax

1. **Start with your California adjusted gross income.** This is generally your federal AGI adjusted for California-specific differences.
2. **Subtract deductions.** Take the standard deduction or your itemized total — whichever is larger.
3. **Apply the bracket rates slice by slice.** Work up the table, multiplying each chunk of taxable income by its rate, exactly like the worked example above.
4. **Subtract exemption credits.** Reduce the resulting tax by your personal and dependent exemption credits.
5. **Compare with withholding.** Check your pay stubs: if your employer withheld more than the estimate, the difference comes back as a refund; if less, you will owe the balance in April.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is California's top income tax rate?
Illustratively 12.3%, plus a 1% surcharge on income over $1 million, for a top marginal rate of 13.3% — the highest of any state. It applies only to income inside that top bracket.

### Will a raise push me into a higher bracket and cost me money?
No. Because rates are marginal, only the income above the new threshold is taxed at the higher rate. A raise always increases your take-home pay.

### Should I itemize deductions in California?
Itemize if your total itemized deductions exceed the standard deduction; otherwise take the standard amount. Run both numbers — the difference can be worth hundreds of dollars.

### Are the bracket numbers in this article official?
No. They are illustrative examples showing how the calculation works. Always verify current brackets with the California Franchise Tax Board or a tax professional.
