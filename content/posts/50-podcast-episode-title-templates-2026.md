A podcast title has to work twice: once in a feed, where it competes with a hundred other episodes, and once in search, where it must match what a listener types. These fifty fresh templates cover the episode types podcasts actually make in 2026 — interviews, stories, how-tos, debates, explainers, and solo episodes — each with a structure that carries both curiosity and a keyword. Swap in your guest, topic, or question and the title is ready. They are written for podcasters at every level, from first-episode beginners to shows refreshing their format for a new year. The templates work because they balance two forces every title needs: curiosity that earns the click, and clarity that earns the right listener. A vague title attracts nobody, while a misleading one attracts the wrong audience and loses them. Use these structures to promise exactly what the episode delivers, and listeners will come back because they trust your titles.

## What's inside this resource

The templates are grouped by job, not just by type. Story templates promise a journey, how-to templates promise a result, debate templates promise tension, and explainer templates promise clarity. That mix matters because a feed full of identical title shapes looks monotonous even when the episodes are different. Rotate shapes across episodes: pair a story title one week with a how-to the next, and save the boldest claims for episodes that truly deliver on them. Bracketed placeholders show exactly what to swap in, so no template ever ships with a placeholder still in it.

## How to use this resource

1. Pick the template shape that matches the episode's real promise — do not use a debate title for a gentle explainer.
2. Fill every bracket with specific words: a real guest name, a real number, a real outcome.
3. Read the finished title aloud and check it fits on a phone screen without cutting off the key words.
4. Put the topic keyword near the front of the title so search and scanning both catch it fast.
5. Keep a running list of your best-performing titles and reuse their shapes for future episodes.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should episode titles include the episode number?

For series, yes — listeners follow sequences. For standalone episodes, the number adds nothing and wastes front-loaded space.

### How specific should a title be?

Very. 'Marketing tips' loses to 'How a bakery doubled bookings with one email' because specificity promises a concrete payoff.

### Do titles affect podcast search?

Yes. Players and search engines match title words to queries, so the topic keyword in the title matters more than cleverness.
