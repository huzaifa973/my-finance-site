Conclusions are where good writing most often falls apart. After a strong middle, writers run out of energy and end with a shrug — a repeated introduction, a sudden new argument, or a vague 'in conclusion' that adds nothing. Yet the ending is what readers remember: it is the last impression, the moment the main point either lands or fades. A good conclusion does three things quickly: it restates the point, names the benefit, and points to one clear next step.
This pack gives you 50 conclusion paragraph templates across five styles: summaries, calls to action, reflections, encouragement, and forward-looking endings. Each template has a [topic] slot and works for essays, blog posts, reports, and speeches. Ten samples are below; the free download holds all 50.

## What's inside this template pack

Summary conclusions restate the main point in fresh words, which helps readers who skimmed the middle still leave with the message. Call-to-action conclusions convert understanding into a next step — the right ending for how-tos, guides, and persuasive writing. Reflection conclusions step back and name what the topic reveals, which suits essays and opinion pieces.
Encouragement conclusions are for readers who need a push: they acknowledge the difficulty and insist the effort is worth it. Forward-looking conclusions point past the article to what comes next, which works well for series, projects, and plans. ## How to use this resource

1. Pick the style that matches your purpose: summary for explainers, call to action for how-tos, encouragement for hard topics.
2. Replace [topic] with your real subject and rewrite the sentence in your own voice — templates are starting points, not finished prose.
3. Restate your main point in new words. Copying the introduction sentence-for-sentence makes the ending feel lazy.
4. End with one clear next step or thought, not three. Readers remember one ending, not a list.
5. Read the last paragraph aloud. If it sounds like it could end any article, it is not specific enough yet.

A conclusion should feel like a door closing with purpose, not like the writer simply ran out of things to say.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long should a conclusion paragraph be?**

Three to five sentences is right for most pieces. Short enough to feel like an ending, long enough to restate the point and name a next step. Longer conclusions start to feel like a second article.

**Can a conclusion introduce new information?**

No — that is the most common conclusion mistake. New evidence belongs in the body. The conclusion's job is to land what is already there, not to reopen the argument.

**Should I start with 'In conclusion'?**

You can, but you usually do not need to. A strong final paragraph signals itself by restating the point and closing the loop. If the phrase feels necessary, the paragraph may not be doing its job yet.
