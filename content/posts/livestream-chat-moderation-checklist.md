A livestream's chat can make the stream — or derail it in thirty seconds. Spam floods, personal info leaks, and arguments escalate fast when nobody is prepared. This twenty-point checklist is the moderator's briefing before going live: slow mode, automod filters, the timeout ladder, private mod channels, and the post-stream debrief. It is written for streamers, community managers, and volunteer moderators on Twitch, YouTube Live, and TikTok Live.

## What's inside this resource

The checklist follows the stream's timeline: setup before going live, rules during the stream, and review after it ends. The most valuable habit is the one most teams skip — briefing moderators on the exact escalation ladder before the stream, so nobody improvises under pressure. Warn, then timeout, then ban, with personal-info posts removed immediately and without discussion. Consistency is what keeps a community fair.

1. Set chat to slow mode (10–30 seconds) before going live
2. Pin the rules message at the top of chat
3. Assign at least one moderator per 100 expected viewers
4. Enable follower-only or subscriber-only mode if spam spikes
5. Prepare a blocklist of banned words and slurs in advance
6. Turn on automod filters at a medium level to start
7. Brief moderators on the timeout ladder: warn, 10-min timeout, ban
8. Keep a private moderator channel (Discord or group chat) open
9. Decide the policy on self-promotion links before the stream
10. Have a plan for raids: welcome message and mod watch
11. Hide personal info fast — timeout anyone posting addresses or phone numbers
12. Use nightbot-style commands for FAQs (!schedule, !links, !rules)
13. Never read hateful messages aloud — delete and move on
14. Log repeat offenders and share the list with all moderators
15. Take breaks on a schedule so moderators can rotate
16. After the stream, review flagged messages and update filters
17. Thank moderators publicly at the end of the stream
18. Keep the VOD chat rules consistent with the live rules
19. Document any ban appeals process in the channel rules
20. Do a post-stream debrief: what worked, what to change next time

## Frequently asked questions

### How many moderators do I need?

A good starting point is one moderator per 100 expected viewers, with at least two so they can rotate. Fast chats need more eyes than slow ones.

### Should I read hateful messages aloud?

No. Delete them, timeout or ban the sender, and move on. Reading them aloud gives the disruption the attention it wants and can upset other viewers.

### What is slow mode?

A setting that limits how often each viewer can post — for example, one message every 30 seconds. It slows spam floods and gives moderators time to act.
