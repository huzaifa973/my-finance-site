Meetings without agendas are where time goes to die: everyone arrives unsure what the meeting is for, and leaves unsure what was decided. These fifty agenda templates fix the most common meetings — standups, planning, reviews, one-on-ones, and retrospectives — each with a timed structure you can copy into the invite. A good agenda is short, names a decision, and gives every item a timebox; these do all three.

## What's inside this resource

The templates follow one rule: every item ends with either a decision, an owner, or a next step. Items without one get cut. Timings are suggestions — shrink them for small teams, stretch them for big ones — but keep the structure, because structure is what turns a chat into a meeting.

## How to use this resource

1. Send the agenda with the invite, not five minutes before the meeting.
2. Assign a timekeeper — even a casual one — or timings are fiction.
3. End every meeting by reading the decisions and owners aloud.
4. If an item has no decision, owner, or next step, cut it from the agenda.
5. Review recurring agendas monthly and delete meetings that no longer earn their time.

A resource is only useful when it gets used — pick one item today, use it as written, and adapt it from experience rather than from theory.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long should an agenda be?**

Half a page. If the agenda needs its own meeting to explain, it is too long — cut items until only decisions and owners remain.

**Who should write the agenda?**

The person who called the meeting. If nobody owns the agenda, nobody owns the meeting, and it drifts.

**What if the meeting runs over?**

Park the remaining items visibly and schedule a follow-up. Extending by “just five minutes” repeatedly is how calendars collapse.
