A voiceover script is not just words on a page — it is a performance plan. The same sentence read flat, warm, or urgent becomes three different messages, and without direction notes, even a talented voice actor has to guess what you meant. Beginners often hand over bare text and hope for the best, then pay for re-records when the tone is wrong. A good template prevents that by building direction, pacing, and pauses into the script itself.
This pack gives you five ready-to-use voiceover script templates — commercial, explainer, narration, e-learning, and promo — each with direction notes, pause marks, and placeholders you replace with your own words. It also includes a recording checklist covering warm-ups, room setup, microphone distance, and takes. It suits creators, course makers, advertisers, and anyone hiring or recording voice work. ## How to use this resource

1. Pick the template that matches your project: commercial for ads, explainer for videos, narration for stories, e-learning for courses, promo for trailers.
2. Replace every placeholder with your own words, then read the whole script aloud twice before recording.
3. Keep the direction notes in the script you hand to the voice actor — they are instructions, not clutter.
4. Mark pauses where listeners need a beat: after a key claim, before a call to action, between sections.
5. Record each section twice and keep the better take; then listen back on headphones before approving.

A script with direction is a kindness to everyone involved — the actor, the editor, and the listener.

## Frequently asked questions

**What do the [direction] notes in a script mean?**

They tell the voice actor how to read each line: pace, tone, and emphasis. Without them, the actor guesses, and guesses lead to re-records. Keep direction notes short and specific.

**How long should a voiceover script be?**

Roughly 150 words per minute for conversational reads, slower for narration. A 30-second commercial is about 75 words; a 60-second explainer is about 150.

**Do I need professional equipment to record voiceover?**

Not to start. A quiet room with soft surfaces and a decent USB microphone produces clean results. Room noise and echo matter far more than the microphone brand.
