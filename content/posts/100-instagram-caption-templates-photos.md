## How to use this resource

1. Pick the group that matches the photo, not the group you used last time.
2. Replace every bracket with a real detail: a place name, a dish, a person's name. Generic captions get generic reactions.
3. Read the caption aloud once. If it sounds like a stranger wrote it, rewrite it in your own words.
4. End with a question or invitation when you want comments — captions that ask something get more replies.
5. Save your favorite five from the pack and reuse their style, not their exact words, for future posts.

The best caption is the one that sounds like you — use these as starting points, then make them yours.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long should an Instagram caption be?**

It depends on the photo. For strong standalone images, one or two lines work best. For storytelling posts, longer captions that explain the moment tend to earn more saves and comments.

**Should I use hashtags in captions?**

A few relevant hashtags help discovery, but they work better in a comment or at the end of the caption than interrupting the first lines, which are what followers actually read.

**Can I reuse the same caption on different photos?**

Occasionally, but not often. Repeated captions look automated and followers notice. Change at least the bracketed details — and ideally the wording — for each post.
