An emergency fund is the least exciting and most useful money you will ever save. It is what turns a broken car, a medical bill, or a lost job from a crisis into a problem you can handle. But a goal that lives only in your head is easy to forget, which is why this printable tracker exists: one page to set your target, record every deposit, and watch your safety net grow week by week. It is built for anyone starting an emergency fund from scratch, rebuilding one after using it, or teaching a family member how saving actually works. This is general information, not personalized financial advice — the right target depends on your own expenses and situation.

## What's inside this resource

The template starts with four setup lines: your target amount, your monthly contribution, the account where the fund lives, and your start date. After that comes a 52-week log with a row for every week of the year — what you planned to save, what you actually saved, and your running total. Fill it in by hand on paper, or copy it into a notebook or spreadsheet if you prefer digital tracking. The weekly rhythm matters more than the amount: small, regular deposits beat grand plans that never start, and seeing the running total climb is its own motivation.

## How to use this resource

1. Add up one month of essential expenses — housing, food, transport, bills, and insurance — and multiply by three to six for your target.
2. Write your target, monthly contribution, account, and start date in the four setup lines at the top of the template.
3. Set up an automatic transfer on payday into a separate savings account, so saving happens before spending can claim the money.
4. Each week, fill in one row: what you planned, what you actually saved, and your new running total.
5. If you use the fund for a real emergency, note the withdrawal in the next row and restart contributions as soon as you can — using it is what it is for.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much should I keep in an emergency fund?

A common guideline is three to six months of essential expenses. Three months suits a stable two-income household; six months fits single incomes, freelancers, or less secure jobs. Start with $500 to $1,000 if the full target feels out of reach — a small fund still beats none.

### Where should I keep my emergency fund?

In a separate savings account you can reach within a few days, but not in your everyday spending account where it will quietly disappear. Easy access matters because emergencies do not wait, but a little separation keeps the money safe from impulse spending.

### What counts as an emergency?

Unexpected, necessary expenses: a car repair you need to get to work, a medical bill, a broken appliance, or a job loss. A sale, a holiday, or a new phone does not count — keeping that line clear is what makes the fund work when it matters.
