A product photo caption should do one thing: connect the thing in the photo to a reason to buy it. “New product!” is not a reason; “made to last, small-batch crafted” starts to be one. These fifty captions pair ten everyday products with five benefit lines each, giving you a pattern you can reuse for your own catalogue: name the product, name the benefit, stop there. Short captions outperform essays under photos — the photo already did the showing.

## What's inside this resource

The benefit lines are deliberately plain: durability, craft, sustainability, everyday usefulness, and gift-worthiness. Those five cover most buying reasons, and they keep the caption honest — a claim you can stand behind beats a superlative you cannot. Swap your product into the pattern and keep the benefit that is actually true.

## How to use this resource

1. Lead with the benefit, not the product name — the photo already shows the product.
2. Keep captions to one or two lines under a photo; details belong on the product page.
3. Use the benefit that is actually true for that product — honesty converts better than hype.
4. End with one action: shop the link, see more colours, or save for later.
5. Test captions in batches and keep the structures that earn saves and clicks.

A resource is only useful when it gets used — pick one item today, use it as written, and adapt it from experience rather than from theory.

## Frequently asked questions

**Should captions include the price?**

Usually not under a photo — price belongs on the product page, where the buyer is ready for it. A caption's job is desire, not checkout.

**How long should a product caption be?**

One or two lines. Shoppers scroll fast; a paragraph under a photo is skipped, while a short benefit line is actually read.

**Do emojis help product captions?**

Sparingly. One relevant emoji can draw the eye; a string of them makes a shop look unserious. Match your brand, not the trend.
