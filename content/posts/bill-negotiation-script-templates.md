Your monthly bills are more negotiable than you think. Cable, internet, phone, and insurance companies all run on a simple math: keeping you is cheaper than finding a new customer. That gives you leverage — and retention departments exist precisely to use discounts to keep you. Yet most people never call, leaving hundreds of dollars a year on the table out of pure habit.

This pack gives you six word-for-word scripts you can read on the call: cable/internet, mobile phone, car insurance, medical bills, credit card fees, and a landlord rent discussion. Each script includes the opening line, the key phrases that unlock better offers, responses to common pushback, and a closing that locks in the deal. Fill in the bracketed details before you dial, and the whole call takes 10-20 minutes.

## What's inside this script pack

Six complete scripts, one per bill type. The cable/internet script targets the retention department with competitor pricing as leverage. The mobile phone script works through plan optimization and loyalty discounts. The car insurance script uses competing quotes to trigger a rate review. The medical bill script negotiates itemized charges and payment plans. The credit card script gets annual fees waived or interest rates reduced. The rent script helps you propose a renewal rate to your landlord. Every script is polite but firm — the tone that gets results without burning bridges.

**Script 1 — Cable and internet**

```
CABLE / INTERNET NEGOTIATION SCRIPT

Before you call: check competitor prices in your area and note your
current bill, plan speed, and how long you've been a customer.

YOU: "Hi, I'd like to speak with the retention department about
lowering my bill, please."

(Transferred to retention)

YOU: "Hi, my name is [YOUR NAME], account number [ACCOUNT NUMBER].
I've been a customer for [X] years, and my bill is currently
$[AMOUNT] per month for [PLAN/SPEED]. [COMPETITOR] is offering
[THEIR OFFER] in my area, and I'm considering switching. Before
I do, I wanted to see what you can offer to keep me."

IF THEY OFFER A SMALL DISCOUNT:
YOU: "I appreciate that. Is that the best available rate for a
loyal customer? I'd really need to get closer to $[TARGET] to stay."

IF THEY SAY NO:
YOU: "I understand. Can you tell me the process for cancelling?
I'd like to compare that against the competitor offer."

(A cancellation mention often unlocks the real offers.)

CLOSING:
YOU: "That works — $[NEW AMOUNT] per month for [PLAN]. Can you
confirm that in writing by email, and confirm there's no contract
extension or installation fee attached?"

Write down: rep name, date, new rate, confirmation number.
```

**Script 2 — Mobile phone plan**

```
MOBILE PHONE NEGOTIATION SCRIPT

Before you call: check your last 3 bills for actual data usage —
most people pay for data they never use.

YOU: "Hi, I'm [YOUR NAME] on account [ACCOUNT NUMBER]. I'd like
to review my plan — I think I'm paying for more than I use, and
I've seen cheaper plans from [COMPETITOR]."

KEY QUESTIONS TO ASK:
1. "What plans do you have under $[TARGET] per month?"
2. "Are there any loyalty discounts for customers of [X] years?"
3. "Do you offer an autopay or paperless billing discount?"
4. "Can you waive the activation/upgrade fee on my account?"

IF THEY OFFER A PLAN CHANGE:
YOU: "Before I switch — will this change my coverage, data
priority, or hotspot allowance? And is there a contract term?"

CLOSING:
YOU: "Great, let's do it. Please confirm the new monthly total
including all taxes and fees, and email me the summary."

Write down: rep name, date, new plan name, monthly total, confirmation number.
```

**Script 3 — Car insurance**

```
CAR INSURANCE NEGOTIATION SCRIPT

Before you call: get 2-3 competing quotes (online, 15 minutes).
Have your current premium, coverage levels, and renewal date ready.

YOU: "Hi, I'm [YOUR NAME], policy number [POLICY NUMBER]. My
renewal is coming up at $[AMOUNT], and I've received quotes of
$[COMPETITOR QUOTE] for the same coverage from [COMPETITOR].
I'd prefer to stay — can you review my rate?"

KEY QUESTIONS TO ASK:
1. "Am I getting all available discounts — safe driver, bundling,
   low mileage, autopay, paperless?"
2. "Has my rate been re-evaluated since [LIFE CHANGE: new car,
   moved, married, improved credit]?"
3. "What would my premium be if I raised my deductible to $[X]?"
4. "Do you offer a telematics/usage-based program discount?"

IF THEY WON'T MATCH:
YOU: "I understand. What's the cancellation process and
effective date? I need to decide before my renewal on [DATE]."

CLOSING:
YOU: "I'll accept $[NEW PREMIUM]. Please email the updated
declarations page showing the coverage and new premium."

Write down: rep name, date, new premium, confirmation number.
```

**Script 4 — Medical bills**

```
MEDICAL BILL NEGOTIATION SCRIPT

Before you call: request an ITEMIZED bill (not a summary) and
check each line for duplicates or services you didn't receive.

YOU: "Hi, I'm [YOUR NAME], account [ACCOUNT NUMBER]. I received
a bill for $[AMOUNT] dated [DATE], and I'd like to discuss the
charges before I pay."

STEP 1 — VERIFY:
YOU: "Can you send me an itemized statement? I want to confirm
each charge is correct."

STEP 2 — NEGOTIATE:
YOU: "I see [QUESTIONABLE CHARGE]. Can you explain what this
covers? ... I'd like to dispute that line."
YOU: "I'm able to pay $[OFFER — start at 40-60% of the bill]
today if we can settle the account. Is that something you
can approve?"
YOU: "What financial assistance or hardship programs do you
offer? I'd like to apply."

STEP 3 — PAYMENT PLAN (if needed):
YOU: "I can't pay this in full. What 0%-interest payment plans
do you offer? I can do $[AMOUNT] per month."

CLOSING:
YOU: "Please send written confirmation of the agreed amount
and that the account will show as paid in full / settled."

Write down: rep name, date, agreed amount, confirmation number.
Never agree to a plan with interest if a 0% option exists.
```

**Script 5 — Credit card fees and APR**

```
CREDIT CARD FEE / APR NEGOTIATION SCRIPT

Before you call: note the fee amount, your payment history, and
how long you've held the card. Call the number on the back.

FOR AN ANNUAL FEE:
YOU: "Hi, I'm [YOUR NAME]. I've been a cardholder for [X] years
with a perfect payment history, and I see a $[FEE] annual fee
posted. I'd like that waived — otherwise I'll need to close
the account."

IF THEY OFFER A PARTIAL WAIVER OR CREDITS:
YOU: "I appreciate it. Can you waive it in full, or convert me
to the no-annual-fee version of this card?"

FOR A HIGH APR:
YOU: "My current APR is [X]%. I've received offers at [Y]% from
other issuers. Can you lower my rate to keep my business?"

IF THEY SAY NO TO EVERYTHING:
YOU: "Understood. Please note on the account that I requested
a fee waiver and rate reduction on [DATE] and was declined —
I'll be reviewing my options."

CLOSING:
YOU: "Thank you. Please confirm the waiver/new rate by email
and tell me when it takes effect."

Write down: rep name, date, outcome, confirmation number.
```

**Script 6 — Rent renewal**

```
RENT RENEWAL NEGOTIATION SCRIPT

Before the conversation: research comparable rents nearby and
note your record as a tenant (on-time payments, no complaints,
unit condition).

YOU (in person, by phone, or by email):
"Hi [LANDLORD/MANAGER NAME], my lease at [UNIT/ADDRESS] renews
on [DATE], and I see the proposed rent is $[NEW] — an increase
of $[DIFFERENCE]. I'd love to stay; I've been here [X] years,
always paid on time, and kept the unit in great shape. Comparable
units nearby are renting for $[COMPARABLE]. Would you consider
[YOUR COUNTEROFFER — e.g., keeping the current rent / a smaller
increase to $X] for a [12/24]-month renewal?"

VALUE YOU CAN OFFER:
- Sign a longer lease (18-24 months) for rate stability
- Handle minor maintenance yourself
- Pay a few months upfront if cash flow allows
- Renew early so they skip listing the unit

IF THEY WON'T BUDGE ON RENT:
YOU: "I understand. Would you consider keeping the rent flat if
I sign for 24 months? Or including [parking/storage/laundry]
at no extra charge?"

CLOSING:
YOU: "Great — let's put the agreed rent of $[AMOUNT] and the
lease term in writing before I sign."

Get every agreement in the signed lease, not just verbally.
```

## How to use this script pack

1. Pick one bill and do 10 minutes of prep: current rate, competitor prices or quotes, and your account details.
2. Fill in every bracket in the script so you're not searching for numbers mid-call.
3. Call and ask for the retention or loyalty department first — front-line reps have the least authority to discount.
4. Stay polite and unhurried; mention competitors and cancellation calmly, never as a threat.
5. Write down the rep's name, the new rate, and the confirmation number, and get email confirmation before hanging up.

Timing matters more than most people realize. Call near the end of the month when reps are hitting retention targets, and always call before a contract auto-renews rather than after. One more tip: if a call goes badly, hang up and call back — you'll get a different rep, and outcomes genuinely vary. People who negotiate one bill usually negotiate them all within a week, and the total savings often top $500 a year.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does negotiating bills actually work?**
Yes — especially for cable, internet, phone, and insurance. Retention departments have real authority to offer discounts, and companies would rather cut your rate than lose you. Most people who call save 10-25% with a single 15-minute call.

**Will negotiating hurt my credit score or service?**
No. Asking for a lower rate is a normal customer request and has no effect on your credit. Your service stays exactly the same — you're only changing what you pay for it.

**What if the representative says no?**
Ask to speak with the retention or loyalty department — they have more authority than front-line reps. If the answer is still no, politely say you'll shop around and call back later; sometimes the best offers appear once you start the cancellation process.
