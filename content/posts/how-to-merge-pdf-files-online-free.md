Job applications want one file. Your accountant wants one file. The admissions portal accepts one file. Merging PDFs is a constant chore — and the usual "free" tools come with a catch: they upload your documents to someone else's server.

Here's how to merge PDFs quickly, plus the private method for sensitive documents.

## When you need to merge PDFs

- **Job applications:** resume + cover letter + certificates in one download.
- **Expenses and taxes:** a month of receipts combined for your accountant.
- **University applications:** transcripts, statements, and references as one file.
- **Scanned documents:** phone scanner apps often save one PDF per page.
- **Contracts:** the agreement plus its addenda, kept together.

## Other free methods

- **Mac Preview:** open one PDF → View → Thumbnails → drag additional PDFs into the thumbnail sidebar → File → Export as PDF. Built in, no internet needed.
- **Windows:** no built-in merger — a browser tool (above) is the fastest option; the Microsoft Store's "PDF Merger & Splitter" app works offline too.
- **Adobe Acrobat online:** free tier merges PDFs but requires an Adobe account and uploads your files.
- **Google Drive:** upload PDFs, open with a Drive PDF-merger add-on — convenient but uploads everything.

## The privacy problem with most "free" mergers

Read the fine print: most free PDF tools upload your files to their servers, process them there, and keep copies for some retention period. That's fine for a public brochure — but terrible for:

- Employment contracts and offer letters
- Tax documents and bank statements
- Medical records
- Scanned IDs and passports

If the document contains anything you wouldn't email to a stranger, use a tool that processes locally in your browser. That's exactly why DollarWise builds privacy-first tools: your files never leave your browser, period.

## Troubleshooting

- **"Merge failed" error:** the file may be password-protected or corrupted. Open it in your PDF reader — if it asks for a password, remove the protection first.
- **Pages in the wrong order:** reorder before merging by dragging cards in the file list.
- **Huge output file:** merging doesn't compress; if the result is too big, the individual source PDFs were already large. Compress scanned pages before merging.
- **Scanned images won't merge:** make sure each file is a real PDF, not a renamed image file.

### Turning scanned photos into one PDF

Phone scanner apps often save each page as a separate PDF — or worse, as JPG images. To build one document from JPG scans: first convert each scan to PDF (most phones do this in the share sheet: Print → pinch out on the preview → Share as PDF), then merge the resulting PDFs with the tool above. For the cleanest result, scan in good light, keep pages aligned, and merge in page-number order. If your scans came out as images inside one PDF already, no merging is needed — that file is complete.
