Australia's 2026–27 income year (1 July 2026 – 30 June 2027) brings a small but welcome tax cut: the second bracket rate drops from 16% to **15%**. Combined with the $18,200 tax-free threshold and the 2% Medicare levy, here is exactly how much tax you will pay at every income level.

## 2026–27 resident tax brackets

These are **marginal** rates for Australian residents for tax purposes who claim the tax-free threshold:

| Taxable income | Tax rate |
|---|---|
| $0 – $18,200 | 0% (tax-free) |
| $18,201 – $45,000 | 15% |
| $45,001 – $135,000 | 30% |
| $135,001 – $190,000 | 37% |
| Over $190,000 | 45% |

Or in ATO formula form: 15c per $1 over $18,200; then $4,020 + 30c per $1 over $45,000; then $31,020 + 37c per $1 over $135,000; then $51,370 + 45c per $1 over $190,000.

**The 2% Medicare levy** applies on top of income tax for most residents. Think of your true marginal rate as the bracket rate + 2 — so 32% in the middle bracket, 47% at the top.

## Worked examples

<!-- ADSENSE: in-article ad slot -->

**On $60,000 taxable income:** Tax-free on the first $18,200. 15% on $18,201–$45,000 = $4,020. 30% on $45,001–$60,000 = $4,500. Income tax = **$8,520**; Medicare = $1,200; **total $9,720** (~16.2% effective).

**On $100,000:** $4,020 + 30% × ($100,000 − $45,000) = $4,020 + $16,500 = **$20,520** income tax; Medicare = $2,000; **total $22,520** (~22.5% effective). Your marginal rate is 30% (32% with Medicare) — but your *average* rate is far lower. That is the power of marginal brackets.

**On $150,000:** $31,020 + 37% × ($150,000 − $135,000) = $31,020 + $5,550 = **$36,570**; Medicare = $3,000; **total $39,570** (~26.4% effective).

## The Low Income Tax Offset (LITO)

LITO automatically reduces tax for lower earners — you do not claim it, the ATO applies it:

- **Maximum $700** for taxable incomes up to $37,500.
- Phases out at 5c per dollar from $37,500 to $45,000, then 1.5c per dollar to $66,667.
- Gone entirely above $66,667.

Combined with the tax-free threshold, LITO means the effective tax-free point stretches a bit beyond $18,200 for eligible earners.

## Medicare levy details

- **Standard rate: 2%** of taxable income for residents.
- **Low-income exemption:** no levy below $28,011 (singles), phasing in up to $35,013.
- **Medicare Levy Surcharge (MLS):** an extra 1–1.5% if you earn above the surcharge thresholds ($113,000 singles / $226,000 families for 2026–27) and do not hold private hospital cover. This is the one that surprises people — check your cover before June 30.

## What changed vs 2025–26

The headline change: the **$18,201–$45,000 bracket fell from 16% to 15%**, worth up to **$268/year** for anyone earning above $45,000. Thresholds are unchanged (Australia does not auto-index brackets). And it is already legislated that the same bracket drops again to **14% from 1 July 2027** — another ~$268 saving.

## Non-residents: different rules

Non-residents get **no tax-free threshold** — 30c per dollar from the first dollar earned in Australia, with 45% above $190,000. If you are on a temporary visa, check your residency status carefully; it makes an enormous difference.

## Frequently asked questions

**What are Australia's income tax brackets for 2026–27?**
Residents pay 0% to $18,200, 15% to $45,000, 30% to $135,000, 37% to $190,000, and 45% above $190,000 — plus the 2% Medicare levy. The 2026–27 year runs 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027.

**What is the Medicare levy?**
An extra 2% of taxable income paid by most Australian residents to fund the public health system. It sits on top of income tax — so a 30% marginal rate is really 32% including Medicare.

**What changed in Australia's tax rates for 2026–27?**
The second bracket rate fell from 16% to 15% (income $18,201–$45,000), saving up to $268 a year. A further legislated cut to 14% applies from 2027–28.

**Do I pay the top rate on all my income?**
No. Australia uses marginal rates — each rate applies only to the slice of income inside its bracket. On $100,000 of taxable income, your effective rate is about 22.5% including Medicare, not 30%.
