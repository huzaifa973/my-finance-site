Your typing speed quietly taxes or subsidizes everything you do on a computer. At 30 WPM, writing a one-page email takes 15 minutes of pure typing; at 70 WPM, it takes six. Over a career, that difference is thousands of hours. The good news: typing speed is one of the most trainable skills there is, and measuring it takes 60 seconds. Here is how to test yours free, what the numbers mean, and how to actually get faster.

## What WPM means (and the benchmarks)

**WPM = words per minute**, where one "word" is standardized as **5 characters including spaces**. So 300 characters typed correctly in 60 seconds = 60 WPM. There are two flavors:

- **Gross WPM:** everything you typed, errors included
- **Net WPM:** gross minus an error penalty — this is the number that counts

Benchmarks for context:

| Level | WPM | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Beginner | 20–30 | Hunt-and-peck typists |
| Average adult | ~40 | Most casual computer users |
| Competent office | 50–60 | Typical admin/data-entry expectation |
| Fast | 70–80 | Writers, developers, support staff |
| Professional | 90–110 | Transcriptionists, court reporters in training |
| Elite | 120+ | Competitive typists |

**Accuracy is the hidden multiplier.** 60 WPM at 98% accuracy is far more useful than 90 WPM at 85% — every error costs backspace time plus the mental break in flow. Employers hiring for typing roles typically demand 95%+ accuracy minimums. If your accuracy is under 92%, speed training is premature: fix accuracy first.

## Why you are slower than you could be (the usual culprits)

- **Looking at the keyboard.** Every glance breaks rhythm and forces re-orientation. This alone costs most people 10–20 WPM.
- **Wrong finger placement.** If your index fingers do all the work, your hands travel too far. Touch typing assigns each finger a zone.
- **Tension.** Hunched shoulders and pounding keys cause fatigue within minutes. Speed comes from relaxation, not force.
- **Inconsistent practice.** One long session a month does nothing; ten minutes daily rewires muscle memory in weeks.

## How to actually get faster: the training plan

**Week 1–2: accuracy lockdown.** Slow down to whatever speed gives you 98%+ accuracy. This feels painfully slow — that is the point. Your fingers are learning correct paths.

**Week 3–4: touch typing.** If you do not touch-type, learn home-row positioning (fingers on ASDF JKL;, thumbs on space). Free lessons exist everywhere; the first week is the only hard part.

**Week 5+: deliberate drills.** Do not just retype random passages. Drill:
- Your 10 most-missed keys and bigrams (th, er, on, an)
- Common words at speed: *the, and, that, have, with*
- Numbers and symbols if your work needs them — most people never practice these and they crater real-world speed

**The 10-minute daily rule.** Short daily sessions beat weekend marathons because typing is motor learning — sleep consolidates it. Retest weekly on the [typing speed test](/tools/typing-speed-test/) and track net WPM plus accuracy. Expect 5–10 WPM per month of consistent practice; plateaus around 60–70 WPM are normal and break with targeted weak-key drills.

## Ergonomics: the speed you are leaving on the table

Technique is not just fingers. Your setup caps your speed more than most people realize:

- **Keyboard height:** wrists should float straight, not bend up or down. A keyboard tray or a low desk beats stacking books under a laptop.
- **Chair and posture:** feet flat, back supported, screen at eye level. Slouching compresses your shoulders and slows your hands within minutes.
- **Key switches matter:** mushy laptop keyboards and cheap membranes slow you down and increase errors. You do not need an expensive mechanical board — but a keyboard with crisp, consistent key travel helps.
- **Take micro-breaks.** Hands are small muscles; 50 minutes of typing followed by a 5–10 minute break sustains higher speeds across a workday than grinding through fatigue.

Ignore ergonomics and you do not just type slower — you risk repetitive strain injuries that stop you typing entirely. Speed built on tension is borrowed time.

### What is a good typing speed in WPM?

40 WPM is the average for most adults. 60+ WPM is solid for office work, 80+ is genuinely fast, and 100+ puts you in professional territory. Accuracy matters more than raw speed — 60 WPM at 98% accuracy beats 90 WPM at 85%.

### How is WPM actually calculated?

One "word" is standardized as 5 characters including spaces. Type 300 correct characters in a minute and that's 60 WPM. Most tests also report raw vs net WPM — net subtracts a penalty for errors, which is the number that matters.

### How can I increase my typing speed?

Fix accuracy first (slow down until errors vanish), learn proper touch-typing finger placement if you haven't, practice in short daily sessions rather than marathons, and drill your weakest keys and common words deliberately.
