Everyone quotes "60 characters" for title tags. It is a decent rule of thumb — and it is wrong in the way that matters. Google does not count characters; it measures **pixels**. A title full of wide letters like W and M hits the limit far sooner than one full of narrow i's and l's. In 2026, with Google's result fonts and layouts settled, the real limit is about **580–600 pixels** on desktop.

This guide explains how pixel-width truncation works, why Google rewrites some titles entirely, and how to write titles that survive intact.

## Pixels, not characters: how truncation really works

Google renders your title in its results font and cuts it off with an ellipsis (…) when it exceeds the available width — roughly 600px on desktop, less on mobile. That means:

- **"WWW MMM WWW"** (wide characters) truncates much earlier than **"iii lll iii"** (narrow characters), even at similar character counts.
- **50–60 characters** is a safe planning range, but it is an approximation, not a rule.
- **ALL CAPS titles** burn through pixels fast — another reason to use normal capitalization.
- **Pipes and dashes** as separators are pixel-cheap; long brand suffixes ("| Best Running Shoes Reviews Guide 2026") are pixel-expensive.

The only reliable check is a tool that renders the title at Google's actual font metrics. Character counters will mislead you at the margins — exactly where it matters.

## How to write truncation-proof titles (5 rules)

1. **Front-load the important words.** Put the primary keyword and the page's promise in the first 40 characters. If truncation happens, the cut part should be expendable.
2. **Keep brand names short or at the end.** "Primary Keyword — Site Name" beats "Site Name | Primary Keyword | Category" every time.
3. **Check with a pixel-width checker** — paste every important title into [DollarWise's free Title Pixel Checker](/tools/title-pixel-checker/) before publishing. It takes ten seconds.
4. **One idea per title.** Titles trying to rank for three keywords end up truncated *and* diluted. Pick the page's single strongest intent.
5. **Match the H1 and content.** Google increasingly rewrites titles that do not reflect the page. A title that mirrors your H1 and opening paragraph survives far more often.

## Why Google rewrites titles (and how to stop it)

Since 2021, Google has openly rewritten title tags it deems poor — and it still does. Common triggers:

- **Keyword stuffing** — "Cheap Shoes, Discount Shoes, Buy Shoes Online" gets rewritten to something sane.
- **Boilerplate** — "Home", "Untitled", or the same title on fifty pages.
- **Mismatch** — the title promises one thing, the page delivers another.
- **Overly long titles** — Google sometimes just picks a better excerpt itself.

The fix is boring but effective: write a clear, specific, honest title under ~600px that describes the page's actual content. Pages with good titles get rewritten far less.

## Mobile vs desktop: the two limits

Desktop gives you ~600px. Mobile gives you less — roughly **one to two lines** depending on the device, often effectively 50-ish characters before wrapping or truncation.

Since most search traffic is mobile, optimize for the smaller screen: if your title reads well truncated at mobile width, it will be fine on desktop too. Test both if your checker supports it; if not, aim for the conservative end (~550px).

Also watch **SERP features**: featured snippets, site links, and AI overviews can display your title differently or not at all. A good title still matters — it is your headline in every context Google chooses to show it.

## Title formulas that survive truncation

Memorize three patterns and you will rarely write a bad title:

**Keyword + benefit:** "PDF Compressor: Shrink Files to Any Size Free" — the keyword first (survives truncation), the benefit second.

**Question + year:** "How Long Should a Meta Description Be? (2026 Guide)" — matches query phrasing, signals freshness.

**Number + promise:** "7 Free Video Converters That Run in Your Browser" — numbers earn clicks; the promise filters for the right visitor.

And three anti-patterns to delete on sight:

- **Keyword, keyword, keywords** — "Cheap Hotels, Budget Hotels, Discount Hotels" gets rewritten and looks spammy doing it.
- **Brand-first on content pages** — "Acme Corp | How to Tie a Tie" wastes the highest-visibility pixels on the brand. (Brand-first is fine on the homepage.)
- **Cute but cryptic** — "Unleash Your Potential" tells Google and users nothing. Save the poetry for the H1; make the title do the SEO work.

Write the title for the searcher scanning eight blue links in two seconds. Clarity beats cleverness, every time.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many pixels wide can a title tag be before Google truncates it?
Roughly 580–600 pixels on desktop results. Because characters have different widths, that translates to about 50–60 characters — but a pixel-width checker is the only accurate way to verify.

### Why does Google rewrite my title tags?
Google rewrites titles it considers unhelpful — keyword-stuffed, boilerplate, or mismatched to the page's content. Write clear, descriptive titles that match the page's main topic and rewrites drop sharply.

### Does a truncated title hurt SEO rankings?
Truncation itself does not directly lower rankings, but a cut-off title gets fewer clicks, and lower click-through rate means less traffic from the position you earned. It is a CTR problem, not a ranking penalty.
