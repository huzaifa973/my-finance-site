Great guests do not appear by luck — they are invited, well. The difference between a dream guest saying yes and your email sitting unopened usually comes down to three things: proof you did your homework, a specific topic (not "come chat"), and a scheduling step that takes thirty seconds. These six email templates cover the whole guest lifecycle, from first pitch to post-recording thank-you.

They are built for podcasters of any size, from a show with twelve listeners to an established program. Every template uses [bracketed] placeholders so you can fill in names, shows, and details in under two minutes. The first three templates are shown below with copy buttons; all six are in the free download.

## What the 6 email templates cover

The **initial pitch** is a short, specific cold email — why this guest, what topic, how long, and how to book. The **follow-up** is a two-line bump sent five to seven days later, because most bookings come from the nudge. The **confirmation/thank-you** locks in the date, time zone, recording link, and what to expect, so guests show up prepared. The **prep email with questions** sends your question list a day or two before recording, which dramatically improves answer quality. The **post-recording thank-you** goes out the same day you record, with a timeline for when the episode airs. And the **reschedule request** keeps the relationship warm when life gets in the way.

## How to use these guest outreach templates

1. Personalize every placeholder before sending — especially the "why you" line. Guests can smell a mass email instantly.
2. Send the initial pitch, then set a reminder to send the follow-up five to seven days later if there is no reply.
3. When a guest says yes, send the confirmation email immediately with the date, time zone, and recording link.
4. Send the prep email with questions one to two days before recording — never the morning of.
5. Send the post-recording thank-you the same day, and actually follow through when the episode airs.

Guest outreach is a numbers game with a quality filter: send enough pitches, but make every single one feel personal. These templates give you the structure; your homework on each guest gives you the yes. Download all six below and keep them in your email drafts.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I get big guests on a small podcast?**
Make the ask specific and low-effort. Big guests say yes when the email shows you know their work, names the exact topic you want them for, and makes scheduling take thirty seconds. Target people with something to promote — a book, a launch, a cause — and offer to share the episode with your audience plus clips they can reuse.

**How long should a guest outreach email be?**
Under 150 words. Busy people skim. Lead with why them (one specific detail proving you did your homework), state the topic and format in one line each, name the time commitment, and close with a single scheduling link or two proposed times. Save the details for the confirmation email after they say yes.

**When should I follow up if a guest doesn't reply?**
Once, five to seven days after the first email. Keep it to two or three lines — a polite bump, not a rewrite. Most guest bookings come from the follow-up, because your first email arrived during a busy week. If there is still no reply after that, move on; a third email rarely converts and can annoy.
