## How to use this cheat sheet

1. Print it or keep it open on a second screen during your next three editing sessions.
2. Learn five shortcuts at a time — start with transport and cutting, the ones you will use most.
3. Force yourself to use a new shortcut instead of reaching for the mouse, even when it feels slower at first.
4. After a week, add the next group: timeline navigation, then selection, then power shortcuts.
5. Remap only the keys that genuinely fight your habits — most defaults are defaults for a reason.

Muscle memory takes about two weeks of consistent use. Editors who commit to the cheat sheet for one full project rarely go back to clicking menus.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is it worth learning shortcuts if I only edit occasionally?**
Yes — even casual editors benefit from the top ten. Play/pause, cut, undo, zoom, and export cover 80% of what occasional editors do, and they take an afternoon to learn.

**Why do some shortcuts use different keys on Mac?**
On macOS, Ctrl becomes Cmd for most shortcuts (Cmd+Z, Cmd+S, Cmd+M). The letter keys (C, M, V, Space, J-K-L) stay the same. The download notes the Mac equivalents where they differ.
