A good outline does three jobs at once: it locks in the search intent before you write a word, it plans your heading structure around the questions people actually ask, and it tells you where examples, visuals, and FAQs go so nothing gets forgotten. ## How to use these templates

1. Identify your article type first: tutorial, list, comparison, review, or pillar — then pick the matching template.
2. Fill in the keyword, audience, and year slots; write a one-line intent note ("the searcher wants a beginner tutorial, not a product pitch").
3. Draft each H2 as a question your target reader would actually ask, then plan one example or visual per section.
4. Add 3 to 5 FAQ questions from real "People also ask" results before you start writing the body.
5. Write to the outline without skipping sections — thin sections get flagged in the draft, not discovered after publishing.

Templates remove the blank-page paralysis, but the quality still comes from your research. An outline built on real search results and firsthand testing beats a beautifully structured outline built on guesses, every time.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do these outlines work for AI-assisted writing?**
They work even better. A filled outline is the best possible input for any writing assistant: it constrains the topic, enforces structure, and prevents the rambling that comes from open-ended prompts. Outline first, draft second, no matter who or what does the drafting.

**How long should each section be?**
There is no universal answer, but plan roughly equal depth across H2s — if one section is 50 words and the next is 500, the outline is telling you to either expand the thin one or merge it into a sibling.
