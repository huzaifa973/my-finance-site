Going live for the first time is exciting — and a little nerve-wracking. Between the camera, the microphone, the streaming software, the internet connection, and the platform settings, there are dozens of small things that can go wrong, and they tend to go wrong in front of an audience. The good news is that almost every streaming disaster is preventable. The streamers who look effortless on day one are simply the ones who prepared with a checklist.

This free checklist gives you 25 concrete steps across five phases: planning your stream, setting up your gear and software, running a proper test, going live with confidence, and wrapping up afterward. It is written for absolute beginners streaming to YouTube, Twitch, Facebook, or any other platform. You do not need expensive equipment — most of these steps are about settings, preparation, and habits, not hardware.

## What's inside this checklist

The checklist walks you through the full journey of a first stream. The planning phase helps you pick your topic, platform, and schedule, and set one clear goal for the stream. The setup phase covers the essentials — camera, microphone, lighting, streaming software, and internet — with practical placement and settings advice. The test phase is a short private rehearsal that catches audio glitches, echo, and connection drops before they embarrass you. The go-live phase covers your opening minute, chat interaction, and what to do when something breaks mid-stream. Finally, the wrap-up phase shows you how to end cleanly, save your recording, and learn from the replay.

Every item is a single actionable step you can check off, and each one has a copy button so you can paste steps into your own notes.

## How to use this checklist

1. Download the checklist below and work through Phase 1 at least a day before your stream.
2. Set up your gear and software in Phase 2, giving yourself a full hour with no rush.
3. Run the Phase 3 test stream and actually watch the replay — this step catches 90 percent of problems.
4. On stream day, arrive early, open with your hook, and keep the checklist nearby for the go-live steps.
5. After the stream, do the wrap-up steps the same day while everything is fresh.

Your first stream will not be perfect, and that is completely fine — every experienced streamer has a story about their disastrous debut. What matters is that the basics work: viewers can see you, hear you, and the stream does not drop. This checklist covers exactly those basics. Nail them, improve one thing per stream, and by your tenth broadcast you will look like you have been doing it for years.

## Frequently asked questions

**What equipment do I need to start live streaming?**
Less than you think: a computer or phone, a webcam or phone camera, a microphone (even earbuds beat a built-in laptop mic), and a stable internet connection with at least 10 Mbps upload. Upgrade your gear only after your first few streams, once you know what actually limits your quality.

**How fast does my internet need to be for streaming?**
For 1080p streaming, aim for at least 10 Mbps of stable upload speed, and use a wired ethernet connection if possible. Always run a speed test right before going live, since network conditions change through the day.

**Should I do a test stream before my first real one?**
Absolutely. Every major platform lets you stream privately or unlisted. A 10-minute test catches audio problems, lighting issues, and connection drops before any real viewers arrive — it is the highest-value step in this entire checklist.
