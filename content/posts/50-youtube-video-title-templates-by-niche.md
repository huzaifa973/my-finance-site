## How to use these templates

1. Pick the niche group that matches your channel and find the format that fits the video you just filmed.
2. Fill each [bracket] with your specific topic, number, or amount — keep fillers short so the title stays under 60 characters.
3. Front-load the clickable words: numbers, outcomes, and timeframes belong near the start, not buried at the end.
4. Match the thumbnail to the title's promise — a curiosity title with a bland thumbnail still loses the click.
5. Track click-through rate per video in YouTube Studio and reuse the templates that beat your channel average.

Templates are starting points, not finished titles. The creators who get the most from this pack customize the fillers with their niche's exact vocabulary — the pattern does the structural work, your specifics do the persuading.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long should a YouTube title be?**
Keep titles under 60 characters so they display fully in search results and suggested feeds. Front-load the most clickable words, because titles start truncating on mobile after about 40 characters.

**Do YouTube titles affect the algorithm?**
Titles strongly affect click-through rate, and CTR is one of the signals YouTube uses to decide how widely to recommend a video. A title that earns clicks from its impressions gets shown to more people — the template patterns in this pack are built around that dynamic.

**Can I reuse the same template for many videos?**
Yes, within reason. Viewers respond to familiar formats from channels they trust — "I Tested X" can become your signature series. Just vary the fillers and avoid publishing identical structures back-to-back.
