Recording an episode is only half the work — and for most creators, it is the half that reaches the fewest people. A single podcast episode or video can become a week of content: short clips for social feeds, a written article, quote graphics, a checklist, and a newsletter issue. Yet most creators publish once, post one link, and move on, leaving most of the value of their best work untouched.
This checklist fixes that with a 28-step repurposing workflow, from picking the right episode and marking its best moments to publishing clips on a staggered schedule and measuring what worked. It suits podcasters, YouTubers, and anyone who records regularly and wants more reach without recording more hours. Ten steps are below; the free download holds the complete checklist.

## What's inside this checklist

The planning section starts where repurposing should start: with your best-performing episode, not your newest one. You watch once, mark timestamps for the strongest moments, and write down the three to five takeaways the episode genuinely delivers — those takeaways become the raw material for everything that follows.
The short-form section turns those moments into captioned vertical clips, the written section into articles, threads, and newsletters, and the visual section into quote graphics, audiograms, and infographics. The final section covers the part most creators skip: a staggered publishing schedule, links back to the full episode, and a review of which formats earned the most views, so the next episode's repurposing gets smarter.

## Podcast video repurposing checklist (free sample)

Ten steps from the full 28-step checklist. The complete checklist is in the free download.

1. Pick one strong episode or video to repurpose — start with your best-performing content.
2. Watch or listen once and mark timestamps for the best moments, quotes, and stories.
3. Write down the three to five key takeaways the episode actually delivers.
4. Cut 3 short vertical clips (30-90 seconds) for Reels, Shorts, and TikTok.
5. Add captions to every clip — most viewers watch with sound off.
6. Turn one strong quote into a square image post for social feeds.
7. Write a short thread or carousel from the episode's main points.
8. Create a blog post or show-notes page summarizing the episode with links.
9. Extract the audio as a standalone podcast episode or bonus clip.
10. Make a audiogram: a short audio clip with a waveform animation.

## How to use this resource

1. Pick your best-performing episode first — repurposing a strong episode beats repurposing a recent weak one.
2. Mark timestamps as you watch once, so clipping later is fast instead of a second full rewatch.
3. Always add captions to clips: most social viewers watch with sound off.
4. Publish clips on a staggered schedule, one per day, with a link back to the full episode.
5. After two weeks, check which formats performed best and build your next repurposing round around those.

Repurposing is a system, not a chore: once the workflow exists, one episode reliably becomes a week of content.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many pieces of content should one episode become?**

A realistic target is five to ten: three short clips, one article or newsletter, two social posts, and one visual asset. More than that usually means lower quality per piece.

**Should I repurpose every episode?**

Start with your best performers. Once the workflow is fast, extend it to new episodes as they publish, and revisit top episodes quarterly for new audiences.

**Do clips need captions?**

Yes. Most social video is watched with sound off, and captions also make clips accessible. They are the single highest-impact step in this checklist.
