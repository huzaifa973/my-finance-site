Your YouTube banner is your channel's billboard — the first thing visitors see, and the fastest signal of whether a channel is worth subscribing to. But YouTube displays one uploaded image across TVs, desktops, tablets, and phones, each cropping it differently. Get the dimensions wrong and your channel name gets sliced in half on mobile. This guide covers the exact 2026 banner dimensions, how each device crops your image, a simple resize workflow, design tips that survive every crop, and the mistakes to avoid.

## YouTube banner dimensions for 2026

Upload one image at the full recommended size, and YouTube handles the per-device cropping automatically:

| Spec | Dimensions |
|---|---|
| Recommended upload size | 2560 × 1440 px |
| Minimum accepted size | 2048 × 1152 px |
| Maximum file size | 6 MB |
| Safe area (always visible) | 1546 × 423 px, centered |
| Desktop display crop | 2560 × 423 px (center strip) |
| Mobile display crop | 1546 × 423 px (center) |
| TV display | Full 2560 × 1440 px |

The single most important number is the **safe area: 1546×423 pixels in the exact center**. Everything outside it is decoration — visible on some devices, cropped on others. Treat the safe area as your actual canvas and the rest as bleed.

## How your banner displays on TV, desktop, and mobile

<!-- ADSENSE: in-article ad slot -->

Understanding the crops explains every banner horror story:

- **TV:** shows the entire 2560×1440 image. This is the only device where edge-to-edge artwork is fully visible, so a beautiful full-bleed background pays off here.
- **Desktop:** shows a wide center strip (2560×423). Side regions appear, but top and bottom are cropped away.
- **Tablet:** shows a crop between desktop and mobile widths — roughly the center 1855×423.
- **Mobile:** shows only the center 1546×423 — identical to the safe area. This is the strictest crop and the one most viewers use.

The pattern is simple: design for the 1546×423 center, and let the surrounding pixels be an attractive background that enhances the image on bigger screens without carrying any critical information.

## Design tips: keep key content in the safe zone

- **Center everything that matters.** Channel name, tagline, upload schedule, social handles — all inside the 1546×423 safe area. Assume mobile viewers will never see a pixel outside it.
- **Use the bleed for atmosphere, not information.** Gradients, patterns, blurred photo extensions, and brand colors fill the outer regions beautifully on TV and desktop without risking a bad crop.
- **Keep text large and minimal.** Banner text is competing with thumbnails and video titles. One line — your channel name plus a short value proposition ("Weekly budget breakdowns") — beats a paragraph nobody can read on a phone.
- **Match your brand colors and fonts.** Your banner, profile picture, and thumbnails should feel like one family. Reuse two brand colors and one font across all three for instant recognition.
- **Mind the profile picture overlap.** On desktop, your circular channel icon overlaps the banner's lower-left area. Keep that corner free of text so nothing hides behind your own logo.

## 5 common YouTube banner mistakes

1. **Putting text near the edges.** The number-one banner killer. Anything outside the safe area vanishes on mobile — including, too often, the channel name itself.
2. **Uploading a tiny image.** A 800-pixel-wide image stretched to 2560 looks blurry on every screen. Always start from a high-resolution source at or above 2560×1440.
3. **Ignoring the file size limit.** YouTube rejects uploads over 6 MB. Export at sensible quality settings instead of maximum — nobody can see the difference, but the platform can see the megabytes.
4. **Cramming in too much.** Schedules, three social handles, a mission statement, and a QR code do not fit in 1546×423 pixels. Pick one message and make it legible.
5. **Never updating it.** A banner advertising "New videos every Friday" when you last posted in March erodes trust. Refresh the banner whenever your schedule, branding, or content focus changes.

### What size should my YouTube banner be?
Upload at 2560×1440 pixels, keep all important content inside the centered 1546×423 safe area, and keep the file under 6 MB.

### Why is my banner blurry on YouTube?
Usually the source image was smaller than 2560×1440 and got upscaled, or it was over-compressed. Start from a high-resolution original and export JPEG at 80–85% quality.

### Does the banner safe area really matter?
Yes — it is the only region visible on all devices. Text or logos outside it will be cropped on mobile, where most viewers watch.
