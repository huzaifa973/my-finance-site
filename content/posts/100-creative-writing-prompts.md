Every writer knows the blank page problem. You want to write — you even have time — but the moment you sit down, every idea evaporates and you end up reorganizing your desk instead. That is not a motivation problem; it is a starting problem. Writing prompts solve exactly that by handing your brain something specific to react to. A good prompt is a small doorway: it gives you a character, a situation, or a question, and your imagination does the rest. This collection of 100 prompts is built for that purpose — a full year's worth of starting points you can dip into whenever the blank page stares back.

The prompts are organized into four groups so you can match them to your mood and your project. The fiction prompts build stories, scenes, and characters — perfect for short stories, novels-in-progress, or flash fiction practice. The journaling prompts turn the lens inward: memory, gratitude, goals, and the questions that matter. The poetry prompts work with images, sounds, and constraints that loosen up your language. And the dialogue prompts are pure conversation — two voices in a room — which is one of the fastest ways to discover character. Writers, teachers, journalers, and students can use the same pack differently: daily warm-ups, classroom exercises, morning pages, or a rescue kit for NaNoWriMo slumps.

## How to use these prompts

1. Pick one prompt — don't browse all 100 looking for the "perfect" one; the first that sparks anything is the right one.
2. Set a timer for 10–15 minutes and write continuously without editing, deleting, or re-reading.
3. If a prompt stalls after two minutes, switch prompts instead of forcing it — momentum is the goal.
4. Keep everything you write, even the bad drafts; revisit them monthly and you'll spot ideas worth developing.
5. Share the pack with a writing group or class and write from the same prompt — comparing results is where the real learning happens.

The blank page only wins when you wait for inspiration to arrive first. A prompt flips the order: start writing, and the ideas catch up.

## Frequently asked questions

**What if my response to a prompt is terrible?**
Then the prompt worked. Prompt writing is training, not performance — most responses are raw material, and one in ten contains a seed worth growing. Keep the seeds, recycle the rest.

**Can these prompts work for kids or students?**
Yes — most prompts are age-flexible, and teachers commonly use prompt packs for creative writing warm-ups. For younger writers, start with the journaling and poetry groups, which need no plot-building experience.
