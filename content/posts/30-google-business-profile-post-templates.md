A Google Business Profile that never posts looks closed even when it is open — customers check the profile, see nothing new for months, and move on to a competitor who posted this week. These thirty templates remove the “what do I post?” problem: offers, updates, events, product news, and reminders, each with a clear structure and a call to action. They suit shops, clinics, restaurants, and any local business that wants its profile to look alive.

## What's inside this resource

The templates are grouped by post type because Google treats them differently: offers carry expiry dates, events carry dates and times, and updates carry news. Each template leaves placeholders in brackets for your details, so a post takes two minutes: pick a type, fill the blanks, add a photo, publish. Post at least weekly — freshness is the whole point.

1. OFFER: [20% off all haircuts] this week only — book before [Friday] and mention this post.
2. UPDATE: We are now open [Sundays]! Come by between [10am and 4pm].

8. EVENT: Free workshop: [bread basics] on [Saturday morning] — six places left.

10. REMINDER: Book your [December] appointment now — slots fill fast.

## How to use this resource

1. Pick the post type that matches your news — offers need dates, events need times.
2. Add one real photo per post; posts with photos get far more views than text alone.
3. Keep the call to action to one thing: book, call, visit, or order.
4. Post at least once a week, and pin nothing — freshness beats perfection.
5. Reply to every comment and question on your posts within a day.

A resource is only useful when it gets used — pick one item today, use it as written, and adapt it from experience rather than from theory.

## Frequently asked questions

**How often should I post on my Business Profile?**

At least weekly. Businesses that post regularly look active and current, which is exactly what a searcher deciding between two listings wants to see.

**Do Business Profile posts help rankings?**

They support visibility and clicks more than they directly move rankings — but a profile that earns clicks and calls is a profile Google keeps showing.

**What makes a good post photo?**

A real photo of your place, product, or team, in good light. Stock photos are easy to spot and do nothing for trust.
