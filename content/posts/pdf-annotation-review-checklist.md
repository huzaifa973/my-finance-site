Collaborative PDF reviews fall apart in the details: a comment thread left unresolved, a highlight hiding a signature, a 'redaction' that is just a black box anyone can remove. This twenty-point checklist is the final pass before a reviewed PDF goes out — for editors, lawyers, designers, project managers, and anyone who marks up documents for a living. Run through it once, and the file you send is the file you meant to send.

## What's inside this resource

The checklist moves in the order a reviewer actually works: comments and threads first, then markup legibility, then the dangerous items — redactions, signatures, and hidden content — and finally export and accessibility checks. The single most important habit here is the last one: open the finished PDF fresh, as your recipient will see it, and read every annotation once. Reviews done only in the editing app miss what the recipient sees.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between a redaction and a black box?

A true redaction removes the underlying text from the file. A black rectangle drawn over text only hides it visually — the text can still be copied or revealed. Always use your editor's redaction tool, then verify.

### Should I flatten annotations before sending?

Only when the recipient should not edit them. Flattening bakes markups into the page; keeping them live lets the recipient reply. Decide based on whether the review is still open.

### How do I keep annotation colors consistent?

Agree on a legend before the review starts — for example, yellow for key points, green for approved, pink for issues — and write it at the top of the first page.
