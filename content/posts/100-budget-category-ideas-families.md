Most family budgets fail not from overspending, but from missing categories. The forgotten costs — school supplies, vet visits, birthday gifts, car tires — do not disappear when they are left out of the budget; they just ambush it. These one hundred category ideas cover the full life of a family budget, from housing and groceries to the sinking funds for holidays, appliances, and elder care. They are written for parents setting up a first budget, and for families whose budget keeps breaking for reasons they cannot name.

## What's inside this resource

The categories are grouped the way a family actually spends: home, food, kids, transport, health, fun, savings, and the long tail of subscriptions, insurance, and debt. The practical move is to start with the categories you know, then scan the rest for the ones you forgot — those forgotten ones are usually the leak. Add a sinking fund line for each irregular cost, funded monthly, and the 'surprise' expenses stop being surprises.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many budget categories should a family have?

Enough to see where money goes, not so many that tracking becomes a chore. Fifty to seventy active categories is plenty for most families; start with the big ones and add forgotten costs as they appear.

### What is a sinking fund?

A small monthly saving for an irregular cost — car tires, holidays, appliance replacement. Funding it monthly turns a large surprise bill into a planned one.

### Should kids' costs be separate categories?

Yes, at least the big ones: childcare, school, activities, and clothes. Lumped together, kids' spending hides the fastest-growing part of most family budgets.
