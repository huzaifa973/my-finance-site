Editing is where amateur audio becomes professional audio. The recording gives you the raw material; the edit removes the mistakes, evens out the levels, tames the hiss, and delivers a file that sounds consistent from the first second to the last. Beginners often skip editing because it looks technical — this checklist breaks it into 20 ordered steps anyone can follow.

It is written for podcasters, YouTubers, and course creators editing their first projects in free software like Audacity. The steps run in the order professionals actually work: structural cuts first, cleanup second, sweetening third, loudness and export last. The free download below has all 20 steps to keep beside you while you edit.

## What the 20 steps cover

Steps 1-5 are the structural edit: duplicating your raw file (never edit your only copy), marking sections, cutting mistakes and filler, and trimming dead air. This is the highest-value work — a tight 20 minutes beats a rambling 40. Get the structure right before touching any effect.

Steps 6-13 are cleanup and sweetening: gentle noise removal from your room tone, EQ to remove muddiness and add presence, light compression to even out loud and quiet moments, de-essing harsh S sounds, and fixing clicks and plosives. Steps 14-20 are quality control and delivery: checking on two playback devices, hitting loudness targets, exporting correctly, and — the step everyone skips — listening to the actual export before it goes public.

## How to use this checklist

1. Work strictly in order: structural cuts (1-5), cleanup (6-13), then loudness and export (14-20). Effects before structure wastes time.
2. Go gentle on every effect — you can always add more processing, but you cannot undo over-processing.
3. Use steps 14 and 19 as your quality gates: if it fails the two-device test or the export listen, fix it before publishing.
4. Save numbered versions as you go (edit_v1, edit_v2) so you can always step back.
5. After three or four projects, the list becomes instinct — keep the download handy until then.

Every professional editor follows essentially this sequence; the only difference is speed. Twenty steps, in order, turn a rough recording into something people actually enjoy hearing. Download the checklist below and edit your next project with it open.

## Frequently asked questions

**What software should a beginner use for audio editing?**
Audacity (free, open source) handles everything on this checklist and runs on any computer. If you want a gentler learning curve with good defaults, most podcast hosts also offer simple web editors. Do not buy expensive software until free tools limit you — for most beginners, they never do.

**How loud should my podcast or video audio be?**
Match your platform: podcasts target around -16 LUFS for stereo (-19 for mono), YouTube normalizes to about -14 LUFS. True peak should stay below -1 dBTP so encoding does not distort. These numbers sound technical, but every major editor shows them in its loudness meter — just mix until the meter agrees.

**Why does my audio sound different after exporting?**
Usually one of three things: the export format compressed it harder than expected (use higher bitrates), the loudness was normalized by the platform, or you monitored on headphones and the export revealed issues your headphones hid. Always listen to the actual exported file once before publishing — step 19 exists for exactly this reason.
