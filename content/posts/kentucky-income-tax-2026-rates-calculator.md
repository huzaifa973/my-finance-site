Kentucky made taxes simpler — and cheaper — for 2026. The state's individual income tax is now a **flat 3.5%**, down from 4.0% in 2025, and the standard deduction rose to **$3,360**. No brackets, no marginal-rate math: one rate on your Kentucky taxable income.

Here is everything you need to estimate your 2026 Kentucky tax.

## The 2026 Kentucky rate: 3.5% flat

Kentucky abandoned graduated brackets back in 2018 and has been cutting its flat rate ever since: 4.5% for 2023, 4.0% for 2024–2025, and now **3.5% for tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2026**.

The cut was locked in after the state met the revenue "trigger" conditions set by earlier legislation — and the law allows further reductions in future years if the triggers keep being met. For 2026, though, the math is beautifully simple: Kentucky taxable income × 0.035.

## How the calculation works

Kentucky starts with your **federal adjusted gross income (AGI)** — the number at the bottom of your federal Form 1040 — then applies state-specific additions and subtractions:

1. **Start with federal AGI.** Most federal adjustments (retirement contributions, student loan interest, half of self-employment tax) carry over automatically.
2. **Apply Kentucky additions/subtractions.** Kentucky has its own list — for example, interest from non-Kentucky municipal bonds gets added back.
3. **Subtract deductions.** Take the **$3,360 standard deduction** (same for all filing statuses) or itemize if that saves more.
4. **Multiply by 3.5%.** The result is your Kentucky income tax before credits.
5. **Subtract credits.** Kentucky offers credits (including for taxes paid to other states and various personal credits) that reduce the bill dollar-for-dollar.

**Worked example:** Suppose your federal AGI is $60,000, you take the standard deduction, and have no special adjustments or credits. Kentucky taxable income = $60,000 − $3,360 = $56,640. Tax = $56,640 × 3.5% = **$1,982.40**.

## Who must file a Kentucky return

- **Full-year residents** with gross income above the filing threshold (which the $3,360 standard deduction effectively sets).
- **Part-year residents and nonresidents** with income from Kentucky sources (wages earned in Kentucky, rental property, business income).
- Anyone who had Kentucky tax withheld and wants a refund.

The return is due **April 15** (same as federal), with extensions available.

## Do not forget local occupational taxes

Kentucky's headline rate is not the whole story. Many Kentucky cities and counties levy **occupational license taxes** on wages and business profits — commonly 1%–2%, varying by locality. Louisville, Lexington, and most mid-size cities have them. These are withheld from paychecks separately and filed separately from the state return. When budgeting, add your local rate to the 3.5% state rate.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Kentucky's income tax rate for 2026?**
3.5% flat. Effective January 1, 2026, Kentucky taxes all individual income at a single 3.5% rate, down from 4.0% in 2025. There are no graduated brackets.

**What is Kentucky's standard deduction for 2026?**
$3,360 for all filers, up from $3,270 in 2025. Subtract it (or your itemized deductions) from Kentucky income before applying the 3.5% rate.

**Does Kentucky have local income taxes?**
Kentucky has no local sales tax, but many cities and counties impose occupational license taxes on wages (typically 0.5%–2.5%), which are separate from the state income tax.

**How do I estimate my Kentucky income tax?**
Start with federal AGI, apply Kentucky additions/subtractions, subtract the $3,360 standard deduction (or itemized), then multiply by 3.5%. For the federal side, use our free US federal income tax estimator.
