You have a product to sell — a t-shirt design, a candle label, a book cover, an app icon — but no photographer, no studio, and no Photoshop. A product mockup solves this: it drops your design into a realistic scene (a shirt on a model, a mug on a desk, a phone in a hand) so it looks like a professional photo shoot.

The good news: you can make convincing mockups entirely in your browser, for free, in minutes. Here is how.

## What a mockup actually is

A mockup is a template image — usually a photo of a blank product in a real setting — with a placeholder area where your design gets composited. Good mockup generators handle the hard parts automatically: perspective warping (so your design follows the folds of a shirt), lighting, and shadows. Your job is only to supply a clean design file.

**Step 1 — Cut out your product.** A clean mockup starts with a clean cut-out. **Step 2 — Place it on a mockup scene.** Use any free online mockup maker or template site, upload your transparent PNG, and let the template handle perspective and shadows. Most let you adjust position, scale, and rotation.

**Step 3 — Export at the right size.** For marketplaces, export at least 2000px on the longest side (Etsy recommends 2000px+; Amazon wants 2000px+ for zoom). For social posts, 1080px square works.

## Method 2: DIY mockup in a free photo editor

If you want full control, you can build a mockup manually with a free browser-based photo editor:

1. Find a free stock photo of the blank product in a nice setting (a plain mug on a table, a folded shirt).
2. Open your design PNG and the photo in the editor.
3. Paste your design as a new layer, reduce opacity to ~50% temporarily, and use the transform/distort tool to match the product's perspective.
4. Set the layer blend mode to "Multiply" for light-colored products so the photo's texture and shadows show through.
5. Restore full opacity, add a slight blur or noise if the design looks too sharp compared to the photo.

This takes 10–15 minutes but gives you a mockup nobody else is using — useful when every competitor is using the same five templates.

## Which mockup scenes work best

- **Apparel:** flat-lay shirt on a neutral background, or a "ghost mannequin" style shot. Avoid busy lifestyle photos for the main listing image — save those for image 2–5.
- **Mugs and drinkware:** a simple desk scene with soft window light; white or light-gray backgrounds convert best.
- **Books and ebooks:** a 3D book render at a slight angle, or a hand holding the book for lifestyle listings.
- **Phone cases and devices:** a hand holding the phone in a real environment — this sells scale better than a floating render.
- **Posters and art prints:** a framed print on a wall above furniture; frame the print at a slight angle for depth.

## Common mockup mistakes to avoid

**Using low-resolution designs.** Your design file should be at least as large as the area it will cover in the final image. A 500px logo stretched across a 2000px shirt mockup will look blurry and unprofessional.

**Ignoring the light direction.** If the scene's light comes from the left, your design's shading should agree. Templates handle this; DIY mockups need a manual check.

**Overcrowded scenes.** The product should occupy 60–80% of the frame in the main image. Lifestyle context is great for secondary images, not the hero shot.

**Forgetting mobile.** Most shoppers browse on phones. Check your mockup at thumbnail size — if the design is unreadable at 300px wide, simplify.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I make product mockups without Photoshop?**
Yes. Free browser-based tools can place your design or product photo onto realistic scenes — t-shirts, mugs, book covers, phone screens — with no software to install and no design skills required.

**What do I need to make a mockup?**
You need two things: a clean cut-out of your product or design (a transparent PNG works best), and a mockup scene or template. Our free background remover can cut your product out in seconds.

**Are free mockup makers good enough for a real store?**
For most listings, yes. Clean free mockups with good lighting and realistic shadows convert just as well as expensive ones. What matters most is a sharp product cut-out and a believable scene.

**Do mockup images work for print-on-demand?**
Absolutely — mockups are the standard way POD sellers present products. Pair a transparent design file with a garment or poster mockup and your listing looks like a real photo shoot.
