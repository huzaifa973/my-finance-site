Starting a livestream feels harder than it is, mostly because of the equipment questions: which camera, which microphone, which software, and how fast must the internet be? The honest answer for beginners is simpler than the gear reviews suggest. A phone or basic webcam, a decent USB microphone, one light, and a stable connection are enough for a stream that looks and sounds professional — the rest can wait until you have an audience that justifies it.
This checklist walks you through all 30 steps in order: planning your stream, setting up software and connection, getting camera, lighting, and audio right, running a private test, and handling the live show itself. It suits new streamers on YouTube, Twitch, TikTok, or Facebook, and it doubles as a pre-stream routine you can repeat every time. Ten steps are below; the free download holds the complete checklist.

## What's inside this checklist

The planning section settles the decisions that shape everything else: topic, format, platform, and the platform's own requirements. The connection and software section covers the unglamorous part that actually determines stream quality — upload speed, a wired connection, and free software that does the job.
Camera, lighting, and audio get their own section because audio matters more than video: viewers forgive a plain picture, but they leave fast when they cannot hear you clearly. The before-you-go-live section is the test routine — private test stream, title, thumbnail, and run-of-show — and the final section covers the stream itself: greeting viewers, handling quiet chat, taking breaks, and turning the recording into clips afterwards.

## Livestream equipment checklist (free sample)

Ten steps from the full 30-step checklist. The complete checklist is in the free download.

1. Decide your topic and format: Q&A, tutorial, gameplay, chat, or interview.
2. Pick a platform and check its streaming requirements (account age, followers, software).
3. Choose streaming software: OBS Studio is free and works for most beginners.
4. Test your internet speed: aim for at least 10 Mbps upload for 720p, 20 Mbps for 1080p.
5. Use a wired ethernet connection instead of Wi-Fi whenever possible.
6. Camera: a phone or basic webcam is fine to start; upgrade later.
7. Lighting: face a window or use one affordable ring light — never sit with light behind you.
8. Audio: use a USB microphone or headset mic; built-in laptop mics sound echoey.
9. Test audio levels before going live: your voice should peak in the green-to-yellow range.
10. Set up your scene: camera, screen share, and any overlays you want.

## How to use this resource

1. Work through the checklist top to bottom for your first stream, and keep it open during setup.
2. Always run a private test stream before going public — it catches audio and connection problems cheaply.
3. Spend your first money on audio, not a camera: a USB microphone improves a stream more than any other single upgrade.
4. Save your streaming software profile after a good setup, so a crash never costs you your scenes.
5. After each stream, write down one thing to improve next time; small fixes compound fast.

Your first stream will not be perfect, and it does not need to be — it needs to happen, so the second one can be better.

## Frequently asked questions

**What equipment do I really need to start streaming?**

A device with a camera, a USB microphone or headset, one light source, and a stable internet connection. That is enough. Upgrade gear only after you have streamed regularly for a month or more.

**How fast does my internet need to be?**

At least 10 Mbps upload for a smooth 720p stream, and 20 Mbps for 1080p. A wired ethernet connection is far more reliable than Wi-Fi at the same speed.

**Should I use OBS Studio?**

Yes, for most beginners. It is free, works on all major platforms, and handles scenes, overlays, and screen sharing. Start with one simple scene and add complexity later.
