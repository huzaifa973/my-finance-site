Rocket Money (formerly Truebill) promises to find the money leaking out of your accounts — forgotten subscriptions, sneaky bill increases, unused memberships — and either cancel them for you or negotiate them down. It's one of the most advertised finance apps in the US, which raises the obvious question: is it worth paying for, or is the free version enough?

I dug into the features, the pricing, and the fine print so you don't have to.

## What Rocket Money actually does

Strip away the marketing and Rocket Money does four things:

1. **Finds your subscriptions** — connects to your bank accounts and credit cards, scans your transactions, and builds a list of every recurring charge it detects.
2. **Cancels subscriptions for you** — you tap "cancel" in the app, and Rocket Money's team handles the phone calls and chats with the provider.
3. **Negotiates bills** — internet, cable, phone, and similar bills get reviewed for lower rates; if they succeed, they take a cut of your savings.
4. **Budgets and tracks spending** — spending categories, alerts, and a basic budget view round out the app.

It's essentially a subscription auditor with a budgeting app attached. If your main problem is "money keeps disappearing and I don't know where," that's exactly the problem it solves.

## Pricing: free vs Premium

<!-- ADSENSE: in-article slot -->

Rocket Money's pricing is unusual. The **free version** shows your subscriptions and basic spending data but locks the genuinely useful features — cancellation concierge, bill negotiation, premium budgeting tools, and credit score monitoring — behind **Premium**.

Premium uses a sliding scale: you choose what you pay within a range (historically around $6–$12 per month, with annual billing cheaper per month). The psychology is clever — picking your own price makes you feel in control — but don't overthink it: pick the lowest tier first. You can always change it later.

One important detail: the **bill negotiation service takes a percentage of what it saves you** (typically 30–40% of the first year's savings). So if they lower your $120/month internet bill to $90, that's $360/year in savings, and their cut is roughly $108–$144. Still a net win, but know the fee exists.

## Where Rocket Money shines

**Subscription discovery is genuinely good.** Most people underestimate how many recurring charges they have. The average user finds subscriptions they'd forgotten — a free trial that converted, a streaming service they stopped watching, an app subscription from two phones ago. Finding even two $15/month charges you don't use pays for a year of Premium.

**The cancellation concierge saves real hassle.** Canceling a gym membership or a cable add-on can mean a 40-minute phone call with a retention specialist. Handing that to someone else is legitimately valuable, especially if you hate phone calls.

**Bill negotiation works often enough.** Success isn't guaranteed — it depends on your provider and your account history — but when it works, a single negotiation can cover the annual cost of the app.

## Where it falls short

**The budgeting tools are basic.** If you want serious budgeting — zero-based budgeting, envelope methods, detailed category control — dedicated apps or even a spreadsheet do it better. Rocket Money's budgeting feels like a bonus feature, not the main event.

**It can't cancel everything.** Some companies legally require the account holder to cancel directly. In those cases the app gives you instructions, which is helpful but not the hands-off experience advertised.

**Detection isn't perfect.** It occasionally flags one-time purchases as subscriptions or misses annual charges until they hit. You still need to review the list yourself, especially around annual renewal season.

**The upsell pressure is real.** The free tier exists partly as a funnel to Premium. Expect regular prompts to upgrade — mildly annoying, though easy to ignore.

Before paying, consider what problem you're actually solving:

The honest math: if you suspect you're wasting $30+/month on subscriptions you don't use, one month of Premium plus a cancellation spree pays for itself many times over. You could even subscribe for 2–3 months, clean house, then cancel — there's no long-term contract on monthly billing.

## The verdict: is it worth paying for?

**Yes, temporarily, for most people.** Rocket Money Premium is best thought of as a one-time cleanup service, not a forever subscription. Pay for a few months, let it find and kill the charges draining your accounts, negotiate a bill or two, then cancel it — using an app to cancel an app has a nice symmetry to it.

**The free tier alone is worth downloading.** Seeing every subscription in one list, even without the cancellation service, is eye-opening. You can then cancel the easy ones yourself in an afternoon.

**Skip Premium if** your subscriptions are already under control, you enjoy negotiating bills yourself, or you're on a very tight budget where every subscription gets scrutinized — in that case, put the money toward your emergency fund instead (see [how to save $1,000 in 30 days](/posts/how-to-save-1000-in-30-days/)).

### FAQ

**Q:** How much does Rocket Money cost in 2026?

A: Rocket Money uses sliding-scale pricing for its Premium plan, typically ranging from about $6 to $12 per month depending on the billing cycle you choose. A limited free version exists, but most useful features — cancellation assistance, bill negotiation, and full budgeting tools — require Premium. Check the app for current pricing, as it changes.

**Q:** Can Rocket Money really cancel subscriptions for me?

A: Yes, for many services. You authorize Rocket Money to act on your behalf, and their team contacts the provider to cancel. It works well for mainstream subscriptions, though some companies require you to cancel directly, in which case Rocket Money gives you step-by-step instructions instead.

**Q:** Is Rocket Money safe to connect to my bank account?

A: Rocket Money uses bank-level 256-bit encryption and connects through third-party data aggregators with read-only access — it can see transactions but can't move your money. That said, any account aggregation carries some risk, so use a strong unique password and enable two-factor authentication.

**Q:** What is the best free alternative to Rocket Money?

A: For subscription tracking, your bank's own app plus a quarterly manual audit gets you 80% of the value for free. For budgeting, free apps like the budgeting tools built into many banking apps, or a simple spreadsheet, cover the basics. Rocket Money's paid tier is worth it mainly for the done-for-you cancellation and bill negotiation.
