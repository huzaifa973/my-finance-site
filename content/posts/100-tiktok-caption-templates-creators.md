A TikTok caption is not a summary — it is the nudge that gets someone to watch, comment, or follow. The best captions are short, specific, and a little open-ended: they promise something, ask something, or start a sentence the video finishes. This pack builds one hundred captions from ten proven hooks crossed with ten everyday topics, so you can generate captions for a month of posts in one sitting.

## What's inside this resource

The hooks cover the styles that perform on TikTok: point-of-view openers, day-count series, ratings, attempts, and direct calls to watch. The topics are deliberately ordinary — routines, recipes, setups, budgets — because ordinary topics with a good hook outperform exotic topics with a weak one. Swap in your niche and the pattern keeps working.

## How to use this resource

1. Pick a hook that matches the video's energy — a calm video wants a calm hook.
2. Keep captions under 150 characters so they display fully on most screens.
3. End with a question or a prompt when you want comments, not just views.
4. Use two to four relevant hashtags, not thirty — discovery favours focus.
5. Save your best-performing captions and reuse their structure, not their words.

A resource is only useful when it gets used — pick one item today, use it as written, and adapt it from experience rather than from theory.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long should a TikTok caption be?**

Short — usually under 150 characters. The video does the talking; the caption's job is the hook and the call to action.

**Should captions include hashtags?**

A few relevant ones help discovery, but stuffing hashtags looks spammy and rarely helps. Two to four well-chosen tags beat thirty random ones.

**Do captions affect the algorithm?**

Indirectly: a caption that earns comments and re-watches signals engagement, and engagement is what the algorithm rewards.
