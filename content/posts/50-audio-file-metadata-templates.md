Metadata is the difference between a track that looks professional in every player and one that shows up as 'Unknown Artist — Track 01'. These fifty fields cover everything worth setting on an audio file: the basics every listener sees, the credits that matter to musicians, and the technical fields that DJs, libraries, and distributors use. They are written for musicians, podcasters, DJs, and audio editors tagging files before release or distribution.

## What's inside this resource

The fields are ordered by importance: identity first (title, artist, album), then organization (track number, genre, year), then credits and rights (composer, publisher, ISRC), then technical details (BPM, key, bitrate). The single most valuable habit is setting the ISRC code for released music — it is the permanent identifier distributors and royalty systems use. After that, a square cover image of at least 1400 pixels is what makes the track look right everywhere it appears.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is an ISRC code?

The International Standard Recording Code — a unique identifier for a specific recording, used by distributors, charts, and royalty systems. Get one from your distributor or national ISRC agency before release.

### What size should album art be?

At least 1400 by 1400 pixels, square, in JPEG or PNG. Streaming services reject smaller artwork, and larger images stay sharp on high-resolution screens.

### Which metadata format do most players read?

ID3 for MP3 files and Vorbis comments for FLAC and OGG. Most tagging tools handle both — set the fields once and the tool writes the right format per file type.
