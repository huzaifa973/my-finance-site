Ohio's tax return has more moving parts than most residents expect: a state income tax, a school district tax in many districts, and a stack of credits and deductions that do not appear on the federal return. This twenty-point checklist walks Ohio residents through the items worth checking before filing — retirement credits, the 529 and ABLE deductions, business income rules, and property tax relief — so nothing valuable is missed. It is written for Ohio residents filing their own returns, and for anyone helping family members file in the state.

## What's inside this resource

The checklist is organized from the most common items to the most overlooked: start with the state and school district taxes themselves, then the credits nearly everyone qualifies for, then the deductions tied to savings and retirement, and finally property tax relief for homeowners. Two warnings apply to the whole list: Ohio's amounts and percentages change with legislation, so verify the current year's figures before filing, and this checklist is general information, not tax advice — complex situations deserve a professional.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do all Ohio residents pay school district income tax?

No — only residents of school districts that levy one. Check your district before filing; many Ohioans owe it without realizing it exists.

### What is the Ohio 529 deduction?

Ohio lets you deduct up to $4,000 per beneficiary per year for contributions to an Ohio 529 college savings plan, with a carryforward for larger contributions.

### Is this checklist tax advice?

No. It is a general checklist for orientation. Tax law changes yearly and personal situations vary — consult a qualified tax professional for advice about your return.
