Clean voice audio is 80% setup and 20% gear. Podcasters, YouTubers, course creators, and voiceover beginners all make the same mistake: they buy a microphone, hit record in an echoey room, and wonder why it sounds amateur. This 20-step checklist walks you through the entire setup — room choice, noise control, mic placement, gain staging, and file habits — before you record a single word that matters.

It is built for beginners recording at home with whatever they own, from a USB mic to a phone with a headset. Follow it in order the first few times; after that it becomes a five-minute pre-flight routine. The free download below has all 20 steps in a printable file.

## What the 20 steps cover

Steps 1-5 tame your environment: picking the quietest room, killing hums and buzzes, and doing a silence test so you hear what the mic hears. Most bad audio is decided here, before any equipment is involved. A closet full of clothes genuinely beats an untreated spare bedroom — small and soft wins.

Steps 6-14 are the microphone itself: placement four to six inches off-axis, pop filters, closed-back monitoring headphones, and gain staging so peaks sit in the safe zone. Steps 15-20 are session discipline: warming up your voice, marking mistakes for easy editing, capturing room tone for noise removal, test recordings, and the unglamorous hero of professional audio — clear filenames and immediate backups.

1. Walk through all 20 steps in order the first three times you record — it takes about ten minutes.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need an expensive microphone for good voice recordings?**
No. A $30-$100 USB microphone in a quiet, soft-furnished room will outperform a $500 microphone in an echoey kitchen. Room and mic placement matter far more than price. Upgrade your space first — blankets and closed doors are free.

**Why does my recording echo even with a good mic?**
Echo comes from hard, parallel surfaces: bare walls, tile floors, empty rooms. The mic hears your voice plus its reflections. Soften the room — record in a smaller space, hang blankets, add rugs and curtains, and get the mic close to your mouth so your voice dominates the reflections.

**What recording settings should I use?**
Record at 44.1kHz or 48kHz, 24-bit, as WAV. That gives you maximum quality to edit with; you can always export a compressed MP3 later. Set your gain so the loudest peaks land around -12dB to -6dB — loud enough to be clean, with headroom so nothing clips.
