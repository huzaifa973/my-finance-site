Good sleep is less about one big change and more about a repeatable routine — the same cues, in the same order, every night, until your brain learns that the routine means sleep. This twenty-point checklist builds that routine: light and screens, bedroom temperature, wind-down activities, and the habits that keep racing thoughts off the pillow. It is written for anyone who lies awake too long, wakes groggy, or knows their evenings work against their mornings.

## What's inside this resource

The checklist follows the evening in order: afternoon cutoffs (caffeine, heavy meals), the wind-down hour (dim lights, screens off, a book or stretch), and the bedroom itself (cool, dark, quiet, phone charging elsewhere). The most underrated item is the notepad by the bed: writing down tomorrow's tasks externalizes them, which is what actually stops the mind from rehearsing them. Pick ten items to start, run them for two weeks, then add more — a routine you keep beats a perfect one you abandon.

## Frequently asked questions

### What time should I go to bed?

Whatever time lets you get 7–9 hours before your fixed wake time. The wake time matters more than the bedtime — keep it consistent, even on weekends.

### Are screens really that bad before bed?

The content matters as much as the light: stimulating news or work email keeps the brain alert. If you must use a screen, choose something calm and use a blue-light filter.

### What if I cannot fall asleep?

Get up after about 20 minutes, read something dull in dim light until drowsy, then return to bed. Lying awake trains the brain to associate bed with wakefulness.
