Budgets do not fail in January — they fail in week three, quietly, when nobody looks at them. A weekly review is the habit that keeps a budget honest: fifteen minutes, same day each week, checking what came in, what went out, and what next week needs. This twenty-point checklist turns that review into a routine you can actually keep, covering income, spending, bills, savings, and the plan for the week ahead.

## What's inside this resource

The checks are ordered the way a review should run: first confirm the money that arrived, then scan spending for surprises, then handle bills and savings, then plan next week. Do them in order and the review takes fifteen minutes; skip the order and it takes an hour and gets abandoned.

## The resource (sample)

1. Confirm all expected income arrived this week.
2. Scan every transaction for anything you do not recognise.
3. Total spending by category and compare with the weekly target.
4. Flag any category over budget and note why.
5. Check upcoming bills due in the next seven days.
6. Move the planned savings amount before spending the rest.
7. Review subscriptions: cancel anything unused this week.
8. Plan next week's big expenses in advance.
9. Update the budget tracker with this week's real numbers.
10. Write down one money win from this week.

## How to use this resource

1. Pick a fixed day and time — Sunday evening works for most people.
2. Do the review with your bank app open, not from memory.
3. Keep it to fifteen minutes; a review that drags gets skipped.
4. Celebrate one win each week — reviews that only punish do not survive.
5. Carry exactly one action into next week, not a list of ten.

A resource is only useful when it gets used — pick one item today, use it as written, and adapt it from experience rather than from theory.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long should a weekly review take?**

About fifteen minutes once it is routine. The first few take longer while you learn where everything lives.

**What if I overspent badly?**

Note it without panic, adjust next week's plan, and move on. A review that feels like punishment is a review you will stop doing.

**Should couples review together?**

Yes, briefly. A five-minute shared check prevents most money arguments, which usually start as surprises.
