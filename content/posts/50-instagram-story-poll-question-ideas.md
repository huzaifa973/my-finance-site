Story polls only work when the question is easy to answer in one tap and fun enough to bother with. “Do you like this?” is neither — it is vague, and nobody cares about the result. These fifty questions are built the way polls should be: this-or-that choices, quick opinions, and light debates where both answers feel valid. Use them to warm up an audience, test content ideas, or just get people tapping again.

## What's inside this resource

The questions are grouped by type: choices (morning or night), opinions (best season, unpopular takes), habits (how people actually live), and fun debates (the arguments friends never settle). A good poll also teaches you something — which product, topic, or time your audience prefers — so note the winners and use them.

## The resource (sample)

1. Morning person or night owl?
2. Coffee or tea — pick one forever.
3. Beach holiday or mountain trip?
4. Early bird or snooze button?
5. Books or movies on a rainy day?
6. Cook at home or order in tonight?
7. Cats or dogs — the eternal debate.
8. Summer or winter, no middle ground?
9. Plan everything or wing it?
10. Texting or calling — be honest.

## How to use this resource

1. Post polls when your audience is actually online — evenings and lunch breaks beat mornings for most accounts.
2. Keep the two options short; long options get skipped, not read.
3. Follow up on the result in the next story — “you voted, so here it is” closes the loop.
4. Use one poll per story set, not five in a row — fatigue kills taps.
5. Save winning questions and reuse them with new audiences.

A resource is only useful when it gets used — pick one item today, use it as written, and adapt it from experience rather than from theory.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many poll stickers per day is too many?**

One or two per day is plenty. Polls are seasoning, not the meal — too many and followers start skipping your stories entirely.

**Do polls actually help reach?**

Yes, indirectly: taps are engagement, and accounts whose stories get tapped are shown more often. The effect is real but modest — consistency matters more.

**What if almost nobody votes?**

That is data too: either the question was dull or the audience is cold. Try easier, funnier questions before assuming the audience is gone.
