A great podcast interview rarely comes from a great guest alone — it comes from questions that give the guest something real to answer. Yes-or-no questions get yes-or-no answers, but an open question about a turning point, a failure, or a lesson learned gets a story. These fifty question templates are built for podcast hosts at any level, from a first episode recorded in a spare room to a long-running show looking for fresher conversations. Use them as written, or adapt the bracketed parts to your guest's world, and your interviews will sound less like a Q&A and more like a conversation worth listening to.

## What's inside this resource

The templates are grouped by the job each question does: warm openers that settle a guest, story questions that draw out real moments, turning-point questions that reveal decisions, craft questions about how they actually do the work, advice questions listeners can use, and closers that end on a high note. Every template is open-ended and starts with what, how, or why — the words that invite a story instead of a sentence. Mix three or four types in one interview and you get a natural arc: easy start, deep middle, useful ending.

## How to use this resource

1. Before recording, read your guest's work and pick eight to ten questions that fit their actual story, not just their job title.
2. Replace every bracketed placeholder with your guest's details, so questions sound researched rather than generic.
3. Open with a warm-up question and save the hardest or most personal question for the middle, once trust is built.
4. Listen for the interesting detail inside an answer and follow it with one unscripted question before moving on.
5. Close with a forward-looking question and a clear invitation for listeners to find your guest's work.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many questions should I prepare for one episode?

Eight to twelve solid questions usually fill a 30 to 45 minute conversation, because good answers lead to follow-ups. Over-preparing a long list makes hosts rush; a shorter list with room to listen produces better episodes.

### Should I send questions to the guest in advance?

Sharing broad topics in advance helps guests prepare stories and relax, but sending the exact word-for-word list can produce rehearsed answers. A middle path works best: share themes, keep a few questions as surprises.

### What if a guest gives a very short answer?

Follow up gently with a story prompt, such as asking for an example or a moment when that changed. Short answers usually mean the question was too abstract — make it concrete and the guest will usually open up.
