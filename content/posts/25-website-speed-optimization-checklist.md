A slow website quietly costs you visitors, sales, and search rankings. Most people leave a page that takes more than a few seconds to load, and search engines now treat speed as a ranking signal through Core Web Vitals. The good news is that the biggest wins are usually simple: oversized images, unused code, and missing caching do most of the damage, and all of them are fixable without rebuilding your site.

This checklist gives you 25 practical speed checks, ordered from the fixes that matter most to the fine-tuning that comes last. Work through it top to bottom, testing your site after each batch of changes, and you will usually see the largest improvement in the first ten items alone. It is written for website owners, bloggers, and small business owners — no developer jargon, and every item says exactly what to do.

One principle runs through the whole checklist: measure, change one thing, and measure again. Speed work is tempting to do in a big bang — a new theme, a new host, a dozen plugins swapped at once — but then you never learn which change actually helped, and you cannot undo a change that hurt. Treat the checklist as a sequence of small experiments instead. Note your starting scores, work through the image items first, and celebrate the early wins: compressing a handful of hero images often cuts seconds off a page on its own. The later items matter too, but they are polish on a foundation that the first ten items build.

## What's inside this resource

The checklist covers five areas. The first group tackles images and media, which are the single biggest cause of slow pages. Next come code and caching fixes, then server and hosting improvements, followed by Core Web Vitals targets, and finally the ongoing habits that keep a fast site fast. Each item is one clear action, so you can tick items off one at a time or hand the list to a developer as a brief. ## How to use this resource

Start by measuring your site with a free tool such as PageSpeed Insights or WebPageTest, and write down your scores. Work through the checklist in order, re-testing after each group of changes so you can see what actually helped. Fix images first — they deliver the fastest visible improvement. Then move to caching and code, and only then consider hosting changes. Re-run the checklist every few months, or after adding new plugins, themes, or media, because speed drifts as a site grows.

## Frequently asked questions

### How fast should my website load?

Aim for the main content to appear in under 2.5 seconds on a mobile connection. That is also the threshold for a good Largest Contentful Paint score, one of Google's Core Web Vitals.

### Do I need a developer to use this checklist?

Most items — image compression, plugin cleanup, and caching plugins — need no coding at all. The code items can be handed to a developer as a short brief, or skipped until you have help.

### Will speed improvements help my search rankings?

Yes. Page experience is a confirmed ranking signal, and faster pages also reduce bounce rates and increase conversions, which compounds the benefit over time.
