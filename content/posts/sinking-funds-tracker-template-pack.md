Big expenses don't have to become emergencies. The car needs new tires, the annual insurance premium lands, the holidays arrive — and every year the same bills feel like surprises. Sinking funds fix that. A sinking fund is simply a planned savings bucket for a known future expense: you divide the cost by the months you have, save that amount monthly, and when the bill arrives, the money is already there. No credit card, no stress, no derailed budget.

This free template pack gives you four print-ready tools to run your sinking funds: a goal planner to list every fund and its monthly target, a monthly contribution tracker to log what you actually save, a per-fund progress sheet to watch each goal fill up, and a quick-start worksheet that calculates your numbers for you. Print them, use them in a binder, or copy the layouts into a spreadsheet. They work for any currency and any set of goals.

The Per-Fund Progress Sheet tracks a single fund filling up toward its goal with a visual progress bar. ## How to use this template pack

1. Start with Template 4 and list every predictable expense for the next 12 months — insurance, holidays, car maintenance, subscriptions, gifts.
2. Do the division math for each: total cost divided by months until due, plus a 10% buffer for the unpredictable ones.
3. Transfer the results into Template 1, your master planner, and check the total monthly commitment fits your budget.
4. Each month, log your actual deposits in Template 2 and update the per-fund progress in Template 3.
5. When a bill arrives, pay it from the fund — then immediately start a fresh fund for the next cycle of that expense.

The habit that makes sinking funds work is treating the monthly contribution like a bill. Move the money on payday, before you spend anything else, ideally with an automatic transfer to a separate savings account. And be honest in Template 2 when you fall short: a missed month isn't failure, it's data — adjust the timeline or the monthly amount and keep going. The goal isn't perfection, it's never being blindsided by a predictable expense again.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a sinking fund?**
A sinking fund is money you set aside each month for a known future expense — car repairs, holidays, insurance premiums, a new laptop. Instead of the bill surprising you, you save a little each month until the full amount is ready.

**How much should I put into each sinking fund monthly?**
Divide the total cost by the number of months until you need it. A $1,200 car-insurance bill due in 12 months needs $100/month. Add a 10% buffer for expenses that can run over, like home repairs.

**Where should I keep sinking fund money?**
In a separate savings account — ideally a high-yield one — so it doesn't mix with daily spending money. Some people use one account with a spreadsheet tracking each fund; others open sub-accounts per goal.
