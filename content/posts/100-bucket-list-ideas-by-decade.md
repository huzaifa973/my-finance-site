A bucket list is not really about the items — it is about refusing to let life happen by accident. The dreams we postpone have a way of quietly expiring: the trip we meant to take, the skill we meant to learn, the person we meant to visit. Writing them down changes the math. A dream on paper becomes a goal, and a goal gets a plan. This collection of 100 ideas is organized by decade, from your 20s through your 70s, because ambition looks different at 25 than at 65 — and every stage of life deserves its own kind of adventure.

Each decade has its own flavor. Your 20s are for bold experiments and cheap thrills: the years when you have more energy than money and everything is a first. Your 30s balance building a life with still chasing one — deeper travel, bigger skills, real mastery. Your 40s are for perspective: revisiting dreams with better resources, mentoring others, and choosing experiences over things. Your 50s bring the freedom of the kids growing up and careers peaking — time to go bigger. Your 60s and 70s are for savoring, legacy, and proving that adventure has no retirement age. Wherever you are, borrow freely from every decade — the list is inspiration, not a rulebook.

## How to use this list

1. Read through your current decade first, then steal your favorites from every other decade too.
2. Pick 10–15 items that genuinely excite you — a bucket list should thrill you, not guilt you.
3. Put one small item and one big item on this year's calendar with real dates.
4. Share your list with a friend or partner — shared dreams get planned, private ones get postponed.
5. Review the list every New Year: check off wins, retire what no longer fits, and add new dreams.

The items are the excuse; the point is a life you chose on purpose. Start with one this month.

## Frequently asked questions

**Should a bucket list include small, easy items?**
Absolutely — in fact, it should. Big dreams take years, and small wins ("watch a sunrise from a mountaintop," "learn to make perfect pancakes") keep the list alive and checkable while the big ones brew. A list with only moonshots feels impossible; a mix feels like a life.

**How do I afford the expensive bucket list items?**
Treat the big ones like any major goal: price them, divide by months, and save a little automatically. Many "expensive" dreams are cheaper than assumed when planned a year out — and the planning itself becomes part of the adventure.
