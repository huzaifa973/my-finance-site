## MOV vs MP4: what actually matters

**MOV** is Apple's container format — the default for iPhone recordings and many Mac apps. It can hold excellent quality video, but compatibility outside Apple's ecosystem is spotty: some Windows players choke on it, some websites reject it, and some Android apps refuse it.

**MP4** is the world standard. Every browser, phone, TV, social platform, and editing app on earth plays MP4. YouTube, Instagram, TikTok, and every client you'll ever send a video to expect MP4.

The video quality inside can be identical — this is about the wrapper, not the contents. Converting is a compatibility fix, not a quality downgrade.

## MOV, MP4, and HEVC: what's inside the file

The container (MOV or MP4) is only the wrapper — the codec inside does the real work:

**H.264 (AVC)** is the universal video codec. Nearly every device made in the last 15 years plays it. When converting for maximum compatibility, H.264 video in an MP4 container is the safe choice.

**HEVC (H.265)** is newer and about 50% more efficient — same quality at half the file size. iPhones record HEVC by default in many modes. The catch: older devices and some browsers can't play it, which is a common reason "MP4" files still won't play somewhere.

**ProRes** is Apple's professional editing codec, found in some MOV files from cameras and Macs. It looks gorgeous and the files are enormous — always convert these to H.264 MP4 before sharing.

If your converted MP4 still won't play somewhere, the codec is the likely culprit — re-encoding to H.264 fixes it.

## Troubleshooting conversion issues

**"The file is too big."** Browser converters handle large files, but very long 4K videos need patience and a desktop browser. Close other tabs to free up memory.

**"The converted file has no sound."** The source likely used an unusual audio codec. Re-exporting the source from your phone or camera app in a standard format usually fixes it.

**"It still won't play."** Check the codec, not the container — an MP4 with HEVC video won't play on older devices. Convert again targeting H.264.

**"The quality looks worse."** Make sure the converter isn't downscaling resolution or bitrate. A proper conversion preserves the original stream untouched.
