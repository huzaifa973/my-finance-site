Internal links are the cheapest ranking lever most sites ignore. Crawlers follow them to discover new pages, and every link quietly moves authority from pages that have it to pages that need it. Yet on most blogs, internal linking is improvised — a link remembered here, a "click here" there, and a graveyard of orphan pages Google barely visits. This free internal linking checklist template replaces improvisation with a system: 25 copy-and-paste checks split into three routines. Run the per-article checks every time you publish, the structure checks quarterly, and the maintenance checks monthly. Bloggers, niche-site builders, and in-house SEOs get a repeatable workflow that turns the site's link graph into a genuine ranking asset instead of an afterthought.

## What's inside this checklist

The template is organized the way real SEO work happens. Per-article checks (12) cover what to do at publish time: minimum link counts, anchor-text rules, pillar-page connections, and technical traps like nofollow attributes and redirect chains. Site-wide structure checks (8) keep the big picture healthy: orphan-page sweeps, click-depth audits, and making sure cornerstone pages sit close to the homepage. Maintenance checks (5) are the monthly hygiene that prevents link rot. Every item is written as a concrete, checkable action — not vague advice — so a VA or junior writer can run the same list you would, with the same results.

## How to use this checklist

1. Copy the full checklist into a doc or print it — the download at the bottom has the clean text version.
2. On every publish, work the 12 per-article checks into your editing pass before you hit the button.
3. Within 48 hours of publishing, add at least one inbound link from an existing related page.
4. Quarterly, run the 8 structure checks across the whole site and log every fix in a spreadsheet.
5. Monthly, sweep for broken internal links and flatten any redirect chains you find.

The checklist compounds. The first audit usually surfaces a dozen orphan pages and a handful of buried cornerstone pages; fixing those is often the fastest ranking win a site can get without writing a single word of new content.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many internal links should a blog post have?**
A practical rule is 3 to 10 internal links per 1,000 words. More important than the number is relevance: every link should help the reader and connect genuinely related pages, not pad a quota.

**What is an orphan page in SEO?**
An orphan page is a published page with no internal links pointing to it. Search engines discover pages by following links, so orphan pages get crawled rarely and accumulate no authority. The quarterly structure check exists specifically to keep that count at zero.

**Should internal links open in a new tab?**
No. New tabs are for external links you do not want to interrupt the session for. Internal links should open in the same tab so readers flow naturally through your site and crawlers follow a clean path.
