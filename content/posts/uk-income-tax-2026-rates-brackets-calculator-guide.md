UK income tax looks simple until your first payslip arrives and the numbers do not match what you expected. The confusion usually comes from one misunderstanding: **you do not pay your top rate on your whole income** — only on the slice of it that falls inside each band. Once that clicks, the whole system makes sense.

This guide covers the 2026/27 UK income tax bands, the personal allowance (and its sneaky taper), how Scotland differs, and how to estimate your own bill in seconds.

## The 2026/27 UK income tax bands

For England, Wales, and Northern Ireland, the 2026/27 tax year (6 April 2026 – 5 April 2027) looks like this:

| Band | Taxable income | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Personal allowance | £0 – £12,570 | 0% |
| Basic rate | £12,571 – £50,270 | 20% |
| Higher rate | £50,271 – £125,140 | 40% |
| Additional rate | Over £125,140 | 45% |

Every threshold is currently **frozen until April 2031**. That freeze matters: as wages rise with inflation but bands stay put, more people drift into higher bands each year without feeling richer — economists call this *fiscal drag*.

**A worked example:** earn £60,000 and you do *not* pay 40% on £60,000. You pay 0% on the first £12,570, 20% on the next £37,700 (£12,571–£50,270), and 40% only on the final £9,730 above £50,270. Total income tax: about £11,432 — an effective rate of roughly 19%, not 40%.

## The personal allowance and the £100k trap

The **personal allowance** (£12,570) is the amount you earn tax-free. But it has a tripwire: once your adjusted net income passes **£100,000**, the allowance shrinks by £1 for every £2 you earn above it, vanishing entirely at £125,140.

This creates the famous **60% effective rate** between £100,000 and £125,140 — you pay 40% higher-rate tax *plus* lose tax-free allowance at the same time, equivalent to another 20%. It is the most expensive income stretch in the UK tax system, and exactly why pension contributions (which reduce adjusted net income) are so valuable in that range.

## Scotland: different bands entirely

Scotland sets its own income tax bands, and they are more granular:

| Band | Income | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Personal allowance | Up to £12,570 | 0% |
| Starter | £12,571 – £16,537 | 19% |
| Basic | £16,538 – £29,526 | 20% |
| Intermediate | £29,527 – £43,662 | 21% |
| Higher | £43,663 – £75,000 | 42% |
| Advanced | £75,001 – £125,140 | 45% |
| Top | Over £125,140 | 48% |

Which system applies depends on where your main home is, not where you work. Cross-border commuters should check their tax code starts with the right letter.

## Beyond income tax: the full deduction picture

Your payslip shows more than income tax:

- **National Insurance** — a separate payroll tax with its own thresholds and rates. It is often the bigger surprise for new earners.
- **Student loan repayments** — 9% of earnings above the repayment threshold for Plan 2 loans, deducted like a tax but technically a loan repayment.
- **Pension contributions** — usually deducted *before* tax is calculated, which is why they are the single best lever for reducing your bill.

A £50,000 salary can easily lose 30%+ to the combination of these. Always budget on take-home pay, never gross.

## Common UK tax mistakes

- **Wrong tax code.** An emergency or outdated tax code (check your payslip) can over- or under-deduct for months. HMRC's personal tax account shows your current code.
- **Ignoring the £100k taper.** Earning £100,001 can cost you far more than £1 in extra tax. If you are near the line, pension contributions or Gift Aid donations can pull you back under.
- **Forgetting savings and dividend allowances.** The first £1,000 of savings interest (£500 for higher-rate taxpayers) and £500 of dividends are tax-free on top of the personal allowance.
- **Not filing when required.** Employees usually need no tax return, but side income over £1,000, rental income, or high-earner child benefit charges can trigger Self Assessment.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the UK income tax rates for 2026/27?
For England, Wales and Northern Ireland: 0% on the first £12,570 (personal allowance), 20% basic rate to £50,270, 40% higher rate to £125,140, and 45% additional rate above that. Scotland has its own six-band system.

### How much can I earn before paying income tax in the UK?
£12,570 in 2026/27 — the personal allowance. Note it tapers away by £1 for every £2 earned above £100,000, disappearing entirely at £125,140.

### Is UK income tax calculated on my whole salary?
No. The UK uses marginal bands: you pay 0% on the first £12,570, 20% only on the slice from £12,571 to £50,270, and so on. Only the portion inside each band is taxed at that band's rate.

### Are these figures final for 2026/27?
Thresholds are frozen until April 2031 per current policy, but Budgets can change rates. Treat any online calculator as an estimate and verify against HMRC before making financial decisions.
