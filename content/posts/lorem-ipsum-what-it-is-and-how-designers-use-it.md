Open any website mockup, app wireframe, or print layout in progress and you'll find the same strange "Latin" filling the text blocks: *Lorem ipsum dolor sit amet, consectetur adipiscing elit…* It's not real Latin, it doesn't mean anything useful — and it's one of the most practical tools in design. Here's what it is, where it came from, and how to use it properly.

## What is lorem ipsum?

**Lorem ipsum is placeholder text** — dummy copy inserted into a design to show where real text will go before that copy exists or is finalized. It mimics the *shape* of real prose (word lengths, sentence rhythm, paragraph flow) so a layout can be judged honestly: does the headline hierarchy work, is the line length readable, does the page feel balanced?

The standard passage starts *"Lorem ipsum dolor sit amet…"* and most designers recognize it on sight. It's deliberately scrambled: close enough to Latin to look like language, broken enough that nobody tries to read it. That tension is the whole point.

## A 2,000-year history in 60 seconds

Lorem ipsum's ancestor is **Cicero's *De Finibus Bonorum et Malorum*** ("On the Ends of Good and Evil"), written in 45 BC. In the 1500s, an unknown typesetter making a type-specimen book scrambled passages of Cicero to demonstrate fonts — *lorem ipsum* is a corrupted fragment of *"dolorem ipsum"* ("pain itself").

It then rode every wave of publishing technology: **Letraset** dry-transfer sheets popularized it with ad agencies in the 1960s, **Aldus PageMaker** bundled it as a template in the 1980s, and the web era turned it into a cliché — and a standard. Two millennia after Cicero, his words (sort of) still fill mockups worldwide.

## Why designers use it instead of real copy

This is the part clients question most: *"Why not just write the real text?"* Three reasons:

1. **It prevents premature word-critique.** Show a stakeholder a layout with real draft copy and they'll edit the sentences instead of reviewing the design. Placeholder text forces the conversation to stay on layout, typography, and hierarchy.
2. **It simulates realistic volume.** Designers know roughly how much text a section needs. Lorem ipsum fills that space so spacing, image placement, and page rhythm can be judged before the copywriter delivers.
3. **It's language-neutral.** For international projects, real copy in one language misleads — German runs long, Chinese runs compact. Placeholder text (or localized variants) keeps the layout honest across languages.

## When NOT to use lorem ipsum

Placeholder text is a tool, not a crutch. Don't use it when:

- **Content drives the design.** For a landing page whose headline *is* the product, designing around dummy text produces generic layouts. Write the copy first.
- **It's a client deliverable.** Nothing says "unfinished" like lorem ipsum in a final presentation. Replace it before anything leaves your hands — clients have accidentally shipped it to production more than once.
- **Accessibility testing matters.** Screen readers will cheerfully read scrambled Latin aloud. Test with real copy.

A good rule: lorem ipsum belongs in **wireframes and early mockups**, never in anything public.

## Alternatives to classic lorem ipsum

- **Real draft copy** — best when the message shapes the layout.
- **Content-first frameworks** — write headlines and key sentences first, then design around them.
- **Localized placeholder** — for multilingual projects, some teams use scrambled text in the target language's script to test line-breaking and font support honestly.
- **AI-generated draft text** — modern tools can produce plausible draft copy instantly; treat it as a *better placeholder*, not final copy.
