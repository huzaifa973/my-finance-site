A rambling, improvised intro makes even great content sound amateur, while a tight, confident opening tells the listener exactly what the show is, why it matters, and what is coming in this episode. The problem is that writing a fresh intro for every episode is exhausting — and staring at a blank page before recording kills momentum.

## How to use this pack

1. Download the full pack using the button below and find the group that matches your show style.
2. Pick a template and fill in every [BRACKET] with your show details.
3. Read the finished script aloud and adjust any line that feels unnatural to say.
4. Time yourself — aim for 30 to 60 seconds at a natural speaking pace.
5. Record the intro separately so you can reuse the show-identifying lines each episode.
6. Update only the episode-specific lines from then on to keep recording fast.

The brackets are your customization checklist: show name, your name, tagline, episode topic, and guest details. Once you have filled in the permanent parts, save that version as your master intro and only rewrite the episode hook each week. Reading the script aloud before recording is the step most people skip — it catches awkward phrasing and helps you sound confident instead of rehearsed.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long should a podcast intro be?**
Most effective intros run 30 to 60 seconds — long enough to state the show name, hook the listener, and preview the episode, but short enough that nobody hits skip. The templates here are written to land in that range when read aloud.

**Should I use the same intro for every episode?**
Keep the show-identifying lines consistent so listeners recognize you instantly, but customize the episode-specific lines every time. A fixed opening plus a fresh episode hook is the format most successful shows use.
