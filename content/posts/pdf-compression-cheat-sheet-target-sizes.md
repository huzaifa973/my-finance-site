## How to use this pack

1. Find your scenario in the cheat sheet and note its target size.
2. Apply the recommended DPI, JPEG quality, and color settings in your PDF tool or scanner app.
3. Compress once, then check the actual file size — if you missed the target, lower JPEG quality by 10 points before touching DPI.
4. Open the compressed PDF and zoom to 100 percent: text must stay crisp and photos recognizable.
5. If the file is still too large after two passes, split it into parts instead of degrading it further.

Keep the downloaded cheat sheet next to your scanner or PDF tool. After two or three uses you will know your three most common targets by heart, and uploads will stop bouncing.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why does my scanned PDF end up so much larger than expected?**
Scanners default to 300 or 600 DPI in full color, which is print quality. A ten-page color scan at 600 DPI can exceed 50 MB. Dropping to 150 DPI grayscale for text pages usually brings the same document under 5 MB with no visible difference on screen.

**Should I compress or use a cloud link for large PDFs?**
Compress when the recipient expects an attachment, like visa officers or courts. Use a cloud link when the file is a portfolio, ebook, or anything over 25 MB that does not need to live inside an email. The cheat sheet targets the attachment cases.
