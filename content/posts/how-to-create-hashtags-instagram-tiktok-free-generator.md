You've posted the perfect photo or video — and then comes the awkward part: staring at the caption box wondering which hashtags to add. Most people either skip them, copy-paste the same 30 generic tags every time, or use #love #instagood #photooftheday and wonder why nothing happens. Hashtags still work in 2026, but the strategy has changed. Here's how to create hashtags that actually get you discovered — plus a free tool that does the tedious part.

## How hashtags work now (not in 2019)

The old game was volume: stuff 30 tags, rank on a few hashtag pages, collect likes. That game is over. Today's algorithms use hashtags primarily as **categorization signals** — they tell Instagram and TikTok what your content is about so it can be matched to interested viewers.

That means relevance beats reach. A niche tag like #sourdoughstarter (50k posts) will put your bread video in front of bread people; #food (500M posts) puts it in front of nobody, because you're competing with half a billion posts for a signal the algorithm barely weighs.

## The hashtag formula that works

Build every hashtag set in three tiers:

1. **Niche tags (2–4):** Specific to your exact topic and community. Under 500k posts each. Examples: #lofibedroomproducer, #vanlifediy, #budgetmealprep. These are where small accounts actually get discovered.
2. **Mid-size tags (2–4):** Your broader category, 500k–5M posts. Examples: #homebaking, #indiemusic, #personalfinance. Competitive but winnable.
3. **Broad tags (1–2):** The big category, 5M+ posts. Examples: #baking, #fitness. Include one or two for categorization, but expect zero direct traffic from them.

**Total: 5–10 hashtags.** Instagram's own creator guidance suggests 3–5; TikTok similarly rewards a tight, relevant set. Quality and specificity win.

## How to research hashtags manually (when you have 10 minutes)

Generators are fast, but knowing the manual method helps you judge quality:

1. **Start with a seed keyword.** Type your topic into Instagram or TikTok search and open the Tags tab. Note the post count on each suggestion.
2. **Check the 50k–500k sweet spot.** Tags in this range have active audiences but beatable competition. Tap a tag and scroll: if the top posts have engagement levels similar to yours, you can compete there.
3. **Steal (ethically) from competitors.** Find 3–5 accounts your size in your niche with good reach. Note which tags repeat across their best posts — those are proven in your exact lane.
4. **Test in batches.** Use one tag set for a week, another the next. Compare reach-from-hashtags in your analytics. Keep a simple spreadsheet of winners.

Do this once per content pillar and you'll build a personal tag library that beats any generic list.

## Platform-specific hashtag rules

**Instagram (2026):**
- 3–5 hashtags is the official recommendation; up to ~15 is fine in practice
- Hashtags work in Reels, feed posts, and Stories (Stories: keep to 1–3, or hide them behind stickers)
- Keyword-rich captions now do heavy lifting — write naturally about your topic *and* tag it
- Banned or "broken" tags (flagged for spam) can suppress reach — if a tag's page shows limited results, drop it

**TikTok (2026):**
- 3–5 relevant tags per video; the algorithm reads your caption text and audio, so hashtags are supporting signals
- Trending challenge tags (#duet, branded challenge tags) can spike discovery when genuinely relevant
- SEO-style captions matter more here than on Instagram — write what people would search

## Hashtag mistakes killing your reach

- **Copy-pasting the same block every post.** Vary at least half your tags each time.
- **Only using mega-tags.** #love has over 2 billion posts. You will never rank there. One niche tag beats ten mega-tags.
- **Irrelevant trending tags.** Jumping on a trending tag unrelated to your content annoys viewers and trains the algorithm to miscategorize you.
- **Ignoring your analytics.** Check which posts got Explore/For-You traffic and note their tag mix. Double down on what worked.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many hashtags should I use on Instagram in 2026?**
Instagram officially recommends 3–5 highly relevant hashtags per post. Using 8–15 is still common and safe; avoid stuffing 30 loosely related tags, which can look spammy and dilute relevance.

**How many hashtags should I use on TikTok?**
TikTok's own guidance suggests a mix of broad and niche tags — 3–5 per video works well. The caption's keywords matter more than hashtag count, so prioritize relevance over volume.

**Do hashtags still work in 2026?**
Yes, but their role has shifted. Hashtags now categorize your content for the algorithm more than they drive browse traffic. Relevant, specific hashtags still boost discovery — generic mega-tags like #love do almost nothing.

**Should I put hashtags in the caption or comments?**
It makes no algorithmic difference on Instagram. Caption placement is simpler and keeps everything in one place; first-comment placement keeps captions cleaner visually. Pick whichever you prefer and stay consistent.
