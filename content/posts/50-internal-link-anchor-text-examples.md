Internal links are how readers discover your best content and how search engines understand what each page is about — but most sites waste them on vague phrases like 'click here' or 'read more'. Descriptive anchor text tells both readers and search engines exactly what waits on the other side, which earns more clicks and stronger rankings for the linked page. These fifty anchor text examples show bloggers, SEOs, and site owners how to write links that feel natural in a sentence while still carrying real meaning. Every example is built around a realistic target page, so you can see how the same link can be phrased five different ways depending on context.

## What's inside this resource

The pack covers ten common target pages — guides, tutorials, explainers, and buying guides — with five anchor styles each: the full-walkthrough invitation, the beginner pointer, the step-by-step promise, the simple see-also reference, and the deeper-dive suggestion. That variety matters because repeating identical anchor text across your site looks mechanical and misses the chance to match each linking sentence to its context. Use exact-match phrases sparingly and descriptive, sentence-fitting phrases most of the time; the goal is a link a reader would actually want to click.

## How to use this resource

1. Find the pages on your site that deserve more traffic, and list the three most natural phrases a reader would use to describe each one.
2. Pick an anchor style that fits the sentence you are writing in — never force a keyword phrase into a sentence where it sounds wrong.
3. Link from relevant, high-traffic pages first, since a link from a page nobody visits helps nobody.
4. Vary your anchor text across links to the same page, using descriptive phrases rather than repeating one exact phrase everywhere.
5. Audit your links twice a year: fix broken targets, update anchors for rewritten pages, and add links from new posts to your best older content.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is good anchor text for internal links?

Descriptive text that tells the reader what the linked page covers, written to fit naturally in its sentence. It should mention the topic, not just say 'click here', and it should match the destination page's actual content.

### Is exact-match anchor text bad for internal links?

Not in small amounts, but overusing one exact phrase looks unnatural and can appear manipulative. Mix exact phrases with partial-match and descriptive variations for a healthy, natural-looking link profile.

### How many internal links should a post have?

There is no fixed number — link wherever a reader would genuinely benefit from related content. For a typical 1,000-word post, three to eight relevant internal links is a sensible range.
