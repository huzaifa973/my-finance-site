Maine keeps things refreshingly simple: just **three tax brackets**, a standard deduction, and a personal exemption. No nine-bracket maze like New York, no local income taxes to stack on top. If you understand how the three rates layer onto your income, you can estimate your Maine tax in minutes.

This is an educational overview of how the math works — not personalized tax advice. For your specific situation, check Maine Revenue Services or a tax professional.

## Maine's 2026 Tax Rates at a Glance

Maine uses **marginal brackets**: each rate applies only to the income inside its bracket, never to your whole income. The 2026 inflation-adjusted schedules (published by Maine Revenue Services on September 15, 2025) are:

**Single filers (and married filing separately):**

| Maine taxable income | Rate | How the tax is figured |
|---|---|---|
| Up to $27,400 | 5.80% | 5.8% of taxable income |
| $27,400 – $64,850 | 6.75% | $1,589 + 6.75% of the amount over $27,400 |
| Over $64,850 | 7.15% | $4,117 + 7.15% of the amount over $64,850 |

**Heads of household:**

| Maine taxable income | Rate | How the tax is figured |
|---|---|---|
| Up to $41,100 | 5.80% | 5.8% of taxable income |
| $41,100 – $97,300 | 6.75% | $2,384 + 6.75% of the amount over $41,100 |
| Over $97,300 | 7.15% | $6,178 + 7.15% of the amount over $97,300 |

**Married filing jointly (and surviving spouses):**

| Maine taxable income | Rate | How the tax is figured |
|---|---|---|
| Up to $54,850 | 5.80% | 5.8% of taxable income |
| $54,850 – $129,750 | 6.75% | $3,181 + 6.75% of the amount over $54,850 |
| Over $129,750 | 7.15% | $8,237 + 7.15% of the amount over $129,750 |

## Standard Deduction and Personal Exemption for 2026

Before the brackets touch your income, two subtractions happen:

- **Standard deduction:** $15,300 (single), $30,600 (married filing jointly), $22,950 (head of household). These phase out once Maine income exceeds $102,250 (single) or $204,550 (joint).
- **Personal exemption:** $5,300 per taxpayer — and another $5,300 for a spouse on a joint return.

So a single filer with no itemized deductions shields **$20,600** ($15,300 + $5,300) from Maine tax before the 5.80% rate even starts.

## A Worked Example: How the Math Actually Works

Meet a single filer earning **$60,000** in Maine wages for 2026, taking the standard deduction:

1. **Start with income:** $60,000
2. **Subtract standard deduction:** $60,000 − $15,300 = $44,700
3. **Subtract personal exemption:** $44,700 − $5,300 = **$39,400** of Maine taxable income
4. **Apply the brackets:**
   - First $27,400 at 5.80% = **$1,589.20**
   - Remaining $12,000 ($39,400 − $27,400) at 6.75% = **$810.00**
5. **Total Maine tax: $2,399.20**

Effective rate: $2,399.20 ÷ $60,000 ≈ **4.0%** — well below the 6.75% top marginal rate this filer touches. That's the power of marginal brackets: the higher rate only bites the dollars above the threshold.

## New for 2026: The 2% High-Earner Surcharge

One change to know: Maine enacted a **2% income tax surcharge** effective for tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2026. It applies to Maine taxable income **over $1 million** for single filers and **over $1.5 million** for heads of household, joint filers, and surviving spouses (thresholds indexed for inflation). For the vast majority of filers this changes nothing — but if you're in seven-figure territory, factor it in.

## How Maine Compares to Its Neighbors

New England is a patchwork of tax philosophies, and Maine sits in the middle:

| State | Income tax |
|---|---|
| **Maine** | 5.80%–7.15% graduated |
| New Hampshire | No tax on wages (interest & dividends tax repealed) |
| Massachusetts | 5% flat (+4% surtax over $1M) |
| Vermont | 3.35%–7.6% graduated |

Maine's top rate of 7.15% is lower than Vermont's 7.6% but the brackets kick in earlier — Maine's top bracket starts at just $64,850 single, while Vermont's top rate needs much higher income. Compared to New Hampshire's zero wage tax next door, Maine looks pricey — which is exactly why cross-border workers pay attention to where they earn vs. where they live.

## Filing Basics: Deadlines and Where to File

Maine's individual income tax return (Form 1040ME) follows the federal calendar: **April 15** for the prior tax year, with extensions available. Maine Revenue Services runs an online filing portal (the Maine Tax Portal) where most residents can file and pay directly, and it accepts the federal extension automatically — though an extension to *file* is not an extension to *pay*.

If you moved into or out of Maine mid-year, you'll file as a part-year resident and Maine taxes only the income earned while you were a resident (plus any Maine-source income). Keep records of your move date — MRS does ask.

## Credits Worth Knowing About

Beyond the deduction and exemption, a few Maine credits meaningfully lower real bills:

- **Property Tax Fairness Credit** — a refundable credit for qualifying residents whose property tax (or rent equivalent) exceeds a threshold relative to income. This is real money back for many homeowners and renters.
- **Earned Income Tax Credit** — Maine matches a percentage of the federal EITC, boosting refunds for lower-income working filers.
- **Education Opportunity Tax Credit** — for graduates of Maine schools working in Maine, this credit can offset student loan payments against Maine tax — one of the more generous state-level student loan benefits in the country.

## Common Maine Tax Questions, Answered Briefly

- **Is Social Security taxed in Maine?** Maine offers a pension/retirement income deduction that phases in — worth checking if you're retired, as the rules have been expanding.
- **Property taxes?** Maine has no state-level property tax; towns assess it. The state offers property tax fairness credits for qualifying residents.
- **Do I owe tax if I work in Maine but live elsewhere?** Generally yes on Maine-source income — nonresidents file and pay on income earned in Maine.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are Maine's income tax brackets for 2026?

Maine uses three graduated rates for 2026: 5.80%, 6.75%, and 7.15%. For single filers, 5.80% applies up to $27,400, 6.75% from $27,400 to $64,850, and 7.15% above $64,850. Joint filers get roughly doubled thresholds ($54,850 and $129,750).

### What is Maine's standard deduction for 2026?

$15,300 for single filers, $30,600 for married couples filing jointly, and $22,950 for heads of household. It phases out above $102,250 (single) or $204,550 (joint) of Maine income.

### Does Maine have a personal exemption?

Yes — $5,300 per taxpayer for 2026 (and another $5,300 for a spouse on a joint return), per Maine Revenue Services' inflation-adjusted 2026 schedules.
