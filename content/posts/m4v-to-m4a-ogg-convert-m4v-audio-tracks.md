**M4V** is Apple's video container — you'll run into it with iTunes downloads, Apple TV content, and some screen recordings. But what if you only want the *audio* from an M4V file? Maybe it's a music video, a podcast-style recording, or a lecture you want to listen to on the go.

Here's everything you need to know about converting M4V to M4A or OGG, including the one big caveat that trips people up.

## M4V vs M4A vs OGG: What's the Difference?

- **M4V** — a video container based on the same technology as MP4. It holds both video and audio tracks. Some M4V files are DRM-protected (more on that below).
- **M4A** — the audio-only sibling. The "V" becomes "A": video is dropped, audio stays. Most M4V audio tracks are AAC, and M4A is AAC's natural home, so extraction is clean and nearly lossless.
- **OGG** — an open, patent-free audio format (usually Vorbis). Not an Apple format, but a great choice if you want open-source compatibility or slightly smaller files.

The relationship is simple: M4V and M4A are essentially the same container technology, one holding video plus audio, the other holding audio only. Extracting the audio from an M4V is really just *removing the video track*.

## The DRM Caveat (Read This First)

Some M4V files — specifically older **iTunes Store purchases** — carry Apple's **FairPlay DRM** copy protection. No free online converter can (or should) strip DRM from these files. If your M4V came from an iTunes purchase, conversion tools will refuse it.

The good news: most M4V files people actually need to convert are **not** DRM-protected — things like screen recordings, video exports, podcast recordings, and personal footage. Those convert perfectly.

## Choosing M4A vs OGG

Here's a quick decision guide:

| | M4A | OGG |
|---|---|---|
| Best for | Apple devices, phones, general use | Open-source apps, gaming, web |
| Compatibility | Excellent — iPhone, Android, Windows, cars | Good — most players, not native on iOS |
| Quality | AAC, same as the source (near-lossless extraction) | Re-encoded to Vorbis, still very good |
| File size | Slightly larger | Slightly smaller |

**Rule of thumb:** if you'll play the audio on a phone or in a car, choose **M4A**. If you live in the open-source world or publish to the web, **OGG** is the natural pick.

## Is the Conversion Lossless?

In most cases, **yes for M4A**. The audio inside an M4V is typically AAC, and M4A is just AAC in a different wrapper — so the converter can copy the audio stream directly without re-encoding. You get bit-for-bit the same audio that was in the video.

Converting to OGG involves re-encoding (AAC → Vorbis), which technically loses a tiny amount of quality — but at normal settings, the difference is inaudible.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is an M4V file?
M4V is Apple's video container format, closely related to MP4. Some M4V files from the iTunes Store use DRM copy protection, while others — like screen recordings or exports — are DRM-free.

### Can I convert M4V to M4A online?
Yes, if the M4V file is not DRM-protected. Upload it to a converter, extract the audio track, and download the M4A. DRM-locked iTunes purchases will not convert.

### Should I choose M4A or OGG for the extracted audio?
Choose M4A for Apple devices and broad compatibility, since it keeps AAC quality. Choose OGG for an open, patent-free format with slightly smaller file sizes.

### Is M4V to M4A conversion lossless?
Usually yes — the audio track inside most M4V files is already AAC, so extracting it to M4A copies the audio without re-encoding, preserving the original quality.
