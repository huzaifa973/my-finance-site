AI won't pay your bills for you. But it will do the tedious financial thinking most people avoid: building a budget from scratch, comparing debt payoff strategies, explaining confusing money topics in plain English, and sanity-checking big decisions before you make them. And the best tools for all of this are free.

Here are five free AI tools, what each one is actually good at, and the exact prompts to start with.

## 1. ChatGPT — your free financial planning assistant

ChatGPT's free tier handles the core money tasks brilliantly: drafting budgets, running payoff scenarios, and explaining concepts. Its strength is structured, step-by-step reasoning — ideal for anything involving numbers and trade-offs.

**Best for:** building a first budget, comparing debt strategies, drafting savings plans.

**Try this prompt:**
> "I take home $2,400 a month. My fixed costs are: rent $950, car insurance $130, phone $60, minimum debt payments $180. Help me build a zero-based budget that includes $150/month savings and tell me exactly how much is left for groceries, transport, and personal spending."

Follow up with "what would change if I cut $100 from personal spending?" and it recalculates instantly. That back-and-forth is where the value lives — it's like a patient financial coach that never judges your numbers.

## 2. Claude — best for analyzing your real documents

Claude's standout skill is reading long documents carefully. Paste in your credit card statement, a loan agreement, or an insurance policy summary, and ask it to flag fees, rate changes, and anything worth questioning.

**Best for:** decoding statements, loan terms, and fine print; summarizing long financial documents.

**Try this prompt:**
> "Here's my credit card statement summary [paste the transactions section]. Categorize my spending, tell me my top 3 spending categories, and flag any recurring charges or fees I should look into."

Claude is also excellent at the "explain like I'm 18" task — APR vs APY, how balance transfers work, what a 401(k) match actually means. If a money term confuses you, ask Claude before you Google; the explanation will be clearer and tailored to your situation.

**Privacy note for both tools:** use the free web versions, and never paste account numbers, full statements with personal details, or login credentials. Summaries and numbers are fine; identifiers are not.

## 3. Cleo — the AI that watches your money automatically

Cleo is different from the chatbots above: it's a money app with an AI assistant built in. Connect your bank accounts and Cleo categorizes spending, roasts you (affectionately) for bad habits, and answers questions like "how much did I spend on takeout last month?" or "can I afford to spend $200 this weekend?"

**Best for:** people who won't manually track spending but want someone — something — watching it.

The free tier covers budgeting, spending breakdowns, and the chat assistant. There's a paid tier with cash advances and deeper features, but the free version does the core job: making your spending visible without spreadsheets.

**Try asking Cleo:** "What's my biggest unnecessary expense this month?" Then act on whatever it says — the tool only works if you do.

## 4. Google Gemini — best for on-the-go money questions

Gemini's advantage is that it lives on your phone and understands context. Photograph a grocery receipt and ask "categorize this and add it to my weekly food spending" — it reads the image, extracts the items, and does the math. Snap a photo of a price tag and ask whether the "sale" is actually a good deal compared to the unit price.

**Best for:** receipt scanning, quick comparisons while shopping, voice questions on the move.

**Try this:** next time you're grocery shopping, ask "this cereal is $5.99 for 18oz and the store brand is $4.29 for 15oz — which is cheaper per ounce?" It's a small thing, but doing it consistently across a whole cart adds up to real savings.

## 5. Microsoft Copilot — the underrated spreadsheet helper

Copilot (free with a Microsoft account) shines inside Excel. Describe the tracker you want in plain English — "a monthly budget tracker with categories, actual vs planned columns, and a chart" — and it builds the spreadsheet structure for you. It can also write formulas you'd never remember, like debt payoff amortization schedules.

**Best for:** building custom budget and debt trackers in Excel without learning formulas.

**Try this prompt:**
> "Create an Excel table for tracking 3 credit card debts with columns for balance, APR, and minimum payment. Add a formula column showing estimated months to payoff at minimum payments."

Pair the result with our [debt snowball vs avalanche guide](/posts/debt-snowball-vs-avalanche/) and you'll have both the strategy and the tracker.

## How to use AI for money without getting burned

AI is a thinking aid, not a financial advisor. A few ground rules:

- **Verify numbers that matter.** AI can do arithmetic wrong. Double-check any payoff timeline or budget total before acting on it.
- **Never share credentials or account numbers.** No legitimate use case requires them.
- **Don't outsource judgment.** If AI suggests an aggressive debt payoff plan that leaves you with $0 buffer, push back — you know your life better than it does.
- **Use it monthly, not once.** The real power is the recurring check-in: paste each month's spending in and ask "what changed, and what should I fix?"

### FAQ

**Q:** Is it safe to share financial details with AI tools?

A: Share numbers and scenarios freely, but never share account numbers, passwords, Social Security numbers, or anything that identifies your accounts. Describe your situation in general terms — "I have $4,200 in credit card debt at 24% APR" is useful to the AI and useless to an attacker.

**Q:** Can AI actually help me save money?

A: Yes, mainly in three ways: building realistic budgets from your numbers, running debt-payoff scenarios you'd never calculate by hand, and spotting spending patterns when you paste in categorized expenses. It won't move money for you — the value is in planning and analysis.

**Q:** Which free AI tool is best for budgeting?

A: ChatGPT and Claude are the strongest free options for budgeting help because they handle multi-step math well and can work through your actual numbers conversationally. Cleo is better if you want an AI that connects to your accounts and watches spending automatically.

**Q:** Do I need to pay for premium AI to manage my finances?

A: No. The free tiers of ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Copilot are more than capable of budgeting, debt planning, and financial explanations. Paid tiers mainly buy you speed and higher usage limits, which a monthly money check-in doesn't need.
