## How to use this pack

1. At the start of the month, fill the Planned column for every line based on your expected income and bills.
2. As the month goes on, jot each actual amount in the Actual column — a few seconds per purchase is enough.
3. In section four, treat savings and extra debt payments as bills you owe yourself, not leftovers.
4. At month end, complete the summary: income minus expenses minus savings equals what is left over.
5. If the leftover is negative, scan the variable section first — that is where cuts hurt least.
6. Start a fresh sheet each month and compare totals to spot trends over time.

Do this three months in a row and the Planned column practically fills itself — you will know your real numbers instead of guessing them.

## Frequently asked questions

**Should savings come before or after expenses in the budget?**
Before. The worksheet lists savings and debt payoff as its own section so you pay yourself first, then live on what remains. People who budget savings last almost always find nothing left; people who budget it first adjust spending to fit.

**What if my income changes month to month?**
Fill the income section with your realistic expected total, then budget spending against a conservative estimate — base it on your lowest recent month. In good months, the surplus in the month-end summary goes straight to savings or debt instead of lifestyle creep.
