"What time is it in Tokyo right now?" It is the most-googled scheduling question on Earth — and the one most likely to go wrong. A free online world clock answers it in about five seconds, correctly, with daylight saving handled automatically.

Here is the fastest method and the workflows that make time zones painless.

## The 60-second world clock check

1. Open our free [world clock](/tools/world-clock/) in your browser — no signup, no app.
2. Type the city or country ("Tokyo", "Sydney", "Lagos").
3. Read the current local time, plus the UTC offset and whether daylight saving is active.

That is it. The tool pulls from the authoritative time zone database, so DST transitions, half-hour zones (India's UTC+5:30, Nepal's UTC+5:45), and one-zone countries (China) are all handled — no mental math, no "is London +4 or +5 this week?"

## Why guessing the offset fails

The Earth has 24 nominal time zones but **38+ actual UTC offsets** in use. Then daylight saving scrambles them on different dates: the US and Europe spring forward weeks apart, Australia's DST runs opposite to the northern hemisphere's, and huge regions (most of Asia, Africa) skip DST entirely.

So the New York–London gap is 5 hours in winter, 4 in summer — and briefly something stranger in the weeks when only one side has changed. Memorizing offsets is a losing game; a live world clock never is.

## Workflow 1: scheduling across zones

1. **Find overlap.** Put each attendee's city in the world clock side by side and look for shared 9-to-5 hours. The classic US–Europe–Asia overlap is a narrow 1–2 hour window — find it before proposing times.
2. **Propose in UTC or dual-zone.** "3pm UTC / 11am New York / 4pm London" eliminates the "whose 3pm?" failure mode.
3. **State the date for everyone.** A 9am Sydney meeting is 4pm the *previous day* in New York. Write the date per zone, not just the time.
4. **Recheck near DST transitions.** If the meeting is weeks out and a DST change falls in between, verify the offset again the week of.

## Workflow 2: travel

- **Before you fly:** check your destination's time to plan sleep. Flying east? Start shifting bedtime earlier 2–3 days ahead.
- **On arrival:** set your watch/phone to local time immediately and check the world clock for home — so you know when *not* to call.
- **Booking across zones:** flight times are always local to each airport. A world clock sanity-check ("depart 10pm, arrive 6am — whose time?") prevents missed-connection math errors.

## Workflow 3: remote teams

Keep a standing multi-city dashboard: your office, your clients, your freelancers. One glance tells you who is awake, who is at lunch, and who should not be Slacked at midnight. Teams that do this stop sending "sorry, what time is it there?" messages entirely.

## Pro tips

- **Bookmark your frequent cities.** Most world clocks let you pin cities — build your personal dashboard once.
- **Use UTC as the anchor.** UTC never observes DST. For anything recurring (standups, releases, webinars), schedule in UTC and let everyone convert.
- **Mind the date line.** Scheduling with Auckland or Fiji? You are often a full calendar day apart — the world clock shows the date, not just the time. Check it.
- **Half-hour zones exist.** India, Iran, Afghanistan, Myanmar, parts of Australia, and Nepal (UTC+5:45!) all break the whole-hour assumption.
