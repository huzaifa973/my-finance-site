Narrating your first audiobook is thrilling — and then you press record and hear every mouth click, room echo, and truck passing outside. Professional narration sounds effortless because it's engineered: the room is treated, the mic technique is consistent, and there's a ruthless editing pass before anyone hears a word. The good news is that none of this requires talent you don't have. It requires a checklist.

This 30-step checklist walks a complete beginner through the entire process: preparing your space and voice, setting recording levels, narrating with consistency, editing to platform standards, and doing final quality control. Follow it for your first chapter and you'll skip the months of trial-and-error most beginners suffer through. Each step includes a copy button so you can paste the workflow into your own project notes.

## What's inside this checklist

The checklist has 30 steps in five phases. Phase 1 covers your recording space: taming echo, killing background noise, and building a workable booth on any budget. Phase 2 is voice and mic preparation: warm-ups, hydration, mic placement, and setting levels correctly. Phase 3 is the narration session itself: pacing, character consistency, handling mistakes, and session stamina. Phase 4 covers editing: removing errors, evening out levels, and meeting ACX technical specs. Phase 5 is quality control: the final listen, file checks, and delivery standards. One clear action per step.

## How to use this checklist

1. Set up your space once using Phase 1, then leave the layout untouched for the whole project.
2. Before every session, run Phase 2: warm up, hydrate, position the mic, set levels, record room tone.
3. Narrate one chapter per session using Phase 3 habits — mark mistakes as you go instead of stopping.
4. Edit that chapter the same day or next using Phase 4, while the session sound is fresh in your ears.
5. Run the Phase 5 QC pass on every chapter before moving on — fixing chapter 3 in week six is miserable.

The beginner trap is recording the whole book before editing anything. Don't. Record chapter one, edit it fully, and run the ACX check on it — that single loop teaches you more than ten chapters of raw recording. You'll discover your mic was too hot, your room tone was missing, or your pacing was off, and you'll fix it for chapter two instead of chapter twenty. Slow is smooth, and smooth is fast.

## Frequently asked questions

**What equipment do I need to start narrating audiobooks?**
A decent USB condenser microphone, closed-back headphones, and a quiet room matter far more than an expensive setup. Many successful narrators started with a $100-150 USB mic and a closet full of clothes as a recording booth.

**What are ACX audio requirements?**
ACX (Audible's platform) requires MP3 or WAV files with specific standards: -23dB to -18dB RMS levels, peak values no higher than -3dB, noise floor no higher than -60dB RMS, and each file must open and close with room tone. Chapters must be separate files with consistent naming.

**How long does it take to narrate an audiobook?**
Plan on roughly 6 hours of total work per finished hour of audio: about 2 hours of recording and 4 hours of editing and proofing for beginners. A 10-hour audiobook is realistically a 60-hour project.
