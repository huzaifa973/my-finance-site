Your podcast intro is the handshake of your show: fifteen to thirty seconds that tell a first-time listener what this is, who you are, and why they should stay. Shows with tight, professional intros keep listeners; shows that mumble through a minute of "um, hey guys, welcome back" lose them.

The great news: you can make a broadcast-quality podcast intro for free, today, with just your phone and browser. Here's the full playbook.

## Plan your intro (5 minutes)

Decide the three elements every intro needs:

1. **The hook (3–5 seconds):** music bed or sound design that sets the mood.
2. **The identity (5–10 seconds):** show name + one-line premise in your own voice.
3. **The handoff (5–10 seconds):** today's topic or guest, then straight into the episode.

Write a script. Yes, actually write it down. Read it out loud three times. The difference between a scripted intro and an improvised one is the difference between a show and a hobby.

## Record your voice (10 minutes)

Record in a small, quiet room — a closet full of clothes is a classic free studio. Hold your phone 6 inches from your mouth and slightly off-axis to reduce plosives. Record three takes and pick the best.

Speak with 10% more energy than feels natural. Mics flatten energy, and a slightly-too-upbeat take sounds just right on playback.

## The intro formula that works

Open with energy, say the name, state the promise, tease the episode:

> "Welcome to Money Morning — the five-minute podcast that makes personal finance actually interesting. I'm Huzaifa, and today we're breaking down the one budget trick that finally worked for me."

Name. Premise. Hook. Thirty seconds, done.

## Mistakes to avoid

**The 60-second intro.** Your regular listeners hear it every episode. Keep it tight or they will skip — and skipping trains them to disengage before your content starts.

**Copyrighted music.** That popular song you love will get your episode flagged or pulled. Royalty-free libraries are enormous now; you will find something that fits.

**Inconsistent volume.** If your intro blasts and your episode whispers, listeners bail. Normalize your intro to the same loudness as your episodes.

**Never updating it.** Refresh your intro once or twice a year. Shows evolve, hosts improve, and a stale intro undersells a better show.

## Intro styles for different show formats

Not every podcast needs the same intro. Match the style to your format:

**Interview shows** work best with a short, energetic intro that names the show and teases today's guest — 15 seconds max. Listeners came for the guest, not the host's monologue.

**Solo/educational shows** can run slightly longer (20–30 seconds) because the intro doubles as a positioning statement: what you'll learn and why this host is worth your time.

**Storytelling/narrative shows** often use cold opens — 30 seconds of the most gripping tape — *before* a minimal title card. The hook comes first, branding second.

**Comedy shows** keep intros loose and short, sometimes just a tagline and a laugh. Overproducing a comedy intro kills the vibe.

The common thread: respect the repeat listener. Your intro plays hundreds of times for your biggest fans — every unnecessary second is a small tax on loyalty.

## Testing your intro before launch

**Play it for a stranger.** If they can't name your show and its premise after one listen, rewrite the script. Clarity beats cleverness.

**Listen on phone speakers.** Most podcast listening happens on phones and earbuds. If the music drowns your voice on a phone speaker, lower the bed.

**Live with it for a week.** Listen to your intro daily for seven days. Anything that annoys you on day seven will infuriate regular listeners by episode fifty — fix it now.
