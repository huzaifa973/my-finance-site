You recorded a lecture, saved an interview, or downloaded a music video — and now you realize you only need the **audio**. Carrying around a 500 MB video file to listen to a voice track is wasteful: it eats storage, drains battery, and won't play in your music app.

Converting video to audio is one of the most useful everyday file tricks, and you can do it free in your browser in about a minute. Here's the complete guide.

## When to Convert Video to Audio

If any of these sound familiar, extraction is the answer:

- **Lectures and webinars** — listen on a walk or at 1.5x speed without the video overhead.
- **Interviews and meetings** — audio is easier to transcribe, share, and archive.
- **Music and performances** — keep the song, drop the visuals, add it to a playlist.
- **Voice notes and vlogs** — repurpose spoken content for podcasts or audio archives.
- **Saving space** — an audio-only file is typically 5–10% of the original video's size.

## Picking the Right Audio Format

The format you choose depends on what you'll do with the audio:

- **M4A** — the best all-rounder. AAC audio with excellent compatibility across iPhones, Android, Windows, and cars. Ideal default choice.
- **MP3** — the universal standard. Plays on literally everything, including very old devices. Slightly less efficient than M4A at the same quality.
- **OGG** — open and patent-free, with slightly smaller files than MP3 at similar quality. Great for web use and open-source workflows.
- **WAV** — uncompressed and huge, but perfect if you'll edit the audio further and want zero quality loss.

**Quick decision:** everyday listening → **M4A** or **MP3**. Open-source/web → **OGG**. Editing/mastering → **WAV**.

## How to Convert Video to Audio Online: Step by Step

1. **Open a browser-based converter.** For example, the free [3GP to M4A converter](/tools/3gp-to-m4a-converter/) extracts audio from 3GP videos into the widely compatible M4A format — the same upload-and-download workflow applies to most video-to-audio tools.
2. **Upload your video file.** Click upload and select the file from your phone or computer.
3. **Choose your audio output format** using the guide above — M4A for compatibility, OGG for open formats, WAV for editing.
4. **Check quality settings if offered.** A bitrate of 128–192 kbps is plenty for voice; music deserves 192–256 kbps.
5. **Convert and download.** Save the audio file, ideally with a clear name like `lecture-2026-10-13.m4a`.

## Batch Tips: Converting Multiple Videos

If you have a folder of videos to process, a little organization saves headaches:

- **Convert from originals only.** Never convert an already-converted file — each lossy conversion shaves off quality. Always go back to the source video.
- **Keep a naming scheme.** `project-name-01.m4a`, `project-name-02.m4a` — future you will be grateful.
- **Process one at a time, steadily.** Most free online converters handle one file per run; work through your list in order and download each result before starting the next.
- **Check the first result before batching.** Convert one file, listen to it, confirm the format and quality are right — then repeat for the rest.

## Quality Settings: Bitrate Explained

**Bitrate** (measured in kbps) is the main quality dial:

- **64–96 kbps** — fine for spoken voice, tiny files.
- **128 kbps** — good all-round quality for voice and casual music.
- **192–256 kbps** — high quality for music; hard to distinguish from the original.
- **320 kbps** — maximum for MP3; overkill for most uses but harmless.

If your tool extracts without re-encoding (common when going from video to M4A), you keep the original audio quality automatically — no bitrate decision needed.

## Privacy: Files Never Leave Your Browser

Videos can be deeply personal — family footage, client interviews, unreleased work. Reputable modern converters do all the processing **client-side, in your browser**. Your video is never uploaded to a server, never stored, and never seen by anyone. When in doubt, check that the tool advertises client-side processing before you upload anything sensitive.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I convert a video to audio online?
Upload your video file to a browser-based converter, select an audio output format like M4A or OGG, and download the result. The file is processed on your device, so it stays private.

### Which audio format should I choose: MP3, M4A, OGG, or WAV?
Use M4A or MP3 for maximum compatibility with phones and players. Choose OGG for open-source use and smaller files, and WAV when you need uncompressed quality for editing.

### Will converting video to audio reduce quality?
Extracting to M4A or MP3 preserves the audio well, though converting between lossy formats can lose a little quality. Always convert from the original video file, not from an already-converted copy.

### Can I convert multiple videos to audio at once?
Many online converters handle one file at a time, so batch conversion means repeating the steps per file. Keep a consistent naming scheme to stay organized.
