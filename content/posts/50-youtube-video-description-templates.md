The description box under a YouTube video does quiet, heavy work: it tells the algorithm what your video is about, gives viewers the links and chapters they need, and turns a casual viewer into a subscriber. Yet most creators paste the same two lines under every upload and wonder why their videos stall. These fifty description templates give creators, educators, and small businesses ready-made openers for five common video types, each written to hook the viewer in the first two lines — the only part visible before the 'show more' click. Adapt the template, add your own links and chapters, and every upload starts working harder.

## What's inside this resource

The pack covers five video categories — tutorials, growth advice, travel, fitness, and music — with ten description openers each: the step-by-step promise, the welcome-back hook, the problem solver, the beginner's guarantee, the quick explainer, the mistake-avoider, the mastery promise, the real-example walkthrough, the question answerer, and the join-me invitation. Every template ends with the same practical footer pattern: chapters, resource links, and a subscribe reminder, so your descriptions stay consistent across uploads. Remember that the first 150 characters matter most, since that is what viewers and search see before expanding the box.

## How to use this resource

1. Pick the opener that matches your video type and the promise your video actually delivers.
2. Put your most important keywords and the core benefit in the first two lines, before the 'show more' fold.
3. Add timestamped chapters, links to everything you mention, and your social or website links below the opener.
4. End with one clear call to action — subscribe, comment, or watch the next video — not three competing asks.
5. Reuse the same footer structure on every video so regular viewers always know where to find your links.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should a YouTube description be?

Long enough to cover the topic, links, and chapters — typically 200 to 500 words for tutorial or educational videos. The first two lines matter most, but the full text helps YouTube understand and rank the video.

### Do video descriptions help with search rankings?

Yes. YouTube and Google read descriptions to understand a video's topic, so a keyword-rich, accurate description helps the video surface for relevant searches — though the title, thumbnail, and watch time matter more.

### Should I put links at the top of the description?

Only if the link is essential to the video, since links push your hook below the fold. Usually the first two lines should sell the video, with links and chapters following immediately after.
