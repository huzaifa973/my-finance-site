Your page can rank on the first page of Google and still get almost no traffic. The reason is usually sitting right under your title in the search results: a weak meta description. That short snippet is your page's sales pitch — the one piece of copy every searcher reads before deciding whether to click. In 2026, with AI overviews and rich results competing for attention, a sharp description matters more than ever.

## What a meta description is (and what it is not)

A meta description is the HTML meta tag that suggests the snippet Google shows beneath your page title in search results. It is not a direct ranking factor — Google has said this explicitly for years. Writing a brilliant description will not move you from position 8 to position 3.

What it does is control your **click-through rate (CTR)**. Two pages can sit side by side in the results, and the one with the clearer, more compelling description will win more of the clicks. Since you cannot always control your ranking, your description is the highest-leverage copy on the page: a small rewrite can lift traffic without a single new backlink. Google sometimes rewrites descriptions it finds weak — which is itself a signal that the original was not doing its job.

## Meta description length in 2026

Google does not publish a character limit, because it truncates by pixel width, not characters. In practice, the working guidelines for 2026 are:

| Context | Practical limit |
|---|---|
| Desktop results | ~155–160 characters |
| Mobile results | ~120 characters before truncation |
| Safe target for the full message | 150–160 characters |

The practical rule: put your keyword, benefit, and call to action inside the first 150 characters, and treat anything beyond 160 as likely to be cut. Our [meta description generator](/tools/meta-description-generator/) shows a live character count as you write, so you can see exactly where truncation would hit before you publish.

Length aside, every word has to earn its place. Descriptions that ramble past 160 characters get cut mid-sentence, which looks sloppy and wastes the impression. Descriptions under 100 characters often leave persuasive power on the table. The sweet spot is a complete, punchy pitch in 150–160 characters.

## How to write meta descriptions that get clicks

Follow this formula for nearly any page:

1. **Match the search intent.** A searcher typing "how to write meta descriptions" wants a guide, not a product pitch. Your description should promise exactly what the query asks for — guide, tool, price, answer — in the first few words.
2. **Use the target keyword naturally.** When the description contains the searcher's words, Google bolds them in the snippet, which draws the eye. Work the primary keyword in once, near the start, without stuffing.
3. **Add a specific benefit or differentiator.** "Free calculator, no sign-up" beats "best calculator." Numbers, specifics, and exclusions ("in 2026," "step-by-step," "7 brackets explained") tell the searcher why your result is the one.
4. **End with a call to action.** "Learn how," "Try it free," "See the full table" — a gentle imperative turns a passive summary into an invitation. It costs four words and consistently lifts clicks.
5. **Write for humans, then check the length.** Draft the pitch first, then trim to 150–160 characters. Cutting filler words ("very," "really," "in order to") usually gets you there without losing meaning.

Example for a tax guide: "Connecticut's 2026 income tax brackets and rates for every filing status, plus a worked example and a free calculator to estimate what you owe." That is 156 characters — keyword up front, specific benefits, and an implied call to action.

## 5 common meta description mistakes

1. **Duplicating descriptions across pages.** Reused snippets confuse searchers and waste the one chance to differentiate. Every indexable page deserves a unique description.
2. **Keyword stuffing.** Repeating the keyword three times looks spammy in the results and earns no ranking benefit. Once, naturally placed, is enough.
3. **Burying the benefit.** Leading with your brand name or a generic opener ("Welcome to our page about...") spends precious characters before the searcher learns why they should click.
4. **No call to action.** A description that merely summarizes gives the searcher no reason to choose your result over the nine others. End with an invitation.
5. **Ignoring truncation.** If the key message sits past character 160, most searchers never see it. Front-load the pitch and verify the length before publishing.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the ideal meta description length in 2026?
Aim for 150 to 160 characters. Google truncates descriptions around 155–160 characters on desktop and a bit earlier on mobile, so keep your keyword, benefit, and call to action inside that range.

### Do meta descriptions affect Google rankings?
No — meta descriptions are not a direct ranking factor. But a compelling description raises your click-through rate, and more clicks from the same ranking position means more traffic, which is the entire point of the exercise.

### How do you write a meta description that gets clicks?
Match the search intent, include the target keyword naturally near the start, add a specific benefit or differentiator, and end with a call to action — all within about 155 characters.
