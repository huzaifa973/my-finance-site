Arizona keeps income tax refreshingly simple: one flat rate, no brackets to climb, no marginal-rate math. For 2026 the rate is a flat 2.5% on every dollar of taxable income, whether you earn $30,000 or $300,000. That simplicity makes Arizona one of the easiest state taxes to estimate by hand — subtract your deductions, multiply by 2.5%, subtract your credits, done. This guide walks through the flat rate, the standard deductions, the $40-per-person family credit, a full worked example, and how to get an instant estimate with our free calculator.

## The flat 2.5% rate

Most states use progressive brackets where higher income faces higher rates. Arizona does not. Every dollar of Arizona taxable income is taxed at 2.5%, period. Your effective rate is also 2.5% of taxable income — there is no bracket math, no "only the income above the threshold" subtlety. The calculation is one multiplication.

The trade-off of flat taxes is worth knowing: because the rate never rises, high earners pay the same marginal rate as everyone else, while deductions and credits do relatively more work for lower-income filers. That is exactly why Arizona's standard deduction and family credit matter so much — they are the main tools that keep the tax manageable.

## Standard deductions: shrinking taxable income first

Before the 2.5% rate touches anything, you subtract deductions from your Arizona adjusted gross income. The standard deduction amounts used illustratively in this guide:

| Filing status | Illustrative standard deduction |
|---|---|
| Single | $15,750 |
| Married filing jointly | $31,500 |

You can itemize instead if your itemized deductions — mortgage interest, charitable gifts, state and local taxes up to the federal cap — exceed the standard amount. Most filers take the standard deduction because it is simpler and larger than their itemized total. Either way, this step happens *before* the rate applies, so every deducted dollar saves you 2.5 cents.

## The $40 family income tax credit

After computing the tax, Arizona offers a credit worth $40 per person: you, your spouse if filing jointly, and each dependent. A family of four gets $160 off their final tax bill. Credits are more powerful than deductions — a $40 credit cuts your tax by the full $40, while a $40 deduction would only save $1 at the 2.5% rate.

Claiming it is straightforward on the Arizona return, but it is the line filers most often forget, because it comes after the main calculation when attention is fading. Do not leave it behind.

## Worked example: $60,000 single filer

Follow the three steps with an illustrative example — a single filer with $60,000 of Arizona adjusted gross income:

**Step 1: Subtract the standard deduction.**
$60,000 − $15,750 = **$44,250** of taxable income.

**Step 2: Apply the flat rate.**
$44,250 × 2.5% = **$1,106.25** in tax.

**Step 3: Subtract the family credit.**
$1,106.25 − $40 = **$1,066.25** final Arizona tax.

That is the whole Arizona return in miniature: subtract, multiply by 2.5%, subtract the credit. A married couple filing jointly with two children would subtract the $31,500 standard deduction, apply 2.5%, then subtract $160 (four people × $40).

## Who has to file in Arizona

You generally need to file an Arizona return if your Arizona gross income exceeds the standard deduction threshold for your filing status — illustratively, $15,750 single or $31,500 joint — though part-year residents and nonresidents with Arizona-source income have their own rules. Arizona employers withhold state tax from paychecks throughout the year, so many filers discover they are due a refund rather than a bill. If you moved into or out of Arizona mid-year, you will prorate: Arizona taxes only the income earned while you were a resident, plus any Arizona-source income from outside periods. When in doubt, filing is cheap insurance against penalties.
