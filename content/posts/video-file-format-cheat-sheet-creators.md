Every creator has hit the same wall: a video that will not upload, plays without sound on a client's phone, or exports at ten times the needed size. The cause is almost always the format. This cheat sheet explains the twenty formats creators actually meet — MP4, MOV, WebM, ProRes, AV1, and the legacy formats to avoid — in plain language: what each one is for, where it plays, and when to choose it. It is written for YouTubers, course creators, editors, and social media managers.

## What's inside this resource

The sheet is organized by job, not by alphabet: universal delivery formats first (MP4 H.264 is the safe default for almost everything), then editing intermediates like ProRes and DNxHD, then web and streaming codecs, then the formats to leave behind. The rule of thumb: deliver in MP4 H.264 unless you have a specific reason not to, edit in ProRes or DNxHD, and never send a client an editing master when a delivery file will do.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best video format for YouTube?

MP4 with H.264 video and AAC audio. It uploads fast, plays everywhere, and YouTube re-encodes it anyway — so a clean H.264 master gives the best starting quality.

### Should I use H.265 or AV1?

H.265 gives smaller files for 4K and HDR, but older devices may struggle. AV1 compresses even better but encodes slowly. For most creators, H.264 remains the safe default.

### What is the difference between a codec and a container?

The container (MP4, MOV, MKV) is the wrapper; the codec (H.264, ProRes) is how the video inside is compressed. Both matter: the right codec in the wrong container can still fail to play.
