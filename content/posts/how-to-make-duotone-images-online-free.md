## What a duotone actually does

A duotone takes the grayscale version of your photo and maps the darkest pixels to one color and the lightest pixels to another, blending smoothly between the two. Black areas become the dark tone, white areas become the light tone, and every midtone lands somewhere on the spectrum between them.

## Tips for better duotone results

Start with contrast. A flat, gray photo produces a muddy duotone; bump the contrast first or pick a punchy photo. Portraits with strong side-lighting are the safest bet.

Match the mood to the colors. Dark, moody pairs (deep blue + black, plum + charcoal) suit music, fashion, and editorial work. Bright pairs (coral + yellow, teal + white) suit lifestyle, fitness, and kids' content.

Test at thumbnail size. Most duotone images are viewed small — on phone feeds and video thumbnails. Check that the two colors still separate clearly when the image is tiny.

## Duotone vs. other photo effects

Duotone is often confused with a few lookalikes — here's how they differ and when to pick each:

**Duotone vs. black and white.** Black and white uses the full gray spectrum; duotone replaces it with two hues. Duotone feels warmer and more designed, while B&W feels documentary and timeless.

**Duotone vs. color grading.** Grading shifts the colors of a full-color photo; duotone removes color variety entirely. Use grading for photography, duotone for graphic design.

**Duotone vs. posterize.** Posterize reduces an image to flat bands of color — it looks illustrative and chunky. Duotone keeps smooth gradients, so it looks photographic while still stylized. For portraits and hero images, duotone almost always wins.

## Common duotone mistakes

**Picking two similar tones.** Duotone needs contrast — navy and dark blue merge into mud. If your pair looks flat, push the light tone brighter or the dark tone darker.

**Using a low-resolution source.** Duotone can't hide pixelation; it can actually emphasize it. Start with the highest-resolution original you have.

**Ignoring the background.** Busy backgrounds fight the duotone effect. Photos with clean, simple backgrounds produce the boldest, most poster-like results.
