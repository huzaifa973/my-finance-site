## Detailed breakdown of each tool

### 1. Google Keyword Planner
What it does: The official Google Ads tool shows monthly search volume ranges, competition levels, and suggested bid prices for keywords, plus related keyword ideas. It is the only tool with data directly from Google.
Limits: Requires a Google Ads account (you can create one without running ads). Volume shows as broad ranges (like 10K to 100K) unless you spend money on ads. No keyword difficulty score.
Best use: Getting authoritative volume estimates and discovering high-intent commercial keywords with suggested bid data.

### 2. Google Trends
What it does: Shows the relative popularity of a search term over time, by region, and compares up to five keywords side by side. Also reveals rising and breakout related queries.
Limits: Shows relative interest (0 to 100), not absolute search volume. No difficulty or CPC data.
Best use: Validating whether a topic is growing or declining, picking seasonal content timing, and finding breakout keywords before competitors do.

### 3. Google Search Console
What it does: Shows the exact keywords your site already ranks for, their impressions, clicks, and average position — real data about your own pages.
Limits: Only works for sites you own and have verified. It cannot research keywords for a new site or competitors.
Best use: Finding "almost ranking" keywords (positions 5 to 20) that you can boost with a content refresh to double traffic quickly.

### 4. Ubersuggest (free tier)
What it does: Neil Patel's tool gives keyword ideas, search volume, SEO difficulty, paid difficulty, and CPC, plus content ideas showing which pages rank for a term.
Limits: Free tier allows 3 searches per day without signup (more with a free account). Results are capped per search.
Best use: A quick all-in-one check of volume and difficulty when you are evaluating a single keyword.

### 5. Keyword Surfer (Chrome extension)
What it does: A free Chrome extension that shows search volume, related keywords, and word count estimates directly in Google search results pages.
Limits: Only works in Google search. Data coverage varies by country; some regions show limited volume.
Best use: Researching as you browse — every Google search becomes a keyword research session with related terms visible at a glance.

### 6. AlsoAsked
What it does: Pulls People Also Ask questions from Google and visualizes how questions branch into related sub-questions in a mind map.
Limits: Free tier gives 3 searches per day. Exports require a paid plan.
Best use: Building FAQ sections and content outlines that answer every related question a searcher might ask.

### 7. AnswerThePublic (free tier)
What it does: Visualizes autocomplete data into questions, prepositions, comparisons, and alphabetical lists around a seed keyword.
Limits: Free tier allows 3 searches per day and limited CSV export. Results are not filtered by volume.
Best use: Discovering long-tail question keywords and content angles you would never find in a volume-based tool.

### 8. Keyword Sheeter
What it does: Pulls thousands of Google autocomplete suggestions for a seed keyword in minutes and lets you export the full list as CSV.
Limits: No volume, difficulty, or CPC data — just raw keyword ideas. No filtering beyond positive and negative filters.
Best use: Bulk keyword discovery when you need a massive list of related terms to filter and sort elsewhere.

### 9. SeedKeywords
What it does: Lets you create a scenario, ask people what they would search for, and collect real search queries from a small audience.
Limits: Requires you to drive respondents to your scenario; no built-in audience. Volume is small.
Best use: Finding exact phrasing real customers use when searching, which is gold for ad copy and on-page SEO.

### 10. WordStream Free Keyword Tool
What it does: Returns keyword suggestions with volume, competition, and estimated CPC, with optional industry and country filtering.
Limits: Requires an email signup to see the full results; the first 30 or so are visible immediately.
Best use: Quick PPC-focused keyword research with CPC data when planning Google Ads campaigns alongside SEO.

### 11. Ahrefs Free Keyword Generator
What it does: The free version of Ahrefs' keyword tool gives up to 150 keyword ideas per search with volume estimates and keyword difficulty, across 10+ search engines including YouTube and Amazon.
Limits: Capped at 150 results per search; no full metrics or SERP overview without a paid account.
Best use: Getting a reliable difficulty score and keyword ideas from one of the most trusted data providers in SEO.

### 12. SEMrush (free limited account)
What it does: A free SEMrush account gives 10 searches per day across its keyword magic tool, organic research, and domain analytics, with volume, difficulty, and trend data.
Limits: Strict daily search cap; results are truncated compared to paid.
Best use: Occasional deep dives into competitor keywords and full keyword magic lists when you need more than free tools provide.

### 13. Moz Keyword Explorer (free tier)
What it does: Free account gives 10 queries per month with volume, difficulty, organic CTR, and priority score, plus SERP analysis.
Limits: Only 10 queries per month on the free plan — very limited.
Best use: Checking a small number of high-priority keywords where Moz's difficulty score and CTR estimate help decide whether to target them.

### 14. LowFruits (free trial credits)
What it does: Analyzes the top 10 Google results for weak points (forums, Quora, low-authority sites) and flags keywords where ranking is easy, with free signup credits.
Limits: Free credits are limited; after that it is a paid tool. No credit card required for the initial credits.
Best use: Finding low-competition keywords that a new or low-authority site can actually rank for.

### 15. KWFinder (free limited searches)
What it does: Mangools' KWFinder gives a handful of free searches per day with accurate volume, trend data, and keyword difficulty plus SERP analysis.
Limits: Free tier is very limited — a few searches per day without signup.
Best use: Quick difficulty checks and SERP previews for keywords you are seriously considering targeting.

## How to do keyword research for free in 5 steps
1. Start with Google Trends to confirm your broad topic is stable or growing, not declining.
2. Enter your seed keyword into Keyword Sheeter and export thousands of related ideas.
3. Filter the list for relevance, then check the top 50 candidates in Google Keyword Planner and Ahrefs Free Keyword Generator for volume and difficulty.
4. Plug the most promising terms into AlsoAsked and AnswerThePublic to find question-based long-tail variations to include in your article.
5. After publishing, check Google Search Console every two weeks. Keywords in positions 5 to 20 are your refresh targets — add a section answering that exact query and you will often jump to page one.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a good keyword difficulty score for a new blog?**
Aim for keywords with a difficulty score under 20 (on a 0 to 100 scale) for your first 20 posts. New sites have almost no authority, so only the lowest-competition terms are realistic. As you build backlinks and traffic, you can gradually target harder keywords.

**Should I target high-volume or low-volume keywords first?**
Low-volume, low-competition long-tail keywords first. A new site will not rank for "best laptop" (millions of results), but it can rank for "best laptop under 80000 PKR for students" (a few thousand searches, far less competition). Ten ranking long-tail articles beat zero ranking high-volume articles every time.

**How many keywords should I target per blog post?**
One primary keyword per post, plus 5 to 10 related secondary keywords that naturally fit into subheadings. Google ranks pages for dozens of related queries anyway, so write to cover the topic thoroughly rather than stuffing a single term.

**How often should I do keyword research?**
Do a deep research session once per quarter to build a content calendar, then quick checks before each post. Refresh your Search Console analysis every two weeks to find quick-win keywords you already rank for.
