PNG, JPG, WebP — three formats, three jobs. Using the wrong one means bloated websites, rejected uploads, and photos that won't open. The fix takes seconds: convert to the right format for the task at hand.

This guide covers which format to pick for every situation, then shows the fastest free way to convert — including whole batches at once.

## PNG vs JPG vs WebP: the honest comparison

| | PNG | JPG / JPEG | WebP |
|---|---|---|---|
| Best for | Logos, graphics, screenshots, transparency | Photos, universal sharing | Web photos, smallest files |
| Compression | Lossless (bigger files) | Lossy (smaller files) | Lossy or lossless |
| Transparency | Yes | No | Yes |
| Typical photo size (vs JPG) | 3–5× larger | Baseline | 25–35% smaller |
| Compatibility | Universal | Universal | All modern browsers (2020+) |

**The rule of thumb:** photos → WebP for web, JPG for sharing. Graphics with text or transparency → PNG. Everything else follows from that.

## When to convert (and to what)

- **PNG photo → WebP or JPG:** the #1 win. Screenshots and exports saved as PNG are often 5–10× larger than they need to be. Convert photos to WebP at 85% quality.
- **Anything → JPG:** when compatibility matters — email attachments, job applications, print shops, older devices, government forms.
- **JPG/PNG → WebP:** when it's going on a website. Smaller files = faster pages = better SEO.
- **Keep PNG:** logos, icons, and graphics with transparency. Converting these to JPG destroys transparency and adds artifacts around sharp edges.

## Watch out for these gotchas

1. **JPG has no transparency.** Converting a transparent PNG logo to JPG fills the background (usually white or black). Use PNG or WebP for transparency.
2. **Repeated JPG saves degrade quality.** Every JPG→JPG save re-compresses. Convert once from the best-quality source you have.
3. **WebP isn't accepted everywhere yet.** Some older uploaders, email systems, and desktop apps still reject WebP — check before sending. When in doubt, JPG is the safe choice.

### Converting for specific platforms

Different platforms have their own quirks. WhatsApp and most messaging apps auto-convert everything to JPG anyway, so sending WebP is pointless there — convert to JPG first to control the quality yourself. Marketplaces like Amazon and eBay require JPG or PNG and reject WebP uploads, so product photos should be JPG. WordPress accepts WebP natively since version 5.8 and serves it happily, making it the best choice for blog images. When a platform's requirements are unclear, JPG is always the safe bet because nothing rejects it.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is WebP better than JPG?**
For file size, yes — WebP is typically 25–35% smaller than JPG at the same visual quality. JPG still wins on universal compatibility: every device, email client, and old program opens it.

**Does converting PNG to JPG lose quality?**
JPG is a lossy format, so technically yes — but at 85–90% quality the difference is invisible for photos. For graphics with text or transparency, keep PNG instead.

**How do I convert HEIC photos from my iPhone?**
iPhones save photos as HEIC by default. Most converters handle HEIC→JPG; on the phone itself, Settings > Camera > Formats > "Most Compatible" makes future photos save as JPG directly.
