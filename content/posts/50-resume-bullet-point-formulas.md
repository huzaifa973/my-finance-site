Recruiters spend about seven seconds on a first resume scan, and bullet points are what their eyes land on. The difference between "responsible for customer support" and "resolved 95% of 200+ weekly support tickets within 24 hours, lifting satisfaction scores from 4.1 to 4.7" is the difference between the reject pile and the interview pile. Same job — one shows a duty, the other shows impact.

These 50 formulas give you the sentence skeletons that turn any experience into a results-focused bullet. Each formula includes a fill-in-the-blank structure plus a concrete example so you can see the pattern in action. They are grouped by the type of impact: revenue and growth, cost and efficiency, leadership, customer results, and technical or process wins. Copy the formula, plug in your numbers, and watch your resume change character.

## What's inside this formula pack

The 50 formulas are organized into five impact categories of 10. Revenue and growth formulas frame your work in terms of money earned, customers gained, or markets expanded. Cost and efficiency formulas show what you saved or streamlined. Leadership formulas demonstrate how you moved teams and people forward. Customer impact formulas capture satisfaction, retention, and service quality. Technical and process formulas cover systems built, tools adopted, and workflows improved. Every formula follows the same skeleton: strong action verb + what you did + measurable result, with the example showing a filled-in version.

## The full 50-formula collection

Use each formula as a skeleton: keep the structure, replace the bracketed parts with your facts. Numbered within each category for easy reference.

## How to use this formula pack

1. List your real accomplishments for each job — what changed because you were there — before touching the formulas.
2. Match each accomplishment to a category above and pick the formula whose skeleton fits closest.
3. Fill in your numbers honestly; estimate ranges only when exact figures are confidential, and never invent them.
4. Start every bullet with a strong action verb and keep each bullet to one or two lines maximum.
5. Read the finished resume aloud — if a bullet sounds like a job description, rewrite it with a sharper formula.

The golden rule: duty + number = impact. "Handled payroll" is a duty. "Processed payroll for 140 employees across 4 states with 100% on-time accuracy for 3 years" is impact. You don't need a fancy job to have numbers — volume, speed, accuracy, and before/after comparisons all count. And tailor ruthlessly: pick the 3-5 bullets per role that best match the job you're applying for, not the 3-5 you like most.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many bullet points should each job have?**
Three to five per role is the sweet spot for most jobs. Lead with your strongest achievement, end with a second strong one, and cut anything that merely describes a duty without a result. Quality beats quantity — one quantified win beats four vague lines.

**What if I don't have numbers for my achievements?**
Estimate responsibly or measure differently: scope (team size, budget, customers served), frequency (daily, weekly), scale (pages, tickets, accounts), or before/after comparisons. Even "reduced turnaround from 5 days to 2" without dollar figures shows impact.

**Should bullet points start with action verbs?**
Yes — strong action verbs like "launched," "cut," "grew," and "automated" signal ownership and results. Avoid weak openers like "responsible for" or "assisted with," which describe presence rather than impact.
