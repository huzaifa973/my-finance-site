Whether it's a profile picture that must be exactly 400×400, a blog image that should be 1200px wide, or a job portal demanding "under 200KB" — resizing images is a weekly chore for almost everyone. Done right, it's instant and lossless. Done wrong, you get stretched faces and blurry thumbnails.

Here's how to resize images correctly for every common situation.

## Downsizing vs. upsizing: the one rule that matters

- **Making an image smaller (downscaling):** safe and quality-preserving. A 6000px photo shrunk to 1200px looks *sharper*, not worse, because redundant pixels are averaged away.
- **Making an image bigger (upscaling):** cannot create detail that isn't there. Enlarging beyond about 110% of the original gives you a bigger, softer image.

Most resizing tasks are downscaling — which means there's no reason to lose quality at all.

## Method 2: Resize on your phone (no app needed)

- **iPhone:** Photos → open the image → Share → there's no native resize, but the Shortcuts app has a "Resize Image" action you can add once and reuse forever.
- **Android:** Google Photos → open image → Edit → Crop gives limited options; for exact sizes, Samsung Gallery's Edit → resize icon works well.

## Method 3: Resize on desktop without installing anything

- **Windows:** Right-click the image → Open with → Paint → Resize → enter percentage or pixels.
- **Mac:** Open in Preview → Tools → Adjust Size → enter the width; Preview keeps proportions locked automatically.

## What size should your image be?

Common targets, in pixels:

| Use case | Recommended size |
|---|---|
| Blog content image | 800–1200px wide |
| Website hero/banner | 1600–2000px wide |
| Instagram post | 1080×1080 (square) or 1080×1350 |
| Profile picture | 400×400 |
| Email attachment | Max 1600px wide |
| Job portal photo | Per their spec — often under 200KB |

A good workflow: **resize to display size first, then compress.** Resize removes unneeded pixels; compression removes unneeded data. Together they routinely turn a 6MB phone photo into a 150KB web-ready image.

## Avoid these mistakes

1. **Stretching instead of locking proportions** — uncheck "maintain aspect ratio" and faces go wide. Always keep the lock on unless you're intentionally cropping.
2. **Upscaling tiny images for print** — a 300px web image will print blurry at any real-world size. You need the original high-res file for print.
3. **Resizing a JPEG repeatedly** — every save re-compresses. Do all your edits at full size, then resize and save once at the end.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I resize an image without losing quality?**
Downsizing (making an image smaller) preserves quality perfectly — the image stays sharp. Upsizing (making it bigger) cannot add real detail, so enlarging beyond ~110% of the original size produces a softer image.

**What is the best image size for a website?**
Match the displayed size: most blog content images display at 800–1200px wide, so resize to that width. Keep hero/banner images under 2000px wide and compress afterwards for fast loading.

**How do I resize an image to a specific KB size?**
Use a resizer with a target-file-size mode (like DollarWise's free image resizer), which iteratively adjusts quality and dimensions until the file lands at your target size — perfect for job portals with KB limits.

**How do I resize without distorting the image?**
Lock the aspect ratio so width and height scale together. Every decent resizer has this option — in our tool it's the "Keep original proportions" checkbox, enabled by default.
