A good FAQ page answers the questions visitors are already thinking — before they email you, before they bounce, and before they choose a competitor who answered first. These fifty question templates cover the angles every FAQ page needs: what it is, how it works, what it costs, whether it is safe, how long it takes, and how to get started. They are written for website owners, small businesses, and bloggers adding a FAQ section to a service page or product page. Replace [topic] with your product or service and the page practically writes itself.

## What's inside this resource

The templates are organized by reader intent. Cost and timing questions remove purchase friction; safety and risk questions build trust; how-to and getting-started questions help beginners take the first step. A strong FAQ page also earns search visibility: Google often pulls FAQ answers into search results, so write answers in plain language, keep each answer to two or three sentences, and lead with the direct answer before the explanation.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many FAQs should a page have?

Ten to twenty well-chosen questions beat fifty generic ones. Pick the questions your customers actually ask, and add more as new ones come in.

### Should FAQ answers be short?

Yes — lead with the direct answer in one sentence, then add a sentence or two of detail. Visitors scan FAQ pages; walls of text defeat the purpose.

### Do FAQ pages help SEO?

They can. Clear questions and answers match real search queries, and structured FAQ content may appear in search results — but only when the answers are genuinely helpful, not stuffed with keywords.
