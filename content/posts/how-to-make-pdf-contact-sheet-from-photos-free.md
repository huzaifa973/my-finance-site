A **contact sheet** is one of photography's oldest ideas, reborn for the digital age: a single sheet showing dozens of photo thumbnails in a neat grid. Film photographers used them to proof negatives; today they're how photographers cull a shoot, how real estate agents document a property, how insurance adjusters record damage — and how anyone organizes a big pile of photos into something reviewable at a glance.

## What a Good Contact Sheet Includes

- **Thumbnails in a grid** — 4×5, 5×4, or 6×5 per page are the classics
- **Filenames under each thumbnail** — so selects map back to originals
- **Optional captions or dates** — useful for evidence and archives
- **Page numbers** on multi-page sheets
- **Your name or project title** in a header (for client proofing)

## Choosing the Right Layout

The grid density is a tradeoff between overview and legibility:

| Layout | Thumbnails/page | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| 4×5 (20) | 20 | Proofing a shoot, client selects |
| 5×6 (30) | 30 | Large archives, inventory records |
| 3×3 (9) | 9 | Portfolio review, large readable previews |
| 2×2 (4) | 4 | Before/after documentation, comparisons |

For **client proofing**, 20 per page with filenames is the sweet spot — clients can circle selects and email you the filename numbers. For **insurance or property records**, denser 30-per-page sheets with dates give maximum documentation per page.

**Multi-page sheets** are normal — a 400-photo shoot at 25 per page is 16 pages, which is fine as a PDF. Name the file clearly (`2026-10-21_wedding-smith_contact-sheet.pdf`) so it sorts next to the shoot folder. If you're printing, double-sided printing halves the paper, and a simple staple in the corner keeps pages in order during review sessions.

## From Film Days to Digital: A Quick History

The contact sheet is older than digital photography by a century. Film photographers laid strips of negatives directly on photographic paper and exposed them, producing a single sheet of tiny positive prints — literally made by *contact* between negative and paper. Editors circled selects with a grease pencil, and those marks became the shooting order for prints.

Digital killed the darkroom but kept the workflow: cull hundreds of images down to the keepers as fast as possible. The modern contact sheet PDF is the direct descendant — same grid, same circling (now with a PDF annotator or a printed pen), zero chemicals.

## Contact Sheets vs. Online Proof Galleries

Should you send a PDF or use an online gallery (Pixieset, ShootProof, Google Photos)? Both have a place:

| | PDF contact sheet | Online gallery |
|---|---|---|
| Needs internet | No | Yes |
| Client can zoom individual photos | Limited | Yes |
| Prints nicely | Yes | Rarely |
| Works as a legal/archive record | Excellent | Depends on the service |
| Effort to create | 2 minutes | 15+ minutes of uploading |

For quick internal culling and archival records, the PDF wins. For client delivery where they want to zoom and favorite individual shots, a gallery wins. Many photographers do both: contact sheet for the same-day cull, gallery for the final selects.

## A Smarter Culling Workflow

1. **Import and back up** the shoot first — contact sheets are for selecting, not for storage.
2. **Generate the sheet** with filenames visible, 20–25 thumbnails per page.
3. **First pass: reject.** Mark obvious rejects (blurry, blinked, duplicates) with an X. Don't agonize — speed matters.
4. **Second pass: select.** Circle the keepers. Aim to keep 10–20% of a shoot.
5. **Cross-reference filenames** back to the originals and copy selects into a "SELECTS" folder.
6. **Archive the sheet** with the project — six months later, it's the fastest way to remember what a shoot contained.

## Tips for Better Contact Sheets

- **Sort before you sheet.** Rename files in shooting order (or by rating) before uploading so the grid reads logically.
- **Keep filenames short.** Long camera-generated names (IMG_20261021_143522.jpg) wrap awkwardly — batch-rename first if you can.
- **Mind the resolution.** Thumbnails don't need 24MP originals; the tool downsamples, but huge files take longer to process. It's fine — just be patient with 200+ photos.
- **Print at 100%.** If you print the PDF, use "Actual size" so thumbnails render at their intended dimensions.
- **Add a header.** Project name + date on page one turns a printout into a proper record.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a contact sheet?

A contact sheet is a single page (or PDF) showing thumbnail images of many photos in a grid, like the proof sheets from film photography. It lets you review, select, and share dozens of photos at a glance without opening each one.

### How many photos fit on one contact sheet page?

A typical grid fits 20–35 thumbnails per page depending on layout: 5×4 (20), 6×5 (30), or 7×5 (35) are common. Denser grids are harder to evaluate; 20–25 per page is the sweet spot for proofing.

### Can I add filenames or captions to contact sheet thumbnails?

Yes — good contact sheet tools print each photo's filename (and optionally the date) under its thumbnail, which is exactly how you cross-reference selects back to the original files.
