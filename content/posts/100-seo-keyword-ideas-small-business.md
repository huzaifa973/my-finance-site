Small businesses do not need a thousand keywords — they need the hundred that local customers actually type. This pack builds those hundred from a simple, repeatable pattern: ten everyday services crossed with ten proven search modifiers, from “near me” and “prices” to “how to choose” and “appointment booking.” Use the list as inspiration, then swap in your own service and town to generate your own hundred in minutes.

## What's inside this resource

The pattern works because search demand follows intent: some people are comparing (“best in town,” “reviews”), some are ready to buy (“prices,” “appointment booking”), and some are still learning (“for beginners,” “cost guide”). Covering all three kinds of intent with one service gives you a content plan — a service page, a comparison post, and a beginner guide — instead of a random pile of keywords.

## How to use this resource

1. Pick your service and run the ten modifiers against it before you write anything.
2. Sort the results by intent: ready-to-buy keywords deserve service pages, learning keywords deserve guides.
3. Add your town or neighbourhood to the strongest five — local intent converts best.
4. Check each keyword in a free tool to confirm real search volume before investing in a full article.
5. Revisit the list quarterly: services change, and so do the words customers use.

A resource is only useful when it gets used — pick one item today, use it as written, and adapt it from experience rather than from theory.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are these keywords guaranteed to have search volume?**

No single list can promise that for every town — volume varies by place. Use the pattern, then verify your top picks in a keyword tool before writing.

**Should I target “near me” keywords?**

Yes, on your service and location pages. Search engines match “near me” to the searcher's location, so your Google Business Profile matters as much as the page.

**How many keywords should one page target?**

One primary keyword plus a few close variants. Ten modifiers belong across several pages, not crammed into one.
