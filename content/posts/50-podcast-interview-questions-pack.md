The difference between a forgettable interview and a memorable one is rarely the guest — it is the questions. Generic questions get generic answers: rehearsed stories the guest has told a dozen times. Specific, human questions — about a hard period, a risky decision, a mentor's voice — make guests pause, think, and say something they have not said before. Good interview questions are not clever; they are curious, concrete, and open enough to let a real answer breathe.
This pack gives you 50 interview questions across ten groups: origin stories, craft and process, challenges, turning points, influences, money, community, future trends, personal life, and a quick-fire round to close with energy. It suits podcast hosts, YouTube interviewers, and anyone preparing to interview a guest. Ten questions are below; the free download holds all 50.

## What's inside this question pack

Origin questions open the conversation gently and give listeners the guest's story in their own words. Craft questions go deeper into process — the daily work, the tools, the moment something is finished — which is where experts reveal what amateurs miss. Challenge and turning-point questions are where interviews become memorable: setbacks, risky decisions, and forks in the road produce the honest answers audiences remember.
People and influence questions reveal the mentors and advice behind the success, money questions handle the topic most interviews avoid, and community questions explore the audience relationship. Future questions let the guest look ahead, personal questions show the human behind the work, and the quick-fire round ends on a light note that listeners love to share. ## How to use this resource

1. Pick eight to twelve questions from the pack that fit your guest — never try to ask all 50.
2. Order them as an arc: easy origin questions first, deeper challenge questions in the middle, light quick-fire at the end.
3. Rewrite each question in your own words so it sounds like a conversation, not a questionnaire.
4. Listen to each answer fully before moving on. The best follow-up is the one the answer just suggested.
5. Send the guest two or three themes in advance, not the exact questions — prepared guests give better answers, scripted ones give duller ones.

A question pack prepares you; listening makes the interview. Use the pack to start, then follow where the answers lead.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many questions should I prepare for one episode?**

Eight to twelve strong questions is plenty for a 30- to 45-minute conversation. Fewer, deeper questions with follow-ups beat a long list you rush through.

**Should I send questions to the guest in advance?**

Send themes, not exact questions. Guests who know the topics relax and give richer answers; guests who rehearse exact answers sound scripted.

**What makes a bad interview question?**

Questions that can be answered with yes or no, questions that are actually statements, and double questions that ask two things at once. One clear, open question at a time works best.
