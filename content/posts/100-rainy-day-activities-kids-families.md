Rainy days with kids are a test of planning, not patience: the difference between a good one and a long one is having an idea ready before the “I'm bored” chorus starts. These one hundred activities, in ten groups, cover every kind of rainy day — indoor forts and baking, outdoor puddle adventures for the brave, creative projects, active games, quiet time, and free outings that cost nothing. Pick a group, pick an activity, and the day plans itself.

## What's inside this resource

The groups matter because kids' energy changes through the day: start active, go creative in the middle, and end quiet. A rainy day with one anchor activity per part of the day — morning, afternoon, evening — feels full without feeling scheduled. Keep the list where you can reach it on a grey morning.

## The resource (sample)

1. Build a blanket fort and read inside it
2. Jump in puddles in wellies
3. Cook a meal together from scratch
4. Paint a family mural on paper
5. Dance party in the living room
6. Read aloud with funny voices
7. Host a potluck playdate
8. Library story time
9. Make paper boats and race them
10. Plant a small garden together

## How to use this resource

1. Pick one anchor activity per part of the day — morning, afternoon, evening.
2. Let kids choose from two options, not from the whole list — choice without overwhelm.
3. Prepare materials the night before if rain is forecast.
4. Mix active and quiet activities; all of either one ends in chaos or sleepiness.
5. End the day with a calm activity so bedtime stays possible.

A resource is only useful when it gets used — pick one item today, use it as written, and adapt it from experience rather than from theory.

## Frequently asked questions

**What if the kids reject every idea?**

Offer two choices, not an open question. “Fort or baking?” decides faster than “what do you want to do?” — and let them modify the winner.

**Do rainy-day activities need to be educational?**

No. Play is the point. If learning sneaks in through baking or building, that is a bonus, not a requirement.

**How do I handle different ages?**

Pick activities with roles: older kids lead, younger kids assist. Building, cooking, and shows all scale across ages.
