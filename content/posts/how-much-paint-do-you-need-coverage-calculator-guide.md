Buy too little paint and you're making a panicked mid-project store run, hoping the new gallon matches. Buy too much and you've spent good money on paint that hardens in the garage. The fix is simple arithmetic — here's exactly how to calculate how much paint you need, with worked examples.

## The Paint Coverage Formula

Everything starts from one number: **coverage per gallon**.

| Paint type | Typical coverage per gallon (one coat) |
|---|---|
| Interior latex (smooth walls) | 350–400 sq ft |
| Interior latex (textured walls) | 250–300 sq ft |
| Primer | 200–300 sq ft |
| Exterior paint | 250–350 sq ft |
| Ceiling paint | 350–400 sq ft |

The formula:

```
Gallons = (Total paintable sq ft ÷ Coverage per gallon) × Number of coats × 1.10 (waste factor)
```

Then round **up** to the nearest whole unit (gallons, or quarts for small jobs).

## Step 1: Measure Your Walls

For each wall: **width × height = square feet**. Add all walls together, then subtract doors and windows:

- Standard interior door: ~21 sq ft (3 × 7 ft)
- Standard window: ~15 sq ft (3 × 5 ft)
- Large sliding door: ~40 sq ft

**Shortcut for a rectangular room:** `2 × (length + width) × ceiling height` gives total wall area.

### Worked example: 12×12 bedroom, 8 ft ceilings

- Wall area: 2 × (12 + 12) × 8 = **384 sq ft**
- Subtract 1 door (21) + 1 window (15): 384 − 36 = **348 sq ft** of paintable wall

## Step 2: Factor in Coats

**Two coats** is the professional standard — it gives even color, proper durability, and hides the old color. Plan for:

- **Two coats** when: changing colors, painting over dark with light, new drywall, or wanting a lasting finish.
- **One coat** only when: touching up, or repainting the exact same color over paint in good condition.
- **Primer + two coats** when: painting bare drywall, wood, or making a dramatic color change (dark red → white). Primer is cheaper than paint and dramatically improves adhesion and coverage.

## Step 3: Add the Waste Factor

Multiply by **1.10** (add 10%) for waste — paint left in the roller, tray, brush cleaning, and cutting-in. For textured walls, rough exteriors, or first-time painters, use **1.15–1.20**.

Then **round up**. Paint is sold in gallons and quarts; a calculation of 1.9 gallons means buying 2 gallons. Running 5% short mid-wall leaves a visible lap line where the new batch starts.

## Complete Worked Examples

### Example 1: 12×12 bedroom, two coats

- Paintable area: 348 sq ft (from above)
- Coverage: 350 sq ft/gallon
- Math: (348 ÷ 350) × 2 coats × 1.10 = **2.19 gallons** → **buy 2 gallons + 1 quart** (or 3 gallons if you want touch-up stock)

### Example 2: Living room 16×20, 9 ft ceilings, two coats + primer

- Wall area: 2 × (16 + 20) × 9 = 648 sq ft
- Minus 2 doors (42) + 3 windows (45): **561 sq ft**
- Paint: (561 ÷ 350) × 2 × 1.10 = **3.5 gallons** → **buy 4 gallons**
- Primer (one coat, 250 sq ft/gal): (561 ÷ 250) × 1.10 = **2.5 gallons** → **buy 3 gallons**

### Example 3: Exterior — 2,000 sq ft house, one coat

- Rule of thumb: paintable exterior ≈ house sq ft × 0.75–1.0 for siding (use 1.0 to be safe): **2,000 sq ft**
- Exterior coverage ~300 sq ft/gallon, two coats recommended: (2,000 ÷ 300) × 2 × 1.15 = **15.3 gallons** → **buy 16 gallons** (three 5-gallon buckets + 1 gallon)

### Example 4: Just the ceiling, 12×12 room

- Ceiling area: 12 × 12 = **144 sq ft**
- One coat usually suffices for ceilings: (144 ÷ 375) × 1.10 = **0.42 gallons** → **buy 1 gallon** (or 2 quarts)

## 7 Money-Saving Tips

1. **Don't skip primer on bare surfaces.** Primer costs less than paint and prevents the topcoat from soaking in — skipping it often means needing a third coat of expensive paint.
2. **Buy 5-gallon buckets for big jobs.** They're typically 15–20% cheaper per gallon than individual gallons.
3. **One finish per room.** Eggshell or satin for walls everywhere keeps leftovers interchangeable for touch-ups.
4. **Keep the lid sealed between coats.** Paint skins over fast; a sealed can between sessions prevents waste.
5. **Box your paint.** If using multiple gallons of the same color, mix them together in a 5-gallon bucket first — this eliminates slight batch-to-batch color differences.
6. **Save the label info.** Write the paint name, color code, and sheen on painter's tape inside a closet — future touch-ups will match perfectly.
7. **Use a calculator for odd shapes.** Vaulted ceilings, stairwells, and accent walls are where mental math fails — break them into rectangles and add them up, or use the [free paint coverage calculator](/tools/paint-coverage-calculator/).

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much paint do I need for a 12x12 bedroom?**
A 12x12 ft bedroom with 8 ft ceilings has about 384 sq ft of wall (minus ~60 sq ft for door and window = ~324 sq ft). At 350 sq ft per gallon with two coats, you need roughly 1.9 gallons — buy 2 gallons.

**How many square feet does a gallon of paint cover?**
Most interior paints cover 350–400 sq ft per gallon per coat. Primer covers about 200–300 sq ft per gallon. Textured or unpainted surfaces absorb more paint, dropping coverage toward 250–300 sq ft per gallon.

**How many coats of paint do I need?**
Two coats is the standard for a durable, even finish — especially when changing colors or painting over a darker shade. One coat is only enough for touch-ups or repainting the same color in good condition.

**Should I buy extra paint beyond the calculation?**
Yes — add a 10% waste factor to your calculation and round up to whole gallons or quarts. Leftover paint is essential for future touch-ups, and paint colors can vary slightly between batches.
