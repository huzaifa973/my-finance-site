A form is only as good as its labels: vague labels (“Info,” “Details”) produce vague answers, and missing fields produce follow-up emails. This pack gives you fifty field labels across the five forms almost every business needs — contact, order, feedback, registration, and booking — each phrased the way users actually understand it. Copy them into your PDF form builder and your forms start returning complete answers.

## What's inside this resource

The labels follow three rules: one question per field, plain words instead of jargon, and examples where a format is expected (dates, phone numbers). The groups are ordered by how often you will need them, and every label works as-is or as a starting point you adapt to your own form.

## How to use this resource

1. Pick the group that matches your form, then delete the fields you do not need — shorter forms get finished more often.
2. Keep the wording exactly as written for the first version; change labels only when answers show confusion.
3. Add example text inside fields where a format matters, like dates and phone numbers.
4. Mark only truly required fields as required — every extra required field costs completions.
5. Test the form yourself on a phone before publishing; most form users are on mobile.

A resource is only useful when it gets used — pick one item today, use it as written, and adapt it from experience rather than from theory.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many fields should a form have?**

As few as possible while still getting what you need. Every field you remove raises completion rates; ten good fields beat twenty mediocre ones.

**Should labels end with a question mark?**

For questions, yes. For data fields like “Email address,” a plain label reads cleaner. Match the label style to the field type.

**What about privacy?**

Only ask for data you will actually use, and say why on the form. Unnecessary personal data creates risk and lowers trust.
