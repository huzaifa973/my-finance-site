A signature in the wrong place — on the wrong page, in a shared field, without a date — can delay a deal, confuse a signer, or weaken the document itself. Whether you are sending contracts, consent forms, or approvals, where each signature sits matters as much as the wording above it. This fifty-point checklist covers everything to verify before you send a document for signing: field placement, signer identification, dates, witnesses, digital signature settings, and the audit trail that proves the signature happened. It is built for business owners, freelancers, HR teams, and anyone who regularly sends documents out for signature.

## What's inside this resource

The checklist walks the full signing workflow in order: preparing the document, placing fields, labeling signers and dates, handling witnesses and initials, configuring digital signatures and signing order, and archiving the completed file. Early items catch the mistakes that force a re-send — shared fields, missing dates, signature blocks stranded before the terms. Later items cover the details that protect you afterwards: audit trails, locked documents, copies to all parties, and proper storage. Use it as a pre-send review for every document, and the embarrassing 'please sign again' email becomes rare.

11. The signature area has enough white space for a handwritten or drawn signature to fit comfortably.
12. Signature fields do not overlap text, images, or other form fields.
13. Digital signature fields are set to lock the document after signing, preventing silent edits.
14. The certificate or audit trail records the signer's name, email, IP address, and timestamp.
15. Signers are identified by email invitation, not by a publicly shared signing link, for sensitive documents.
16. The document is sent in its final form — no tracked changes, comments, or draft watermarks remain.
17. All blank fields the signer must complete are highlighted or clearly marked before sending.
18. Optional fields are labeled optional so signers do not stall on fields they can skip.
19. The signing order is set correctly when one person's signature must come before another's.
20. A 'signed copy to all parties' step is configured so everyone receives the completed document.
21. The signature block includes the company name and registration details for business agreements.
22. Counterpart clauses are included when parties sign separate copies of the same agreement.
23. Electronic signature consent language is present where the law requires it.
24. The document names the exact number of original copies or counterparts being signed.
25. Signature fields use a legible font size (at least 11pt) for printed names and dates.
26. The PDF is not password-locked in a way that prevents the signer from opening or signing it.
27. Form field tab order reaches the signature field in a logical sequence.
28. The signature field is marked required so the document cannot be submitted unsigned.
29. A visible note tells signers what happens after they sign (who receives it, what comes next).
30. Contact details for questions appear on the signing page, not buried in an earlier section.
31. The document's effective date is stated explicitly and is not left to inference from signature dates.
32. Amendment or addendum pages carry their own signature lines, not a reference to the original block.
33. Initials are requested on any page where handwritten changes were made before signing.
34. The signer can download or print a copy of the completed document after signing.

36. File naming for the signed copy includes the document title and the signing date.

50. A record of who signed, when, and in what order is archived with the signed document.

## How to use this resource

1. Read through the document once as a signer would, checking that the signature block comes after the terms, not before them.
2. Place one labeled field per signer, with printed name, title, and a date field beside each signature.
3. Add initials boxes to pages with key clauses, and witness lines wherever the document type requires them.
4. Test the signing link or file yourself end-to-end before sending it to a real signer.
5. After signing, verify the completed PDF — valid signatures, filled fields, no missing pages — and archive it with its audit trail.

## Frequently asked questions

### Where should a signature block go in a PDF?

At the end of the document, after all terms and conditions, with one labeled field per signer plus a date field. Placing it before the terms, or on a detached page, creates confusion about what was actually agreed to.

### Do digital signatures need an audit trail?

For business and legal documents, yes in practice. A record of who signed, when, from which email and IP address, and in what order is what makes a digital signature defensible if it is ever questioned.

### Can two people share one signature field?

No. Each signer needs their own field so the document records exactly who signed and when. Shared fields make it impossible to prove individual agreement.
