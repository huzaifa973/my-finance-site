Nothing kills a practice session faster than a guitar that sounds slightly *off*. Chords feel sour, melodies clash with backing tracks, and beginners often blame their fingers when the real culprit is tuning. The good news: tuning takes about two minutes once you know how, and you do not need to buy anything or install an app.

A free online guitar tuner uses your device's microphone to listen to each string and show you exactly how far sharp or flat it is. This guide covers standard tuning, the step-by-step process, and the habits that keep your guitar in tune longer.

## Standard tuning: the six notes

Guitars are tuned in **standard tuning** — E, A, D, G, B, E — from the thickest string (6th) to the thinnest (1st). Memorize the order:

> **E**ddie **A**te **D**ynamite, **G**ood **B**ye **E**ddie.

Two mnemonics worth knowing:

- **String numbers:** the high E (thinnest) is string 1; low E (thickest) is string 6. Most diagrams and tab sites refer to strings by number.
- **"Tune up, not down."** When a string is flat, tune *past* the target note slightly, then bring it *down* to pitch. This settles the string against the tuning peg so it holds longer. If a string is sharp, loosen it well below pitch first, then tune up to it.

## How to tune your guitar online free (7 steps)

1. **Find a quiet spot** — background noise confuses the tuner. Turn off the TV, close the window.
2. **Open the free online tuner** — [DollarWise's free Online Guitar Tuner](/tools/online-guitar-tuner/) runs in your browser with no install.
3. **Allow microphone access** — your browser will ask once. The tuner listens locally; nothing is recorded.
4. **Start with the 6th string (low E)** — pluck the thickest string alone, letting it ring. Watch the tuner's needle or indicator.
5. **Turn the matching peg** — the low E string connects to the peg farthest from the headstock's end on most guitars. Turn slowly while plucking repeatedly.
6. **Center the needle** — keep adjusting until the tuner shows the correct note (E) with the needle centered. Move on to A, then D, G, B, and high E.
7. **Do a second pass** — tightening one string changes the tension on the neck, which nudges the others. Run through all six strings twice.

Your guitar is in tune when each string centers on its target note and an open strum sounds like one clear chord instead of six competing ones.

## Troubleshooting common tuning problems

**The needle jumps around wildly.** You are probably hearing sympathetic vibration from other strings. Mute the other strings with your picking hand and pluck closer to the bridge, where the fundamental note is strongest.

**The tuner shows the wrong note entirely.** You might be a full step off — e.g., your A string is reading as G or B. Check the nearest peg: many beginners turn the wrong peg for the string they are plucking. Trace the string from the bridge to its actual tuning peg.

**A string will not settle.** New strings need to stretch — gently pull each new string away from the fretboard a few times, retune, and repeat. Old, corroded strings also refuse to hold pitch; if your strings are months old and discolored, change them.

**The peg slips.** On older guitars, worn tuning machines let strings unwind. Tighten the small screw on the back of the tuner button if it has one — snug, not forced.

## Habits that keep you in tune

- **Tune every time you pick up the guitar.** Professionals tune before every session. It takes 90 seconds and transforms how everything sounds.
- **Store the guitar upright or on a stand**, away from radiators, windows, and car trunks. Heat and humidity are tuning's worst enemies.
- **Stretch new strings** on day one instead of fighting them for a week.
- **Check tuning after changing capo position** — capos pull strings slightly sharp. Retune with the capo on if you will be playing with it.
- **Learn to hear it.** Play the 5th fret of the low E and compare it with the open A string — they should match. Training your ear this way makes you independent of any tuner eventually.

## Beyond standard tuning: when to explore further

Once standard tuning feels automatic, two doors open:

**Alternate tunings** — Drop D (lowering the 6th string to D) is the gateway: one string change, instantly heavier power chords. Open tunings (open G, DADGAD) make slide guitar and fingerstyle shimmer. The online tuner handles these too — just select the target note per string instead of assuming EADGBE.

**Intonation checks** — if chords sound in tune open but sour higher up the neck, your guitar's intonation may be off (string length vs fret placement). Compare the 12th-fret harmonic with the fretted 12th note on the tuner; a big gap means a setup is due, not more tuning.

Neither replaces the fundamentals: tune standard first, tune often, and your ear will start noticing when something is off before the tuner confirms it. That instinct is the real milestone.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I tune a guitar without a tuner app?
Use a free online guitar tuner in your browser: allow microphone access, pluck one string at a time, and turn its tuning peg until the needle centers on the correct note. Our free online guitar tuner needs no downloads.

### What are the notes for standard guitar tuning?
Standard tuning is E–A–D–G–B–E, from the thickest (6th) string to the thinnest (1st). A memory trick: "Eddie Ate Dynamite, Good Bye Eddie."

### Which string do I tune first?
Tune from the thickest string (low E, 6th) down to the thinnest (high E, 1st). New strings stretch, so expect to go through all six strings two or three times on the first tune.

### Why does my guitar keep going out of tune?
New strings stretch for the first few days, temperature changes move tuning pegs, and aggressive strumming pulls strings sharp or flat. Stretch new strings gently after fitting them and retune before every practice session.
