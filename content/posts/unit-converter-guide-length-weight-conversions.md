## Metric vs imperial: the 60-second primer

The **metric system** (meters, grams, liters) is used by nearly every country in the world. It's built on powers of ten: 1,000 millimeters in a meter, 1,000 grams in a kilogram. Conversions within metric are just moving a decimal point.

The **imperial system** (inches, feet, pounds, ounces) is still standard in the United States. Its units relate by irregular factors — 12 inches in a foot, 16 ounces in a pound — which is exactly why conversions feel harder.

You don't need to memorize everything: a handful of anchor conversions and one reliable converter cover the rest.

### Inches ↔ centimeters

This is the single most-searched length conversion, and for good reason: product dimensions, screen sizes, clothing, and DIY measurements constantly cross the inch/cm boundary.

**Examples:**
- 10 inches × 2.54 = **25.4 cm**
- 24 inches × 2.54 = **60.96 cm** (a standard 24" monitor is about 61 cm wide)
- 100 cm ÷ 2.54 = **39.37 inches**

**Mental shortcut:** multiply inches by 2.5 and add a touch. 10 inches ≈ 25 cm — close enough for shopping and rough planning.

### Feet ↔ meters

Height, room dimensions, and sports fields live in feet↔meters territory.

**Examples:**
- 6 feet × 0.3048 = **1.83 m** (a 6-foot person is 183 cm)
- 10 feet × 0.3048 = **3.05 m**
- 2 meters ÷ 0.3048 = **6.56 feet**

**Mental shortcut:** a foot is roughly 30 cm, so 6 feet ≈ 180 cm.

### Miles ↔ kilometers

Road distances, running races, and fuel economy all need miles↔km.

**Examples:**
- 5 miles × 1.609 = **8.05 km** (a 5-mile run is just over 8K)
- 26.2 miles × 1.609 = **42.16 km** (marathon distance)
- 100 km ÷ 1.609 = **62.14 miles**

**Mental shortcut:** the Fibonacci trick — 5 miles ≈ 8 km, 8 miles ≈ 13 km. Consecutive Fibonacci numbers approximate the mile/km ratio surprisingly well.

## Weight conversions: pounds, kilos, ounces, grams

| Imperial | Metric | Exact factor | Quick mental trick |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 pound (lb) | 0.4536 kg | × 0.4536 | Divide by 2.2 |
| 1 ounce (oz) | 28.35 g | × 28.35 | Roughly 28 g |
| 1 kg | 2.2046 lb | × 2.2046 | A bit over 2 lb |
| 1 stone | 6.350 kg | × 6.350 | UK body weight unit |

### Pounds ↔ kilograms

Body weight, luggage limits, gym plates, and shipping weights all live here.

**Examples:**
- 150 lb ÷ 2.2046 = **68.0 kg**
- 50 lb ÷ 2.2046 = **22.7 kg** (a common checked-baggage limit)
- 80 kg × 2.2046 = **176.4 lb**

**Mental shortcut:** divide pounds by 2 for a rough kilo figure, then add 10%. 150 lb → 75 → about 68 kg after the adjustment. Or simply remember: 1 kg ≈ 2.2 lb.

### Ounces ↔ grams

Cooking is the main arena for ounces↔grams — American recipes in ounces and cups, the rest of the world in grams.

**Examples:**
- 8 oz × 28.35 = **226.8 g** (8 fluid ounces of water)
- 4 oz × 28.35 = **113.4 g** (one stick of butter)
- 500 g ÷ 28.35 = **17.6 oz**

**Mental shortcut:** an ounce is just under 30 grams. Multiply ounces by 30 for a quick kitchen estimate — the error is under 6%.

## How to convert mentally: handy factors worth memorizing

You only need five numbers for 90% of daily conversions:

1. **2.5** — inches to cm (actual: 2.54)
2. **0.3** — feet to meters (actual: 0.3048)
3. **1.6** — miles to km (actual: 1.609)
4. **2.2** — kg to pounds (actual: 2.2046)
5. **28** — ounces to grams (actual: 28.35)

Round numbers make these even easier: 10 inches ≈ 25 cm, 10 feet ≈ 3 m, 10 miles ≈ 16 km, 10 kg ≈ 22 lb, 10 oz ≈ 280 g. Memorize the "tens" and scale up or down.

## When precise conversion matters (and when it doesn't)

**Close enough is fine for:** browsing furniture sizes, converting running distances, rough cooking, and travel planning.

**Precision matters for:**
1. **International shipping** — customs declarations and postage are calculated on exact weight; rounding the wrong way costs real money.
2. **Baking and recipes** — baking is chemistry. A 10% error in leavening or flour ratios can genuinely ruin the result; weigh ingredients when the recipe gives grams.
3. **Construction and DIY** — "measure twice, cut once" exists because a quarter-inch error compounds across a project. Use exact conversions for cuts and fittings.
4. **Medication and health** — dosing is weight-based; never estimate here.
5. **Engineering and manufacturing** — tolerances are specified for a reason.

When precision matters, don't do it in your head — use the [inches to centimeters converter](/tools/inches-to-centimeters-converter/) or a proper calculator and double-check the direction of the conversion (it's embarrassingly easy to multiply when you should divide).

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I convert inches to centimeters?**
Multiply inches by 2.54. So 10 inches = 25.4 cm. For quick mental math, multiply by 2.5 — the result is within 2% of exact.

**How many kilograms is one pound?**
One pound equals 0.4536 kg. Divide pounds by 2.2 for a fast conversion: 150 lb ≈ 68 kg.

**Why do the US and UK use different measurement systems?**
Most countries adopted metric in the 19th–20th centuries for its simple base-10 math. The US never completed the switch due to cost and inertia, so imperial persists alongside metric in daily American life.

**When does precise unit conversion matter most?**
Precision is critical for shipping and customs, baking, construction measurements, medication dosing, and engineering. For browsing furniture sizes or converting running distances, a rough estimate is perfectly fine.
