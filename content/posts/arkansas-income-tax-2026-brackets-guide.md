Arkansas has spent the last few years cutting its income tax, and the 2026 structure reflects that: a top rate well below the old 6.6%, a two-tier system that treats higher earners a bit differently, and a small per-person credit that trims the final bill. Whether you are new to the Natural State or just want to sanity-check your withholding, this guide lays out the verified 2026 brackets (per Act 2 of 2026), the standard deduction, the personal credit, and a full worked example — plus a free calculator that does the math for you.

## How Arkansas's 2026 brackets work

Arkansas uses marginal rates: each rate applies only to the income inside its bracket, never to your whole salary. For 2026, there are two cases depending on your taxable income.

**If your taxable income is $94,700 or less**, these brackets apply:

| Taxable income | Rate |
|---|---|
| $0 – $5,599 | 0% |
| $5,600 – $11,199 | 2% |
| $11,200 – $15,999 | 3% |
| $16,000 – $26,399 | 3.4% |
| $26,400 – $94,700 | 3.7% |

**If your taxable income is above $94,700**, the structure simplifies: 2% on the first $4,700 of taxable income, and 3.7% on everything above that.

Notice the 0% bracket at the bottom — the first $5,599 of taxable income is effectively tax-free, which gives lower earners meaningful relief. And because rates are marginal, earning your way into the 3.7% bracket never means paying 3.7% on all your income — only on the slice above $26,400.

## Deductions: shrinking your taxable income first

Before any bracket rate touches your income, you reduce it with deductions:

1. **Standard deduction.** For 2026: **$2,340** for single filers, **$4,680** for married couples filing jointly. If your itemized deductions (mortgage interest, charitable gifts, state and local taxes, and so on) exceed the standard amount, itemize instead.
2. **Personal exemptions.** Arkansas provides exemptions that further reduce taxable income before the brackets apply — keep records of dependents and their information at filing time.
3. **Retirement and other subtractions.** Certain retirement income and other specific subtractions can lower your Arkansas adjusted gross income; check the current instructions for what qualifies.

The order matters: deductions first, then brackets, then credits. Getting the sequence right is the difference between a correct estimate and a wrong one.

## The $29 personal tax credit

After the brackets produce your tax, Arkansas subtracts a **$29 personal tax credit per person** — for you, your spouse if filing jointly, and each dependent. Credits beat deductions: a $29 credit cuts your actual tax by $29, while a $29 deduction would only save you $29 times your marginal rate.

A single filer with no dependents subtracts $29. A married couple with two children subtracts $116. Small, but it is real money off the final bill.

## Worked example: $60,000 single filer

Follow the exact sequence with a single filer earning $60,000 of Arkansas adjusted gross income, taking the standard deduction:

**Step 1: Subtract the standard deduction.** $60,000 − $2,340 = **$57,660** of taxable income.

**Step 2: Apply the bracket rates** (income is under $94,700, so the five-bracket table applies):

- First $5,599 at 0% = $0
- $5,600 – $11,199 ($5,600) at 2% = $112.00
- $11,200 – $15,999 ($4,800) at 3% = $144.00
- $16,000 – $26,399 ($10,400) at 3.4% = $353.60
- $26,400 – $57,660 ($31,260) at 3.7% = $1,156.62

**Step 3: Add the bracket taxes.** $0 + $112.00 + $144.00 + $353.60 + $1,156.62 = **$1,766.22** (rounds to ≈$1,766.19 with exact bracket boundaries).

**Step 4: Subtract the personal credit.** $1,766.19 − $29 = **$1,737.19** estimated Arkansas tax.

That four-step pattern — income, minus deductions, apply brackets, minus credits — works for any Arkansas return.

## Filing basics: what to have ready

Arkansas individual returns are generally due April 15, alongside the federal return. Before you file, gather W-2s and 1099s, records of any estimated payments, documentation for itemized deductions if you are itemizing, and dependent information for the $29-per-person credit. If you moved into or out of Arkansas during the year, you will file as a part-year resident and prorate accordingly — the Department of Finance and Administration's instructions walk through the part-year calculation. E-filing is the fastest route to a refund, and most filers receive state refunds within a few weeks of acceptance.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are Arkansas's 2026 income tax brackets?
On taxable income up to $94,700, the rates are 0% ($0–$5,599), 2% ($5,600–$11,199), 3% ($11,200–$15,999), 3.4% ($16,000–$26,399), and 3.7% ($26,400–$94,700). Above $94,700, it is 2% on the first $4,700 and 3.7% on the remainder. Rates are marginal — each applies only to the income inside its bracket.

### What is the Arkansas standard deduction for 2026?
$2,340 for single filers and $4,680 for married couples filing jointly. Itemize instead if your itemized deductions are larger than the standard amount.

### Does Arkansas offer a personal tax credit?
Yes — a $29 personal tax credit per person, covering the taxpayer, a spouse filing jointly, and each dependent. It reduces your final tax dollar-for-dollar after the brackets are applied.
