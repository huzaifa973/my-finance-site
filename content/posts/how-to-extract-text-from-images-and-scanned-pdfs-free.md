That scanned PDF someone sent you looks like a document but acts like a photo: you cannot select the text, cannot search it, cannot copy a paragraph. Same with a picture of a receipt, a whiteboard, or a book page. The fix is OCR — optical character recognition — which reads the text out of the image and hands it back to you as real, editable text.

Here is how to extract text from both images and scanned PDFs, free, with no software to install.

## Extract text from images with OCR

An image-to-text OCR tool takes a photo, screenshot, or scan and converts the visible text into characters you can copy, edit, and search.

**Step-by-step:**

1. Open a free image-to-text tool like [DollarWise's image to text OCR](/tools/image-to-text-ocr/).
2. Upload your image — a photo of a document, a screenshot of text, a receipt, a book page.
3. The tool runs OCR right in your browser and displays the recognized text.
4. Copy the text and paste it into a document, email, or note. Give it a quick proofread (see the accuracy tips below).

**What it handles well:** printed or typed text, receipts, business cards, screenshots, presentation slides, signs. If a human can read it clearly, OCR almost certainly can too.

**What trips it up:** handwriting (accuracy varies a lot), very low-resolution images, heavy glare or shadows, text on steep angles, and decorative or unusual fonts. A clean source image is the single biggest factor — more important than which tool you pick.

## Extract text from scanned PDFs

A scanned PDF is just a stack of page-images bundled in a PDF wrapper. Regular copy-paste fails because there is no text layer — only pixels. A PDF text extractor with OCR solves this by reading each page as an image and rebuilding the text.

2. Upload your scanned PDF.
3. The tool runs OCR across the pages and returns the full text.

**When to use this:** tax documents scanned by an accountant, old records and archives, signed contracts you received as scans, textbooks in PDF form, any PDF where selecting text does nothing. Once you have the text layer, you can also convert the file into a proper searchable document.

**Multi-page note:** OCR processes page by page, so a 50-page scan takes a little longer than a single photo. That is normal — let it finish and check the results page by page for anything critical.

**What to do with the text once you have it:** paste it into a document and save it as a proper searchable PDF, so you never have to run OCR on it again. You can also archive the text version alongside the scan — searchable text makes finding that one clause or invoice number trivial months later, instead of flipping through page images.

## Tips for the most accurate OCR results

These small habits make a bigger difference than switching tools:

- **Straighten the source.** Photograph documents head-on, not at an angle. Phone camera apps have a "document scan" mode — use it.
- **Light it evenly.** Shadows and glare are OCR's worst enemies. Natural daylight near a window works better than overhead room lights.
- **Use the highest resolution available.** If you have the original scan settings, 300 DPI is the sweet spot for documents.
- **Crop out the clutter.** If only a paragraph matters, crop the image to just that area before uploading. Less noise, better accuracy.
- **Proofread the output.** OCR sometimes confuses similar characters (0 vs O, 1 vs l, rn vs m). A quick skim catches them, especially in account numbers and names.
- **Watch out for tables.** Text in tables and multi-column layouts can come out in jumbled reading order. Some tools offer layout detection — if yours does not, copy column by column.

## Why browser-based OCR is the privacy-smart choice

Most OCR services ask you to upload your document to their server, where it may be stored, logged, or used to train models. For a restaurant menu that is harmless; for a tax form, contract, or medical record it is not.

Client-side OCR tools process your file entirely on your own device — the file never travels anywhere. That is the pattern the DollarWise tools above use, and it is the right default for anything containing names, numbers, addresses, or financial details. Free does not have to mean giving up your data.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I extract text from a scanned PDF?

Upload the scanned PDF to a free PDF text extractor, which runs OCR on every page and returns searchable, copyable text. Scanned pages are really images, so OCR is what makes the text selectable again.

### Is OCR free and accurate?

Free browser-based OCR is highly accurate on clean, straight, well-lit text — receipts, typed documents, screenshots. Accuracy drops on handwriting, blurry photos, and decorative fonts, but for everyday documents it typically gets 95%+ right.

### Can I extract text from a photo of a document?

Yes — take the photo as straight-on and well-lit as possible, then upload it to a free image-to-text OCR tool. The tool reads the text in the photo and gives you editable text you can copy anywhere.

### Is it safe to upload documents to a free OCR tool?

Use a client-side tool where files never leave your browser — the OCR runs on your own device, so sensitive documents like contracts or tax forms are never uploaded to any server. Avoid tools that require uploading files for anything confidential.
