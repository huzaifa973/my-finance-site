A video title does two jobs at once: it tells viewers what they will get, and it convinces the platform to show the video to more people. Weak titles — vague, keyword-stuffed, or misleading — fail at both. Strong titles follow a handful of patterns that viewers already respond to: a clear promise, a specific number, a mistake to avoid, or a story worth watching. Once you know the patterns, you never have to stare at a blank title field again.

## What's inside this title pack

How-to titles promise a clear outcome and work best for tutorials, because viewers searching for help want to know the video solves their problem. Mistake titles tap into the fear of doing something wrong, which earns clicks from viewers who already tried and failed. List titles set expectations instantly, story titles build a personal connection, and comparison titles attract viewers who are deciding between options.
Challenge titles create a built-in reason to watch to the end, question titles mirror what viewers are already wondering, and quick-win titles promise a fast payoff for busy scrollers. ## How to use this resource

1. Pick the style that matches the video: how-to for tutorials, story for personal journeys, challenge for series content.
2. Replace [topic] with a specific subject — 'sourdough' beats 'baking', and 'budgeting' beats 'money'.
3. Put the hook in the first five words, since titles get cut off on mobile feeds.
4. Check that the video genuinely delivers what the title promises; misleading titles hurt watch time and trust.
5. Test two title styles on similar videos and keep the style that earns the better click-through rate.

Write the title after the video is edited, not before — you will know the real best moment to promise.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long should a video title be?**

Aim for under 60 characters so the full title shows on most screens. If it must be longer, put the most important words first, since the end gets cut off on mobile.

**Are clickbait titles worth it?**

Only if the video delivers. A misleading title may win one click, but viewers leave fast, watch time drops, and the platform shows the video to fewer people next time.

**Should titles and thumbnails say the same thing?**

No — they should complement each other. Let the thumbnail show the emotion or result, and let the title carry the specific promise or question.
