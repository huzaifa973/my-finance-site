Your credit score decides what you pay for car loans, mortgages, credit cards — even apartment approvals and some jobs. Checking it is free, takes minutes, and never hurts your score. Here are the best free apps on both sides of the Atlantic in 2026.

## US: the best free credit score apps

### Credit Karma — best all-rounder (free)

Covers **TransUnion and Equifax** with VantageScore 3.0, updated weekly. Beyond scores: free credit reports, monitoring alerts for new inquiries or accounts, and a decent breakdown of what's helping or hurting your score. It's also absorbed Mint's old budgeting features, making it a two-in-one.

**What's actually free:** everything core — scores, reports, monitoring, alerts.
**Catch:** ad-supported; expect credit card and loan offers matched to your profile.

### Experian app — best for FICO scores (free tier)

The only major free source of a real **FICO score** (Experian bureau), which is the model most US lenders actually use. Also offers free Experian credit report, dark web monitoring basics, and Experian Boost — which adds rent, utility, and streaming payments to your file, sometimes lifting scores 10–20 points instantly.

**What's actually free:** FICO 8 score, report, Boost, basic monitoring.
**Catch:** upsells paid tiers constantly; the free tier is enough — ignore the rest.

### MyFICO (free tier) — best for score education

MyFICO's free plan gives you one **Equifax** FICO score monthly plus excellent simulators and explanations of how scoring works. It's thinner on monitoring than the others, but unmatched for understanding *why* your score is what it is.

**Best for:** people actively rebuilding credit who want to understand the mechanics.

## UK: the best free credit score apps

The UK system differs: three main agencies (Experian, Equifax, TransUnion), scores out of different maximums, and no single FICO equivalent. These apps cover all three — use at least two, since lenders check different agencies.

### ClearScore — best UK all-rounder (free)

Shows your **Equifax** score and report free, updated monthly, with a clean timeline of your score history. Also includes affordability insights and the "no, this won't affect your score" eligibility checker for credit cards — genuinely useful before applying.

### Experian UK app — biggest agency coverage (free)

Your **Experian** score (out of 999) with the free tier, plus the credit report basics. Experian is the agency most UK lenders check first, so this is the single most important free score for Brits to watch.

### Credit Karma UK — TransUnion coverage (free)

Covers the third agency, **TransUnion**, completing your UK coverage. Same app as the US version but showing UK data. Between ClearScore, Experian, and Credit Karma UK, you see everything lenders see — for £0.

## US vs UK: key differences to know

| | US | UK |
|---|---|---|
| Main bureaus | Equifax, Experian, TransUnion | Experian, Equifax, TransUnion |
| Common score model | FICO (lenders), VantageScore (free apps) | Agency-specific (e.g. /999, /1000) |
| Score range | 300–850 | Varies by agency |
| "Good" score | 670+ FICO | Varies — check each agency's bands |
| Free reports by law | AnnualCreditReport.com (weekly) | Statutory reports available on request |

Don't compare a US 720 with a UK 720 — different scales, different systems.

## How to actually use these apps (the 20-minute setup)

1. **Install two apps** covering different bureaus (US: Credit Karma + Experian; UK: Experian + ClearScore).
2. **Screenshot or note your starting scores** — you need a baseline to measure progress.
3. **Read the "factors" section** in each app. It tells you exactly what's dragging your score: utilization, missed payments, thin file, hard inquiries.
4. **Set up alerts** for new accounts and hard inquiries — this is free identity-theft monitoring.
5. **Check monthly**, not daily. Act on the factors, then watch the trend.

## 5 moves that raise your score fastest

1. **Pay down credit card balances below 30% utilization** — below 10% is ideal. This is the fastest lever most people have; utilization updates monthly.
2. **Never miss a payment** — set up autopay for at least the minimum on every account. Payment history is ~35% of a FICO score.
3. **Don't close old cards** — average account age matters. Keep the oldest card open with a small recurring charge.
4. **Use Experian Boost (US)** — free points for bills you're already paying.
5. **Check reports for errors** — ~1 in 5 reports has a mistake. Dispute errors with the bureau directly (free); removals can add 20–50 points.

What *doesn't* help: paying a "credit repair" company for things you can do free, opening five new cards at once, or obsessing over daily score wiggles.

### FAQ

**Q:** Does checking my own credit score lower it?
A: No — never. Checking your own score is a soft inquiry and has zero effect. Only hard inquiries (applying for credit) affect your score, and even those are small and temporary.

**Q:** Which credit score is the 'real' one lenders see?
A: There's no single real score — lenders see versions from different bureaus (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion) and different models (FICO, VantageScore). The free apps show legitimate scores; focus on the trend across months rather than exact numbers.

**Q:** Are free credit score apps accurate?
A: Yes, they show real scores from real bureaus — they're not estimates. A lender might pull a slightly different version, but if your free score is 720 and rising, your lending prospects are genuinely improving.

**Q:** How often should I check my credit score?
A: Monthly is plenty for most people. Weekly checks are useful if you're actively building credit or recovering from damage. Daily checking just creates anxiety — scores move slowly.
