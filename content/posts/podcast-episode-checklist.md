Great podcast episodes aren't accidents — they're checklists executed well. The difference between a show that grows and one that stalls usually isn't talent; it's the boring stuff done consistently: levels checked before recording, a backup running, loudness normalized, show notes published, and the episode actually promoted. This 28-point checklist covers the full episode lifecycle in six phases: pre-recording planning, recording setup, during recording, post-production, publishing, and promotion. New podcasters get a complete workflow to follow; experienced hosts get a safety net that catches the skipped step before it costs them an episode.

## What's inside this checklist

Six sections mirror the real order of episode production. Pre-recording locks the topic, title, outline, and guest logistics before anyone hits record. Recording setup covers the room, mic position, levels, and the backup recording that saves episodes. The during-recording section captures the on-mic habits — markers, room tone, energy — that make editing painless. Post-production walks through editing, noise reduction, loudness normalization to podcast standards, and export settings. Publishing covers titles, descriptions, artwork specs, and directory submission. Promotion closes the loop with clips, announcements, and guest outreach — because an unpromoted episode is an unheard one.

## How to use this checklist

1. Print it or keep it open in a second window during every recording session.
2. Work it in order: never start recording until the pre-recording and setup sections are fully checked.
3. Treat the backup recording and the test playback as hard gates — no exceptions, ever.
4. After publishing, do the promotion section within 24 hours while the episode is fresh.
5. Review which steps you skipped each month; the pattern shows where your workflow needs tightening.

Run this checklist ten episodes in a row and it becomes muscle memory — and your show will sound, look, and perform like it was made by a team twice your size.

## Frequently asked questions

**What audio settings should I export my podcast in?**
MP3 at 128 kbps minimum (192 kbps for music-heavy shows), normalized to -16 LUFS for stereo or -19 LUFS for mono. Always fill in ID3 tags so players display your title and artwork correctly.

**Do I really need a backup recording?**
Yes — non-negotiable. One corrupted file or crashed app means a lost episode and possibly a lost guest. A phone voice memo running alongside your main recording costs nothing and has saved countless episodes.

**How do I promote a new podcast episode?**
Cut one short clip or audiogram within 24 hours, post it everywhere with a direct listen link, and send assets to your guest — guests are consistently the best promoters a new show has.
