Beginner photo editing usually fails in the same two ways: too much of everything at once, or the steps done in the wrong order. This fifty-point checklist gives new editors the full workflow in the right sequence — back up first, cull, fix color and exposure, retouch lightly, sharpen last, and deliver properly. It is written for new photographers, hobbyists, and small business owners editing their own product and portrait photos.

## What's inside this resource

The checklist is deliberately ordered: global corrections before local ones, color before sharpening, and delivery checks at the end. The two rules that save beginners the most pain are at the top — back up originals before touching anything, and sharpen only at the output size. Follow the order, and even simple edits look considered; skip around, and every slider fights the last one.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should I edit first in a photo?

White balance and exposure. They change how every later adjustment looks, so fixing them first keeps the rest of your edit honest.

### Why sharpen last?

Sharpening is output-dependent: a file sharpened for print looks crunchy on a phone screen. Sharpen once, at the final export size, for each destination.

### How much retouching is too much?

If a viewer notices the retouching, it is too much. Remove temporary blemishes and distractions, but keep skin texture — texture reads as real.
