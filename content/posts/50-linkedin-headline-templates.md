## How to use these templates

1. Find the group that matches your current situation — job seeker, freelancer, executive, student, or career changer.
2. Pick the template whose structure fits your story, then fill every placeholder with your real details.
3. Front-load keywords: put your target role and top skills in the first 60 characters so they survive truncation.
4. Read it from a recruiter's perspective — does it say what you deliver, not just what you're called?
5. Test it for two weeks, then refine: LinkedIn search appearances and profile views tell you what's working.

A headline is a first impression you can rewrite in five minutes. Make yours say what you're actually worth.

## Frequently asked questions

**Should I include "Open to Work" in my headline?**
It depends on your situation. Active job seekers can use the #OpenToWork photo frame plus a headline signal like "Seeking [Target Role]" — it boosts recruiter discovery. If you're employed and discreetly looking, skip the headline signal and use LinkedIn's private "open to work" setting instead.

**What are the biggest LinkedIn headline mistakes?**
Three stand out: using only your job title, stuffing it with buzzwords like "guru" or "ninja," and writing it for yourself instead of your audience. Every headline should answer the reader's silent question: "What's in it for me?"
