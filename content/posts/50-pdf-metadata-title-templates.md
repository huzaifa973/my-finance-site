The title baked into a PDF's metadata is what shows up in search results, document managers, and browser tabs — and most PDFs still carry the filename the author forgot to change, like 'final_v7.pdf'. These fifty templates give every document type a clean, searchable title format: report, guide, manual, proposal, invoice, contract, whitepaper, and more. They are made for office workers, freelancers, publishers, and anyone who sends PDFs to clients or publishes them online.

## What's inside this resource

Each template follows the same logic: topic first (so it is findable), then document type, then brand and year. That order matters because search tools and file managers show the start of the title first. The pack includes two patterns per document type plus special formats for confidential reports, client editions, and annual editions — so whether you are titling a quote or a company handbook, there is a pattern ready.

## Frequently asked questions

### Where do I set a PDF's metadata title?

In most editors: File, then Properties or Document Properties, then the Title field. It is separate from the filename — set both, and keep them similar.

### Why does the metadata title matter for SEO?

Search engines index PDF titles and show them in results. A descriptive title like 'Solar Panel Guide 2026' ranks and gets clicked; 'untitled.pdf' does neither.

### Should the title match the filename?

Closely, yes. Matching titles and filenames avoid confusion in document systems and make the file findable whether someone searches by title or by file.
