If you freelance, run a side business, or sell services of any kind, invoices are how you get paid. And a good invoice does more than ask for money — it sets expectations, speeds up payment, and makes you look professional enough that clients take your payment terms seriously.

The good news: you do not need accounting software or a designer. Here is how to create a proper invoice online, free, in minutes.

## What belongs on a US invoice (the 7 essentials)

Every invoice you send should include these — they are what clients expect and what protects you if payment goes sideways:

1. **Your business name and contact info** — name, email, phone, and address. If you operate under a DBA ("doing business as"), use that name.
2. **The client's name and billing address** — who is being billed, spelled right. Double-check this; invoices to the wrong entity cause real payment delays.
3. **A unique invoice number** — INV-2026-001 style. Number sequentially so you can track which invoices are paid and which are outstanding.
4. **Issue date and due date** — when you sent it and when payment is expected. "Net 30" means 30 days from the invoice date; spell it out as an actual date too.
5. **Itemized list of goods or services** — each line item with a description, quantity, rate, and line total. "10 hours of web design at $75/hr = $750" beats a vague "Services rendered: $750" every time.
6. **The total amount due** — bold, clear, unmissable. Include any taxes or discounts as separate lines above it.
7. **How to pay** — your accepted payment methods and where to send payment. The harder you make paying, the slower you get paid.

**Bonus:** a short thank-you line and your late-payment policy ("A 1.5% monthly late fee applies after the due date") keep things friendly and firm at the same time.

## When to send an invoice

Timing matters more than most new freelancers think:

- **Immediately after delivery.** Send the invoice within 24 hours of finishing the work or shipping the product. Same-day invoicing is the single best habit for getting paid on time — the work is fresh in the client's mind and in their budget cycle.
- **For long projects, invoice in milestones.** Agree on payment stages upfront (e.g., 50% to start, 50% on delivery) and invoice each stage as you complete it. Never let a project run months without any payment coming in.
- **For retainers, invoice on a fixed schedule.** Monthly retainers should be invoiced on the same date every month, like clockwork. Predictability gets you paid faster than creativity.
- **Never wait until you "have time."** Invoicing is revenue. Treat it like the highest-priority task of your workday, because it literally is.

## How to get paid faster (what actually works)

The invoice itself is half the battle. These tactics handle the other half:

- **Shorten your terms.** Net 15 beats Net 30. Many clients will simply pay on whatever terms you state — most freelancers just never ask for shorter ones.
- **Offer an early-payment discount.** "2% off if paid within 10 days" (written as 2/10 Net 30) gives accounts-payable departments a reason to put you at the top of the pile.
- **Make paying effortless.** Accept bank transfer, card, or digital wallets — whatever your client prefers. Every extra step between the invoice and the payment button costs you days.
- **Send the invoice to the right person.** The person who hired you is not always the person who pays you. Ask "who should I send the invoice to for fastest processing?" and address it there.
- **Follow up on a schedule.** Day 1 after due: friendly reminder. Day 7: firmer reminder. Day 14: phone call. Polite persistence collects payments; silence does not.
- **Charge late fees and mean it.** A stated late-fee policy that you actually enforce trains clients to pay you first. One enforced late fee usually means you never have to enforce another.

## Frequently asked questions

### What must be on a US invoice?

Every US invoice needs your business name and contact info, the client's name and address, a unique invoice number, the issue and due dates, an itemized list of goods or services with amounts, the total due, and your payment instructions.

### When should I send an invoice to a client?

Send it immediately after the work is delivered or the product ships — within 24 hours is ideal. The longer you wait, the longer you wait to get paid; same-day invoicing consistently shortens payment cycles.

### How do I get clients to pay invoices faster?

Use clear payment terms like Net 15 instead of Net 30, offer a small early-payment discount (2% off if paid within 10 days), accept digital payments, send the invoice the same day work finishes, and set up automatic reminders for overdue invoices.

No — a free online invoice generator gives you a professional, printable invoice in minutes with no software to install or account to create. Paid accounting software only becomes worth it when you are sending dozens of invoices a month.
