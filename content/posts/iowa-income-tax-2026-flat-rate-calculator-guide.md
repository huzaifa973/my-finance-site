Iowa pulled off one of the most dramatic tax transformations in the country: in 2022, its top income tax rate was 8.53% — among the highest in the Midwest. Through a multi-year phase-down, Iowa landed on a **flat 3.8% individual income tax** starting in 2025, and that rate is **unchanged for 2026** (confirmed by the Tax Foundation's 2026 state tax data). No brackets, no graduated tiers — one rate for everyone. Here is what that means for your paycheck, who gets extra breaks, and how to calculate exactly what you owe.

## The flat 3.8%: how Iowa got here

Iowa's journey to a flat tax happened in stages under tax reform signed by Governor Kim Reynolds:

- **2022:** top rate 8.53% (graduated system)
- **2023:** top rate cut to 6%
- **2024:** three brackets with a top rate of 5.7%
- **2025:** flat 3.8% (schedule accelerated by a 2024 law)
- **2026:** flat 3.8%, unchanged

That is a top-rate reduction of more than 4.7 percentage points in three years. Iowa now sits comfortably below neighbors like Minnesota and among the lowest flat-tax states in the region.

## How the flat tax works (it is simpler than you think)

With graduated taxes, you need bracket tables. With Iowa's flat tax, the math is one multiplication:

**Iowa tax = Iowa taxable income × 3.8%**

Example: $60,000 of Iowa taxable income → $60,000 × 0.038 = **$2,280** in Iowa income tax.

But "taxable income" is not your salary. You start from federal adjusted gross income, apply Iowa-specific adjustments, then subtract Iowa deductions and exemptions (Iowa has its own standard deduction and personal exemption amounts). Only the remainder gets the 3.8% rate. Two people earning $60,000 can owe different amounts if one itemizes or claims more exemptions — the *rate* is flat, but the *base* it applies to varies.

## Iowa's retirement income exclusion: a big deal

Since 2023, Iowa **excludes retirement income from state tax** for:

- Residents **age 55 and older**
- Disabled residents
- Qualifying surviving spouses

Covered income includes pensions, IRA distributions, 401(k) withdrawals, and annuities. **Social Security benefits are not taxed by Iowa at all.** Combined with the flat 3.8%, this turned Iowa from one of the region's most burdensome states for retirees into one of its most attractive. If you are planning retirement in the Midwest, Iowa's 2026 tax picture deserves a serious look.

## What Iowa does not tax

- **Estate tax:** Iowa has none (its old estate tax applied only to deaths before 2005)
- **Inheritance tax:** repealed for deaths on or after January 1, 2025
- **Social Security:** fully exempt

Note that Iowa's sales tax is 6% at the state level (7% combined in most areas with the local option), and property taxes run above the national average — income tax is only one piece of the total burden.

## Iowa vs. neighboring states

The flat 3.8% looks even better in regional context. Illinois taxes flat at 4.95%, Missouri's top rate sits above 4.5%, Minnesota's top rate exceeds 9%, and Wisconsin tops out near 7.65%. Among Iowa's neighbors, only states with *no* income tax at all (South Dakota) beat it. For remote workers who can choose where to live, Iowa's combination of a low flat rate, the retirement-income exclusion, and relatively affordable housing makes a compelling case — though higher-than-average property taxes partly offset the income-tax savings, so run the full picture before moving for tax reasons alone.

## Who still owes Iowa tax (residency rules)

Iowa taxes residents on all income and nonresidents on Iowa-source income. If you moved into or out of Iowa during 2026, you file as a part-year resident and the 3.8% applies only to the income earned while an Iowa resident (plus any Iowa-source income). Remote workers: your employer's withholding follows the state where *you* work, not where the company sits — if you live in Iowa and work remotely for an out-of-state employer, Iowa is generally where you owe.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is Iowa's income tax rate for 2026?

Iowa taxes individual income at a flat 3.8% rate for 2026 — the same rate that took effect in 2025. There are no brackets; every dollar of Iowa taxable income is taxed at 3.8%.

### Is Iowa's 3.8% rate really flat for everyone?

Yes. Since 2025, Iowa has had no graduated brackets for individual income. High earners and low earners pay the same 3.8% marginal rate, though deductions and exemptions mean effective rates differ.

### Does Iowa tax retirement income in 2026?

No — since 2023, Iowa excludes retirement income (pensions, IRA and 401(k) distributions, annuities) for residents 55 and older, disabled residents, and qualifying surviving spouses. Social Security is not taxed by Iowa at all.
