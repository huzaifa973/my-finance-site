## What "split in half" actually means

Splitting a PDF in half divides it by page count: a 20-page document becomes two 10-page files, typically named something like `document-part1.pdf` and `document-part2.pdf`. A few details worth knowing upfront:

- **Odd page counts.** A 21-page PDF cannot split evenly — most tools give you pages 1–11 and 12–21, or let you choose the breakpoint yourself. Either is fine; just check which half got the extra page.
- **Nothing is lost.** Splitting copies pages into new files; the original stays untouched. You are not cutting content out, you are duplicating the container.
- **File size roughly halves.** Each output carries about half the pages, so each file lands near half the original size — the simplest possible way to duck under an upload or email limit without touching quality.
- **Order is preserved.** Page 1 of the original is page 1 of the first half. Bookmarks and links inside the document usually survive in capable tools.

## Splitting smarter: beyond the exact middle

Half by page count is the default, but real documents have structure worth respecting:

- **Split at chapter boundaries.** If page 10 lands mid-sentence, move the breakpoint to the nearest chapter or section start. A clean break beats a mathematically perfect one.
- **Split by content, not count.** Need only the appendix? Extract pages 30–40 as its own file instead of halving. Most split tools accept any custom range.
- **Split to hit a size target.** If the goal is emailing, split so each part lands under your provider's limit with encoding headroom — for a 25 MB cap, keep parts under ~18 MB each.
- **Split scanned PDFs the same way.** Page-based splitting works identically on scans; the pages are just images. No OCR or conversion needed first.
- **Re-merge when done.** If you split only to get past an upload limit, merge the halves back afterward for archiving so you keep one canonical copy.

## 5 mistakes to avoid when splitting PDFs

1. **Overwriting the original.** Save the halves as new files with clear names. The moment you need the full document again, you will be glad the original is intact.
2. **Splitting mid-table or mid-paragraph.** A table that starts on page 10 and continues on page 11 becomes unreadable when the halves separate. Always glance at the breakpoint pages first.
3. **Losing track of which half is which.** `document (1).pdf` and `document (2).pdf` are a filing disaster. Name files with page ranges: `report-p1-10.pdf`, `report-p11-20.pdf`.
4. **Forgetting encrypted PDFs.** A password-protected PDF will not split until it is unlocked. Decrypt first with the password, then split — tools cannot and should not bypass encryption silently.
5. **Emailing both halves in one message.** If you split to beat a size limit, sending both parts in a single email defeats the purpose. Send them separately or compress as well.
