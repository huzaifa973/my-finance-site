Every platform recompresses your video, but the quality you get out depends on the quality you put in. Export too low and your video looks soft and blocky forever; export too high and you waste hours uploading a file the platform will crush anyway. The sweet spot is different for YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, and the rest — and guessing it fresh on every project is how creators end up with blurry uploads.

This free cheat sheet gives you the export settings for every major platform: resolution, codec, bitrate, frame rate, and audio, in one scannable reference. ## How to use this cheat sheet

1. Identify where the video will be watched first — export for the primary platform, not all of them at once.
2. Set resolution and aspect ratio before anything else; everything downstream depends on it.
3. Dial in the recommended bitrate for that platform, leaning toward the top of the range for fast-moving content.
4. Match the frame rate to your source footage — converting frame rates at export creates judder you cannot undo.
5. Keep an archival master of every finished project so you can re-export for new platforms without re-editing.

One habit saves more quality than any single setting: always export from the master, never from a previously compressed upload. Each generation of compression throws away detail permanently. The master-first workflow in step 5 means every platform gets a first-generation encode, which is the single biggest quality win on this entire sheet.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the best export format for YouTube?**
MP4 with H.264 video and AAC audio. Export at your timeline's native resolution — 1920x1080 or 3840x2160 — with 8-12 Mbps for 1080p or 35-45 Mbps for 4K SDR. YouTube re-encodes every upload, so your job is to hand it a clean, high-quality source; uploading true 4K even unlocks YouTube's better VP9 processing for your video.

**Should I export in H.264 or H.265?**
Use H.264 when the file needs to play anywhere — it is universally supported. H.265 (HEVC) produces smaller files at equal quality, but older devices and some platforms handle it poorly, and uploads take longer to process. Rule of thumb: H.264 for sharing and client delivery, H.265 for archiving and personal storage.

**Why does my video look blurry after uploading?**
The three usual causes: your export bitrate was too low for the resolution, you uploaded a low-resolution file that the platform scaled down further, or the platform is still processing the HD version — which can take hours on YouTube. Export at the recommended bitrate, upload the highest resolution you have, and wait for processing to finish before judging the result.
